## Chrome VPN Extension vs Full VPN App

| Feature | Chrome VPN Extension | Full VPN App |
|---|---|---|
| Covers Chrome traffic | ✅ | ✅ |
| Covers other apps | ❌ | ✅ |
| DNS leak protection | Sometimes | ✅ Usually |

For browser-only privacy, a Chrome VPN extension is sufficient and faster. For full device protection, use a dedicated VPN app.

## Critical: WebRTC IP Leaks in Chrome

Chrome's WebRTC API can expose your real IP address even when you are connected to a VPN extension. This is a known Chrome behavior — test for it:

1. Connect your VPN extension
2. Visit `browserleaks.com/webrtc`
3. Check if your real IP appears under "Local IP Address" or "Public IP Address"

If your real IP appears → your VPN has a WebRTC leak and is not fully protecting you.

**Fix:** Choose an extension that explicitly mentions WebRTC leak protection (Windscribe does). Or install the **WebRTC Leak Prevent** extension alongside your VPN.

## FAQ

**Q: Does a Chrome VPN extension protect my passwords?**
It encrypts traffic between Chrome and the VPN server. However, password safety depends on HTTPS and your password manager — not the VPN.

**Q: Can a VPN extension bypass geo-restrictions on streaming?**
Sometimes — it depends on whether the streaming service blocks the VPN's IP ranges. Paid VPNs (NordVPN, Surfshark) maintain more servers to stay ahead of blocks.

**Q: Is a free VPN extension safe to use?**
Only if it comes from a company with an audited no-log policy. Windscribe and ProtonVPN meet this bar. Generic free VPNs from unknown developers should be avoided.
