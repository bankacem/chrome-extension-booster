# Chrome Extensions for Beginners: Start Here (2026 Guide)

You have heard people mention Chrome extensions, you know they do something useful, but nobody has explained them without assuming you already know what they are. This guide starts from zero. By the end, you will know exactly what extensions are, how to install them without risk, which ones to start with, and which ones to avoid.

---

## What Is a Chrome Extension, Really?

A Chrome extension is a small program that lives inside your Chrome browser and gives it new abilities.

Think of Chrome as a smartphone. Extensions are apps you install to make it do more. Just like your phone's Maps app navigates and your Weather app forecasts, a Chrome extension can block ads, remember passwords, apply dark mode, or save links for later — all without leaving your browser.

The difference from apps: extensions don't open in separate windows. They work invisibly in the background or through a small button in your Chrome toolbar.

---

## Where Do Extensions Come From?

Extensions come from the **Chrome Web Store** — Google's official marketplace at [chrome.google.com/webstore](https://chrome.google.com/webstore). Think of it like the App Store or Google Play, but specifically for Chrome.

Google reviews extensions before they appear in the store. This doesn't guarantee they're all safe (we'll cover how to check), but it filters out the most obvious bad actors.

**Important rule:** Only install extensions from the Chrome Web Store. Never install extensions from random websites, email attachments, or popups saying "install this extension to improve your experience."

## How to Install Your First Extension (Step by Step)

Installing an extension takes less than a minute:

1. Open Chrome and go to [chrome.google.com/webstore](https://chrome.google.com/webstore)
2. Search for the extension you want — try "Dark Reader" to start
3. Click on the extension in the results
4. Read the description briefly
5. Click the blue **"Add to Chrome"** button
6. A popup appears listing what the extension needs access to — read it
7. Click **"Add extension"** to confirm
8. The extension installs and an icon appears in your toolbar

To see all installed extensions at once, click the puzzle piece icon (🧩) in your Chrome toolbar.

## Understanding the Permissions Popup

When you install any extension, Chrome shows a list of what it needs access to. This is important information — not something to click through without reading.

**Common permissions and what they mean:**

| Permission | What it means |
|-----------|--------------|
| Read and change all your data on websites | The extension can see and modify pages you visit |
| Read your browsing history | It can see which sites you've visited |
| Manage your downloads | It can see and control your downloads |
| Display notifications | It can send you browser notifications |
| Read and change data on specific websites | Limited to certain sites only |

**How to decide:** Ask yourself — does this permission make sense for what the extension does? A dark mode extension needs to modify pages (✅ makes sense). A color picker extension asking to read your browsing history (❌ doesn't make sense — avoid it).

## How to Spot a Safe Extension (Beginner Checklist)

Before clicking "Add to Chrome" on any extension, check five things:

**1. User count:** Look at the number of users shown on the listing. 100,000+ users means many people have tested it without major issues. An extension with 50 users and 5-star reviews could have fake reviews.

**2. Last update:** Shown on the store page. An extension updated in the last 3 months is actively maintained. One last updated 2+ years ago may be abandoned.

**3. Publisher name:** Is the developer a real company or person you can look up? "Google LLC" or "Bitwarden Inc." are trustworthy. "AppsDev2024" with no website is a red flag.

**4. Reviews:** Click "Reviews" and sort by newest. Read the 1-star reviews — they often expose real problems before anyone else reports them.

**5. Does the permission make sense?** We covered this above. Trust your instincts.

## The 5 Best Extensions to Start With

These are the safest, most useful starting extensions for anyone new to Chrome extensions:

### 1. Bitwarden (Password Manager)
**What it does:** Remembers all your passwords and fills them in automatically when you visit login pages.

**Why start here:** Password reuse is the main reason people get hacked. Bitwarden fixes this without you having to memorize dozens of passwords.

**Is it free?** Yes, completely free for individuals.
**User count:** 5M+
**RAM:** 25MB

### 2. uBlock Origin Lite (Ad Blocker)
**What it does:** Removes ads from websites and blocks tracking scripts.

**Why start here:** Many beginners don't realize how many ads follow them around the internet. With an ad blocker, pages load faster, look cleaner, and you're protected from malicious ads.

**Is it free?** Yes.
**User count:** 2M+ (on Chrome, since full uBlock Origin no longer works on Chrome)
**RAM:** 18MB

### 3. Dark Reader (Dark Mode)
**What it does:** Applies a dark color scheme to every website — great for late-night browsing.

**Why start here:** Most beginners install Dark Reader and immediately wonder how they lived without it. Especially useful if you use Chrome at night.

**Is it free?** Yes.
**User count:** 5M+
**RAM:** 30MB

### 4. GoFullPage (Screenshot Tool)
**What it does:** Captures the entire webpage as one image — even the parts you have to scroll to see.

**Why start here:** Chrome's built-in screenshot only captures what's on screen. GoFullPage captures everything. Useful for recipes, articles, instruction pages.

**Is it free?** Yes for basic capture.
**User count:** 2M+
**RAM:** 25MB

### 5. Grammarly (Writing Helper)
**What it does:** Checks your spelling and grammar in real time when you type anywhere online — emails, social media, comment boxes.

**Why start here:** If English isn't your first language, or you just want cleaner writing, Grammarly catches mistakes Chrome's spell-check misses.

**Is it free?** Yes for basic features.
**User count:** 30M+
**RAM:** 110MB (heavier than others, but worth it for writers)

## How to Manage Your Extensions

Once you have a few extensions installed, you'll want to know how to manage them:

**See all extensions:** Click the puzzle piece icon (🧩) in your toolbar
**Pin an extension to the toolbar:** Click 🧩 → click the pin icon next to any extension
**Temporarily disable:** Go to `chrome://extensions` → toggle the switch off (green = on, grey = off)
**Remove permanently:** Go to `chrome://extensions` → click Remove
**Keyboard shortcuts:** Go to `chrome://extensions/shortcuts` to set custom keyboard shortcuts for extensions

## Common Beginner Mistakes

**Installing too many at once:** Start with 2-3 extensions. Add more as you identify specific needs. More extensions = more RAM usage and more security risk.

**Leaving old extensions active:** If you tried an extension and don't use it, remove it. Don't just disable it and forget.

**Installing from popups:** If a website shows you a popup saying "install this extension to access content" — close it. This is almost always a scam.

**Not reading permissions:** Take 10 seconds to check permissions before every install. It's worth it.

**Giving extensions access to incognito:** By default, extensions don't run in Chrome's incognito mode. Don't enable them there unless you specifically need them and trust them completely.

## FAQ

**Can Chrome extensions damage my computer?**
A malicious extension can track your browsing, inject ads, or steal credentials — but cannot damage your hardware or operating system directly. The risk is data theft, not physical damage. Stick to verified publishers and you'll be fine.

**Will extensions slow down Chrome?**
Each extension uses some RAM and CPU. 2-5 well-chosen extensions have negligible impact. 20+ active extensions will slow Chrome noticeably. Keep your list short.

**Can I use extensions on my phone?**
Not on the official Chrome app for Android or iOS. Chrome for mobile doesn't support extensions. Use Kiwi Browser (Android) to use Chrome extensions on a mobile device.

**What happens if I remove an extension?**
Removing an extension deletes it and its data. If you want to temporarily stop it without losing settings, disable it instead of removing it.

**How do I update extensions?**
Chrome updates extensions automatically. You don't have to do anything. To manually check: `chrome://extensions` → click the refresh icon (if you enabled Developer mode).
