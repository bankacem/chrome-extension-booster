# How to Manage Passwords in Chrome (2026 Complete Guide)

**Quick Answer:** Chrome has a built-in password manager at `chrome://password-manager/passwords`. You can save, view, edit, delete, and sync passwords across devices. For stronger security, pair it with Bitwarden or 1Password.

## Chrome's Built-in Password Manager {#builtin}

Google Chrome includes a free password manager that automatically saves login credentials and syncs them across your devices when you're signed into your Google account.

**Access it at:** `chrome://password-manager/passwords`

Features:
- Auto-save when you log into sites
- Auto-fill on return visits
- Password strength checker
- Breach detection alerts
- Cross-device sync via Google account

---

## How to Save Passwords in Chrome {#save}

**Automatic saving:**
1. Log into any website
2. Chrome shows a popup: "Save password?"
3. Click **Save**

**If Chrome doesn't offer to save:**
1. Click the **key icon** in the address bar after logging in
2. Or go to `chrome://settings/passwords` and enable "Offer to save passwords"

---

## How to View Saved Passwords {#view}

1. Go to `chrome://password-manager/passwords`
2. You'll see a list of all saved sites and usernames
3. Click any entry to see the password (requires device PIN or Google verification)

Or via Settings:
1. Chrome menu → Settings → Autofill → Password Manager

---

## How to Edit or Delete Passwords {#edit}

**Edit a saved password:**
1. Go to `chrome://password-manager/passwords`
2. Click the site entry
3. Click the **pencil icon** to edit username or password
4. Click **Save**

**Delete a saved password:**
1. Find the entry at `chrome://password-manager/passwords`
2. Click the **three-dot menu** next to it
3. Click **Delete**

---

## How to Export Your Chrome Passwords {#export}

1. Go to `chrome://password-manager/passwords`
2. Click the **Settings gear** icon
3. Click **Export passwords**
4. Authenticate with your device PIN
5. Choose a save location — file saves as a **.csv**

> ⚠️ **Warning:** The exported CSV file contains all your passwords in plain text. Store it securely and delete it after use.

## FAQ {#faq}

**Is Chrome's password manager secure?**
Yes, but it's tied to your Google account security. If your Google account is compromised, your passwords are at risk. Use two-factor authentication on your Google account.

**Can I use Chrome's password manager offline?**
Saved passwords are cached locally, so autofill works offline. But syncing requires an internet connection.

**Should I use Chrome's password manager or Bitwarden?**
Bitwarden is more secure for most users because it's end-to-end encrypted and not tied to a Google account that controls much of your digital life.

**Can Chrome auto-fill passwords on all sites?**
Almost all sites. Some banking sites disable autofill intentionally. You can still copy-paste passwords from the password manager manually.
