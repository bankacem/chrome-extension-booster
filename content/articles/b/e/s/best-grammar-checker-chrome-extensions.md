## The 7 Best Grammar Checker Chrome Extensions

### 1. Grammarly: The Undisputed King
Grammarly remains the gold standard. In 2026, its AI has evolved to not only catch errors but to understand the "intent" behind your writing.
*   **Pros:** Works in almost every text field online; incredible tone detector.
*   **Cons:** Premium version is expensive.

### 2. ProWritingAid: The Choice for Authors
If you are writing long-form content, ProWritingAid offers reports on "Sticky Sentences," "Clichés," and "Sentence Variation" that Grammarly misses.
*   **Pros:** Enterprise-grade stylistic reports.
*   **Cons:** Can be overwhelming for casual email users.

### 3. LanguageTool: The Multilingual Specialist
Do you write in English and Spanish? Or German? LanguageTool is open-source and provides better support for non-English languages than any competitor.
*   **Pros:** Support for 30+ languages; privacy-focused.
*   **Cons:** Interface is less polished than Grammarly.

### 4. QuillBot: The Paraphrasing Powerhouse
QuillBot is famous for its "Co-Writer" and paraphrasing tool, making it perfect for students and academic researchers.
*   **Pros:** Best at rewriting awkward sentences.
*   **Cons:** Grammar checking is slightly less accurate than the top two.

### 5. Microsoft Editor
If you already use Microsoft 365, this extension is a no-brainer. It brings the power of Word's spellcheck to your browser.
*   **Pros:** Included with M365; clean, distraction-free.
*   **Cons:** Lacks advanced stylistic suggestions.

### 6. Ginger Software
Ginger is one of the oldest players and offers a unique "Personal Trainer" feature that helps you learn from your common mistakes.
*   **Pros:** Good for ESL (English as Second Language) learners.
*   **Cons:** Extension can be a bit buggy on complex sites.

### 7. Wordtune
Wordtune focuses less on "right vs. wrong" and more on "how to say it better." It offers 10 different ways to rephrase every sentence.
*   **Pros:** Incredible for creative inspiration.
*   **Cons:** Limited daily free rephrases.

## What to Look for in a Grammar Extension

When choosing the **best grammar checker Chrome extension** for your workflow, consider these four factors:

1.  **Platform Support:** Does it work in Google Docs, Notion, and Gmail? (Some tools struggle with Docs).
2.  **Latency:** Does it slow down your typing? Heavy extensions can cause a delay as the AI processes your text.
3.  **Privacy:** Check the privacy policy. Does the tool store your text on its servers or process it locally?
4.  **Tone Detection:** Does it know when you are being formal (business email) vs. casual (social media)?

## FAQ: Writing Professionally with AI

### Are free grammar checkers safe?
Most reputable ones are safe, but they monetize by upselling you to premium. Avoid random, unknown extensions that don't have a clear business model, as they may be logging your keystrokes.

### Can grammar extensions replace human editors?
For emails and blogs, yes. For a 50,000-word novel or a legal contract? No. AI can't yet understand the nuance of human emotion or complex legal precedence perfectly.

### Which extension works best for Gmail?
Grammarly and Microsoft Editor have the deepest integration with Gmail's compose window.

### Does using a grammar checker count as AI "cheating" in school?
Most universities encourage the use of grammar checkers like Grammarly as a learning tool, but always check your specific syllabus.
