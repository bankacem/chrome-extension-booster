Juggling two windows — a document on one side, a browser on the other — is a daily reality for students, writers, developers, and anyone who compares information online. **Dualless chrome** is a lightweight extension built for exactly this problem: it splits your Chrome window into two side-by-side panes so you can read one page while working on another, without buying a second monitor or memorizing window-snapping shortcuts.

Unlike full-blown window managers, Dualless chrome keeps things deliberately simple. One click on the toolbar icon, one split ratio, and you are working in a two-pane layout. This guide explains what the extension actually does, how to install it, which split ratios work best for which tasks, and how it compares to the split-screen features already built into Windows, macOS, and ChromeOS.

## What Is the Dualless Chrome Extension?

![Developer workspace with two code panels side by side, illustrating the dualless chrome concept](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1555066931-4365d14bab8c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

Dualless chrome is a free split-screen extension for the Chrome browser. When you activate it, the current window is divided into two panes: your existing tab stays on one side, and a new pane opens on the other where you can browse anywhere else. The idea is to give you the "two monitors" experience inside a single browser window.

The extension is intentionally minimal. There is no dashboard, no configuration wizard, and no sign-up. You get a small menu of preset split ratios — typically ranging from roughly 30/70 up to 70/30, with 50/50 in the middle — plus a maximize option that temporarily returns the active pane to full width. If you are new to how extensions work in general, our guide on [what a browser extension is](/blog/what-is-a-browser-extension-2026) covers the fundamentals before you install anything.

Because the tool does one job, it has stayed popular for years with people who only occasionally need a split view and do not want a heavier productivity suite running in the background.

## Why Split-Screen Browsing With Dualless Chrome Improves Focus

![Person working across two side-by-side browser panes on a single monitor](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1516116216624-53e697fedbea?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

Alt-tabbing between full-screen windows sounds harmless, but every switch forces your brain to reload context: where was that paragraph, which tab had the form, what was the number you were copying. A fixed two-pane layout removes most of that overhead because both sources of information stay visible at all times.

The practical benefits show up in everyday tasks:

- **Research and note-taking:** keep the source article on the left and your notes document on the right, so quoting and summarizing becomes a glance instead of a scavenger hunt.
- **Data entry and cross-referencing:** read values from one pane while typing them into a form in the other — fewer mistakes, fewer window switches.
- **Learning:** follow a tutorial in one pane while practicing in the other, which is far easier than pausing a full-screen video every few seconds.
- **Meetings:** watch a presentation or shared screen in one pane and keep your own notes or agenda visible beside it. Pairing the layout with a tool like the [Zoom Chrome extension guide](/blog/zoom-chrome-extension-guide) makes video calls much easier to manage this way.

A stable split view also quietly reduces tab sprawl. When two panes cover your main task, you are less likely to open a fifth "temporary" tab that never gets closed.

## How to Install and Set Up Dualless Chrome

![Laptop screen showing the Chrome Web Store install flow for dualless chrome](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1542831371-29b0f74f9713?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

Setup takes less than two minutes, as described in <a href="https://support.google.com/chrome_webstore/answer/2664769?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Web Store Help: install and manage extensions</a>:

1. Open the Chrome Web Store and search for "Dualless".
2. Click **Add to Chrome**, then confirm by selecting **Add extension** in the permission dialog.
3. Pin the icon: click the puzzle-piece icon in the toolbar, find Dualless, and click the pin so it stays visible.
4. Open any page you want on the left side, click the Dualless icon, and choose a ratio such as 50/50.
5. Your window splits immediately — navigate the new right-hand pane to whatever you need alongside the first page.

If the icon does not appear after installing, check that you are on the regular Chrome browser and that the extension is enabled under `chrome://extensions`. The <a href="https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official Chrome for Developers extension documentation</a> explains how extension permissions and toolbar behavior work if you want the underlying details.

To undo a split, click the maximize option in the Dualless menu (or simply close the pane you no longer need). Your tabs are never lost — each pane is just a normal Chrome window.

## Dualless Chrome Split Ratios and Layouts Explained

![Diagram-like photo of unequal window columns representing dualless chrome ratios](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1587620962725-abab7fe55159?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

The preset ratios are the heart of the tool, and picking the right one matters more than most people expect:

- **70/30:** the workhorse for note-taking. Give the dominant pane to the page you are actually reading, and keep a narrow notes column on the side.
- **60/40:** balanced reading. Comfortable for long-form articles beside a second tab, without squeezing either pane.
- **50/50:** true side-by-side comparison — two product pages, two documents, or a translation next to the original text.
- **30/70:** the mirror of 70/30, useful when the secondary task (chat, notes, a small video) should stay out of the way.
- **Maximize:** temporarily expands the active pane to full width — handy when a page needs a closer look before you return to the split.

One detail worth knowing: the split applies to the window you click from. If you work with multiple Chrome windows, each one can hold its own layout, which is convenient but easy to forget when a pane seems to "disappear" behind another window.

## Dualless Chrome vs Built-In Split Screen on Windows, macOS, and ChromeOS

![Comparison of a tiled desktop setup versus a browser-only split view for dualless chrome users](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1461749280684-dccba630e2f6?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

Modern operating systems already offer window tiling, so when is an extension the better choice?

| Feature | Dualless chrome | Windows Snap Layouts | macOS Split View | Chromebook split screen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scope | Chrome windows only | Any app window | Any two apps | Any app window |
| Setup effort | One click from toolbar | Hover the maximize button / drag | Long-press the green button | Drag window to edge |
| Preset ratios | Yes (multiple) | Yes (grid of layouts) | 50/50 only | 50/50 or manual drag |
| Works inside browser UI | Yes, feels native | No — tiles whole apps | No — full-screen mode | No — tiles whole apps |
| Best for | Fast, browser-only splits | Mixed app workflows | Distraction-free pairs | Quick two-app study setups |

The rule of thumb: if both halves of your task live in the browser, Dualless chrome is the fastest path. If you need to tile Chrome beside a native app like Excel or Slack, use the operating system's tiling instead, because the extension cannot manage non-browser windows.

## Tips, Limitations, and Troubleshooting for Dualless Chrome

![Notebook with troubleshooting checklist beside a laptop showing split browser panes](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1481487196290-c152efe083f5?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

Dualless chrome is simple, but a few practical notes will save you time:

- **It is an older, minimal extension.** If the toolbar menu ever misbehaves after a Chrome update, removing and reinstalling the extension usually resolves it. Check `chrome://extensions` first to confirm it is enabled.
- **Panes are separate windows.** If a pane vanishes, look for an untiled Chrome window behind the main one rather than assuming a tab was closed.
- **Memory stays reasonable.** Two panes are just two windows; heavy slowdowns usually come from the sites themselves. Our [Chrome memory troubleshooting guide](/blog/why-is-chrome-using-so-much-memory-2026-fixes) covers the deeper fixes.
- **Companion extensions help.** A tab suspender such as [ProTab Suspender](/extension/protab-suspender) keeps idle background tabs from eating memory, while [Redirect Shield](/extension/redirect-shield) blocks malicious redirects — both pair well with a two-pane workflow. For distraction-heavy side panes, [Light Popup Blocker](/extension/light-popup-blocker) keeps popups out of the picture.

Treat the extension as a focused utility: when a task genuinely needs two visible pages, split; when it does not, maximize and enjoy the full width.

## Frequently Asked Questions

![FAQ notes beside a laptop running a two-pane dualless chrome layout](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1504384308090-c894fdcc538d?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

### Is Dualless chrome free to use?

Yes. The extension is free and does not require an account or subscription. Its cost comes only in the form of the small permission footprint typical of window-management extensions.

### Does Dualless chrome work on Mac, Windows, and ChromeOS?

Yes. Because it operates inside Chrome rather than at the operating-system level, the same split behavior works wherever desktop Chrome runs, including macOS, Windows, Linux, and ChromeOS.

### Will splitting the window slow my computer down?

Not by itself. Each pane is a normal Chrome window, so resource use depends on the sites you have open, not the split. Suspending inactive tabs makes a bigger difference than the layout does.

### How do I exit a split and go back to a single full-size window?

Click the maximize option in the Dualless menu to expand the active pane to full width, or simply close the pane you no longer need. Tabs in the closed pane are closed with it, so save any work first.

### Is Dualless chrome safe?

It performs only window splitting — it does not read or alter page content beyond what Chrome's extension model allows. Install it from the official Chrome Web Store listing and review the permissions it requests, as you should with any extension.
