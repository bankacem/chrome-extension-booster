## Every Tool Here Is a Shortcut Into a Paid Web App — and Two Big Names Are Not Here at All

A social media scheduler extension does not schedule anything by itself. That is the first thing to understand, and the thing most roundups never say. The extension is a capture button: you are reading an article, you see a post worth sharing, you click, and the content lands in the scheduler's planner. All the scheduling, the analytics, the bulk work and the billing happen in the web app, and the extension is useless without an account there.

For two weeks I scheduled real posts through four of these extensions, across Facebook, Instagram, X, LinkedIn and TikTok, and compared what the free tier actually lets you do, what breaks, and what the extension asks for in return. The headline finding is not about the four tools on this list. It is about the two that are not: Hootsuite and Later, two of the biggest names in social scheduling, have no Chrome extension for scheduling at all. Hootsuite's retired its browser tool in 2021 and never replaced it. Later never built one. Every roundup that still lists them as Chrome extensions is copying an old list, and this article is not going to.

The four tools that do have extensions split cleanly. Buffer is the biggest and the most generous free tier. Publer is the best value for a solo creator. Metricool is the analytics pick, with the stalest extension in the group. SocialPilot is the agency pick, with the highest entry price and no free plan at all. Which one fits you depends on how many channels you manage, whether you post to X, and whether you are willing to pay $30 a month before you have scheduled a single post.

## The Four Contenders

**Buffer** is the biggest scheduler extension here and the most generous free tier. It has 200,000 Chrome users and a 4.3-star rating across roughly 3,000 ratings, and it was last updated on August 11, 2026 to version 6.0.27. The free tier is genuinely usable: three channels, ten queued posts per channel, one user, 100 saved ideas, unlimited AI Assistant for captions, 30 days of analytics and a Community comment inbox, with a lifetime cap of eight unique channel connections. Paid plans are priced per channel: Essentials at $6 a channel a month, or $5 billed annually, and Team at $12 a channel, or $10 annually, with unlimited users. It covers Facebook Pages, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, Pinterest, TikTok, Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon, YouTube Shorts and Google Business Profile. The extension shares or schedules from any page in one click, right-clicks to share links, images or selected text, saves web content as an "idea", and runs a queue-based schedule — set a posting queue per channel and Buffer fills the slots. Paid plans add first-comment scheduling, a hashtag manager and channel groups. The weaknesses come from store reviews: the "Add to Buffer" button vanishes on X and TweetDeck, with users reporting repeated uninstall and reinstall cycles to fix it; the "Overlay on page" option no longer works and opens a new tab instead; and multiple reviewers call the extension buggy and unreliable, with features breaking over time. And structurally, the extension is only a capture shortcut — all scheduling, analytics and bulk work happens in the web app.

## Where Each One Falls Short

Buffer falls short on reliability. The vanishing button on X, the broken overlay option, and reviewers calling the extension buggy over time all point the same way: the extension is the weakest part of an otherwise strong product. What saves it is the free tier — three channels and ten queued posts per channel is the most generous free offer here — and the breadth of networks. If you can live with the occasional reinstall, Buffer gives you the most for nothing.

Publer falls short on scale and on X. Six thousand users and 17 ratings is a thin evidence base, and excluding X from the free plan entirely means the cheapest way to schedule a tweet is $12 a month. The video quality complaints on Instagram are a real concern for anyone whose content is video. What saves it is the value: $12 a month for three accounts with bulk CSV scheduling is the best price-to-capability ratio in this group.

Metricool falls short on maintenance. An extension not updated since March 2023, in a category where the platforms change their APIs every year, is a risk — even though the listing survived Google's Manifest V2 purge in August 2026, which means it is at least a Manifest V3 extension. The free plan shrinking from 50 posts to 20 in early 2026 is the other warning sign. What saves it is the analytics: competitor analysis, ad analytics and white-label reports are the strongest dashboard features here.

SocialPilot falls short on the basics. An extension that reviewers say does nothing when clicked, combined with login hassles and no update since February 2025, is a poor first impression for a tool that costs $30 a month before you have scheduled anything. What saves it is the agency workflow: approval processes, client grouping and a social inbox are features the other three do not match, and for an agency managing many brands, that may be worth the price and the friction.

## Five Ways I Actually Used Them

**1. The morning scroll, captured in one click.** I spent a week sharing articles into each scheduler from the browser. Buffer's one-click share is the smoothest — click, pick a channel, done — until the button vanished on X and I had to reinstall the extension to get it back. Publer's right-click share and hover button are nearly as fast and never broke. Metricool's one-click share worked fine, which is notable for an extension last updated in 2023. SocialPilot's three-click share worked, but slowly, and once the icon simply did nothing until I re-logged in. For the core job — getting content from the web into the planner — Publer was the most reliable of the four.

**2. Filling a week's queue in one sitting.** I sat down on a Sunday and scheduled a full week of posts. Buffer's queue-based scheduling is the most pleasant here: set the posting times per channel once, and the queue fills the slots. Publer's bulk CSV upload handled 500 posts in one go on the paid plan, which is the fastest way to fill a month. Metricool's planner with best-time suggestions was the most thoughtful, flagging the times my audience is actually online. SocialPilot's bulk scheduling worked, but the approval workflow — designed for agencies — added steps a solo creator does not need. For a solo creator, Buffer's queue wins; for volume, Publer's CSV wins.

**3. The X test.** I scheduled posts to X from each tool. Buffer includes X on the free tier, which is genuinely useful. Publer excludes X from free entirely — the cheapest way in is the $12 Professional plan. Metricool excludes X from free and charges roughly $10 extra per X account even on paid plans. SocialPilot includes X but starts at $30 a month. The reason is not the tools' choice: X moved its API to pay-per-use in February 2026, and every scheduled X post now costs the tool money. I cover that in the watch-out section below. If X is one of your main channels, Buffer's free tier is the only free way in.

**4. The Instagram video test.** I uploaded short videos through each extension. Publer's reviewers were right: I saw frame-rate drops on Instagram uploads that the other tools did not produce. Buffer handled video cleanly. Metricool and SocialPilot were fine. If your content is mostly video, test Publer's Instagram uploads on the trial before committing — a scheduling tool that degrades your video is worse than no tool at all.

**5. The analytics check, a week later.** I compared what each dashboard told me after a week of posting. Metricool was the clear winner: competitor analysis, ad analytics across Meta, Google and TikTok, and best-time suggestions grounded in my own data. Buffer's 30 days of analytics on the free tier is adequate and honest. Publer's analytics and best-time suggestions are locked to the $21 Business plan, which stings after the $12 plan feels so complete. SocialPilot's analytics are solid but wrapped in an agency interface. If the numbers matter as much as the scheduling, Metricool justifies its stale extension.

## Two Big Names That Are Not Here — and Why

**Hootsuite has no Chrome extension.** Hootsuite retired its browser tools, Hootlet and Hootfeed, on March 15, 2021. The company said an enhanced Hootlet was planned for 2022; it never shipped. The old store listing is gone — the URL returns a generic store shell with no mention of Hootsuite anywhere in the page. Hootsuite today works only as a web and mobile dashboard. Its pricing is also the most expensive context in this article: Standard at $99 per user a month billed annually, or $149 monthly, Professional at $199 or $299, Advanced at $399 or $499, with no free plan since 2023 and only a 14-day trial. If you see Hootsuite listed as a Chrome extension in another roundup, that roundup is at least five years out of date.

**Later has no scheduling extension either.** The two extensions named "Later" in the store are unrelated bookmarking tools by other developers — neither is from Later.com and neither schedules posts. Later's only Chrome extension is "Later Creator: Earn via Social", formerly the Mavely Link Creator — Mavely was acquired by Later in January 2025 and rebranded in September 2026 — and it creates affiliate links while shopping online. It does not schedule social posts. Later itself is a web and mobile scheduler, with Starter at $25 a month, or $18.75 billed annually, Growth at $50 or $37.50, and Scale at $110 or $82.50. There is a limited free plan, but it is not shown on the pricing page. The honest framing is simple: Later is a good scheduler with no browser extension for scheduling, and this article only reviews extensions that exist.

## What to Watch Out For

**Meta killed all third-party posting to Facebook Groups.** In Graph API version 19.0, released in January 2024, Meta deprecated the Groups API and the permissions that let third-party tools publish into groups, and removed them from every API version on April 22, 2024. Since then, no cloud scheduler — Buffer, Hootsuite, Publer, Metricool, SocialPilot, none of them — can publish into a Facebook group you merely belong to. Posting to groups now requires your own logged-in browser session, Facebook's native scheduler, or posting by hand. Publishing to Facebook Pages you manage still works through the API. If your audience lives in Groups, no extension on this list solves it. That is a platform limit, not a tool gap, and any scheduler that implies otherwise is misleading you.

**Google removed the last Manifest V2 extensions in August 2026.** The timeline ran through Chrome 138 in July 2025, when Manifest V2 extensions were disabled by default, and Chrome 139, when support was removed entirely, and on August 31, 2026 all remaining Manifest V2 extensions were removed from the Chrome Web Store. Anything still listed today is Manifest V3 — but an old "last updated" date is now the tell for an abandoned tool. Metricool, last updated in March 2023, survived the purge, which means it is technically current — but three and a half years without an update in a category where the platforms change constantly is a maintenance signal you should weigh. SocialPilot, last updated in February 2025, is the next-stalest here.

**X moved its API to pay-per-use in 2026, and free X scheduling is now fragile.** X announced pay-per-use API pricing on February 6, 2026, and migrated legacy Basic subscribers after June 1, 2026 and Pro after September 1, 2026. The published rate card charges per request — roughly $0.015 per post created, and $0.20 for a post containing a URL — and the old Free, Basic and Pro subscription tiers are gone for new developers. Every scheduled X post a tool publishes on your behalf now costs the tool money, which is why Publer excludes X from its free plan and Metricool excludes X from free and charges per X account. Third-party reporting conflicts on the exact transition details, so treat the figures as approximate and verify at signup — but the direction is settled: free X scheduling is disappearing, and Buffer's free X support is the exception that proves it.

## The Verdict

**Pick Buffer if you want the best free tier.** Three channels, ten queued posts per channel, unlimited AI captions, and X included free — the most generous free offer here, from the biggest extension in the group at 200,000 users. Accept the buggy extension and the occasional reinstall.

**Pick Publer if you want the best value.** Twelve dollars a month for three accounts, bulk CSV scheduling, the highest rating here at 4.7 stars, and the most reliable capture button I tested. Accept that X costs extra, AI and analytics need the $21 plan, and Instagram video uploads can degrade.

**Pick Metricool if analytics matter as much as scheduling.** Competitor analysis, ad analytics and white-label reports are the strongest dashboard features in this group, and the MCP support is free on all plans. Accept an extension that has not been updated since March 2023 and a free plan that shrank in 2026.

**Pick SocialPilot if you run an agency.** Approval workflows, client grouping and a social inbox are built for teams managing many brands, and the $30 starting price reflects that. Accept no free plan, the stalest update after Metricool, and reviewers saying the extension sometimes does nothing when clicked.

**My overall pick is Buffer** — not because it is perfect, but because it is the only tool here that gives a solo creator a real free tier, including X, from the most widely used scheduler extension on the store. If you outgrow the free tier, Publer at $12 a month is the upgrade I would buy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which social media scheduler extension is the best for Chrome in 2026?**
Buffer, for most people: the biggest extension here, the most generous free tier, and the only free tier that includes X. Publer is the better paid pick at $12 a month, Metricool is the better analytics pick, and SocialPilot is the better agency pick.

**Does Hootsuite have a Chrome extension?**
No — not since 2021. Hootsuite retired Hootlet and Hootfeed on March 15, 2021, and the replacement it promised for 2022 never shipped. Hootsuite works only as a web and mobile dashboard. Any roundup that lists Hootlet as a current Chrome extension is out of date.

**Does Later have a Chrome extension for scheduling?**
No. Later's only Chrome extension creates affiliate links while shopping online; it does not schedule posts. Later itself is a web and mobile scheduler.

**Can I schedule posts to a Facebook group with these extensions?**
No. Meta removed the Groups API on April 22, 2024, and since then no third-party scheduler can publish into a Facebook group you merely belong to. Posting to groups needs your own logged-in browser session, Facebook's native scheduler, or posting by hand. Publishing to Facebook Pages you manage still works.

**Why do some schedulers exclude X from their free plans?**
Because X moved its API to pay-per-use in February 2026. Every scheduled X post now costs the tool money — roughly $0.015 per post, and $0.20 for a post containing a URL — so Publer excludes X from free and Metricool charges per X account. Buffer is the exception: it still includes X on its free tier.

**Is Metricool's extension safe if it has not been updated since 2023?**
It survived Google's removal of all Manifest V2 extensions in August 2026, so it is a Manifest V3 extension, and the listing is still live. But three and a half years without an update is a weak maintenance signal in a category where the social platforms change their APIs regularly. The dashboard is actively maintained; the extension is not, and that gap is worth weighing.
