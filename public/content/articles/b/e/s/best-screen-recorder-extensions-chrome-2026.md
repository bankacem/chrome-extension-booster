## I Spent Three Weeks Recording Everything With Four Extensions

I used to explain things in writing. Then I counted the cost: a bug report that took me forty minutes to write, with eight screenshots and three paragraphs of "click here, then scroll down, then look at the thing that should not be there", when a two-minute video would have shown all of it. A client update that needed three back-and-forth emails, when a three-minute walkthrough would have ended the thread. I was spending my time describing screens instead of showing them, and the descriptions were worse than the screens.

So for three weeks I recorded the same work four ways: a product walkthrough for a client, a bug report for a developer, a forty-five-minute lesson, a quick piece of feedback for a colleague, and a short demo I needed as a file I could keep. I ran each job through all four, and watched where each free plan stopped me, which exports carried a watermark, and which recordings I could find again a week later.

The four tools split into two camps, and the split matters more than any feature list. Loom and Screencastify are cloud-first: you record, and the video lives on their servers behind a share link. Nimbus and Awesome Screenshot are capture-first: they start from screenshots and annotation, and recording is one more thing the same toolbar does, with files you can save to your own computer. Which camp fits you depends on a question no roundup usually asks first: do you want a link, or do you want a file?

## The Four Contenders

**Loom** is the biggest name in this category: about 8 million users on the Chrome Web Store, now developed by Atlassian. It records your screen, camera bubble, microphone and system audio, then hands you a share link the moment you stop — no export step. The watch page supports comments and reactions, viewer insights show who watched, and the free plan includes transcripts in 50+ languages. The free plan is where the limits bite: 25 videos per person, five minutes per recording, and quality capped at 720p. Business costs $18 per user per month: no video-count or time limits, quality up to 4K, trim and stitch editing, no Loom branding, and you can download your own videos. Business + AI is $24 and adds summaries, chapters and filler-word removal. One flag: the listing showed 2.7 out of 5 from about 10,200 ratings in November 2026, down from 4.6 in a September snapshot with the same count. I would read the recent reviews before paying. Full details are on the [Loom pricing page](https://www.loom.com/pricing).

**Get it:** [Loom — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/loom-screen-recorder-scre/liecbddmohgnpijdlmfapmbpfgdmbcb)

**Screencastify** is the biggest tool here by installs — about 9 million users, with a 3.5 out of 5 rating from roughly 11,700 ratings — and the one built most deliberately for classrooms. It records a tab, your full screen or your webcam, with a teleprompter, drawing tools, questions and polls, and one-click sharing to Google Drive and Google Classroom. The free plan gives you 10 videos, each up to 30 minutes, and your exports carry a Castify watermark. Starter lists unlimited videos up to 60 minutes each, no watermark, up to 1080p; Pro lists unlimited videos up to 180 minutes each, up to 4K, AI enhancements, captions and transcripts, and 90+ language translations. The pricing page shows no dollar prices in its current form, so I quote the September 2026 snapshot other reviewers verified: Starter $7 per user per month billed annually ($19 month to month), Pro $10 billed annually ($25 month to month). Check the [live pricing page](https://www.screencastify.com/pricing) before you buy. Screencastify is now part of Castify, and the only tool here leading with school compliance: FERPA, COPPA and CSPC, plus SOC 2 Type II certification.

**Get it:** [Screencastify — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/screencastify-screen-vide/mmeijimgabbpbgpdklnllpncmdofkcpn)

**Nimbus Screenshot & Video Recorder** is the tool with the most complicated story in this group, and it is the reason I checked every listing myself instead of copying an old roundup. The Nimbus you remember — millions of users, a 4.7-star rating — no longer exists under that name. The old Chrome Web Store listing is gone. The same company, Nimbus Web, now trading as FuseBase, sells the same toolkit as FuseBase Pro, on a new listing with about 10,000 users and a 3.6 out of 5 rating from roughly 39 ratings. The toolkit is still here: full-page, visible-area, selected-region and fragment screenshots, tab or desktop recording with microphone and webcam, on-screen drawing and blur, and an image editor with arrows, text, shapes and blur. The free tier is generous for plain screenshots — visible-area, selected-area and fragment captures are unmetered, and you can edit and save them locally without an account — but it runs out fast: five full-page captures, five-minute recordings, and trimming, cropping, MP4/GIF conversion, watermarks and Drive, Dropbox and YouTube uploads all sit behind the paid plan. The paid price is the one number I could not verify: the order page names a yearly Capture PRO Solo licence but showed no total in November 2026, and the only figure anywhere is $60 a year from 2025 user reviews. The capture itself is described on the [Nimbus capture page](https://nimbusweb.me/screenshot/).

**Get it:** [FuseBase Pro (formerly Nimbus) — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fusebase-pro-capture-scre/fddbloohgcjopkmnjdeodjcfbgiimpcn)

**Awesome Screenshot** is the highest-rated tool in this group by a wide margin — about 4 million users and a 4.7 out of 5 rating from roughly 29,600 ratings — and the only one whose free plan never watermarks anything. It is the all-in-one: screenshots, annotation and recording in one toolbar, with cloud storage behind it. The free plan gives you up to 100 screenshots and 20 recordings in the cloud, each recording up to 6 hours long at up to 720p, and you can record and download in WebM or MP4. The catch is that local and cloud limits differ, and the extension does not explain that clearly: local screenshots are unlimited, but local recordings are capped at five minutes each, and the free editor only works on recordings under an hour. Basic costs $5 a month billed annually ($6 month to month): unlimited screenshots, all annotation tools. Professional costs $6 billed annually ($8 month to month): unlimited recordings and screenshots at up to 4K. For a tool that does screenshots and video in one place, that is the cheapest paid tier in this group. The full breakdown is on the [Awesome Screenshot pricing page](https://www.awesomescreenshot.com/pricing).

**Get it:** [Awesome Screen Recorder & Screenshot — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/awesome-screen-recorder-s/nlipoenfbbikpbjkfpfillcgkoblgpmij)

## Where Each One Falls Short

Loom's weakness is the free plan, and it is not close. Five minutes per recording sounds workable until you record your first real walkthrough and hit the wall at minute five with the conclusion still unrecorded. Twenty-five videos sounds like plenty until you realise the count is per person and does not reset — about two busy weeks for someone who records instead of typing. And the limitation I disliked most is the one nobody mentions in the marketing: on the free plan you cannot download your own videos. Upload and download are Business features, so the only way to get a recording out is to pay $18 a month.

Screencastify's weakness is that its free plan is a countdown, not a tier. Ten videos, total, for the life of the account, and deleting an old video does not give you the slot back. You can earn five bonus videos per person you invite, up to four invites, but that is a referral scheme wearing a free plan's clothes. The watermark on free exports is also the most visible in this group — it says Castify on every video you share, which is fine for a classroom and embarrassing in a client deliverable. The extension warns that recording is CPU-intensive, which I felt on an older laptop: with a dozen tabs open, the recording stuttered where Loom's did not.

Nimbus — FuseBase Pro, as it is now called — has a weakness no feature list captures: you cannot trust what you remember about it. Every old roundup still lists it as a 4-million-user, 4.7-star free tool, and none of that is true any more. The new listing has 39 ratings, so almost nobody has reviewed the tool as it exists today. The free tier's five full-page captures run out in a single afternoon of real work, the paid price is not published on the page that sells it, and the package is heavy — about 36 MB including a bundled copy of ffmpeg, with a content script loaded into nearly every page you open. The editor is genuinely good, but the product is still rebuilding its reputation from scratch.

Awesome Screenshot's weakness is confusion. The free plan is generous on paper — 20 cloud recordings of up to 6 hours each is more recording time than anyone in this group offers for free — but the line between local and cloud limits is buried, and I only found it by hitting it: a local recording stopped at five minutes, and a cloud recording I expected to edit would not open in the editor because it ran past an hour. The browser-only scope is the other limit: the extension records tabs, and for anything outside the browser you need the separate desktop app.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The four-minute product walkthrough for a client.** I recorded the same feature tour four times, once in each tool, and sent each version to the same colleague. Loom won on the experience: the link opened instantly, the comments let her ask a question at 1:42, and the viewer insight told me she watched the whole thing. Awesome Screenshot's version looked just as good, but I had to share a cloud link from a tool she had never heard of. Screencastify's version carried the Castify watermark across a client deliverable, which I would not send again. Nimbus worked, but the five-minute free cap left no room for a second take.

**The forty-five-minute lesson.** This is where the free plans separate completely. Screencastify handled it in two recordings — 30 minutes is its per-video cap, so I had to build the break into the lesson plan. Loom stopped at five minutes, which made it useless for this job on the free plan. Nimbus stopped at five minutes too. Awesome Screenshot recorded the whole 45 minutes to the cloud without complaint, at 720p, and the file was still there a week later. If you teach or train and do not want to pay, the length question answers itself: its free cloud recordings run up to six hours, and nothing else here comes close.

**The bug report with annotations.** A developer does not need a video of a bug; they need a video of a bug with a circle around the broken thing and an arrow pointing at the error message. Nimbus has the best editor for this — arrows, numbered markers, blur for the parts of the screen that show customer data — and Awesome Screenshot is close behind, with annotation tools you can use while you record. Loom's free plan has no annotation at all; the drawing tool is a Business feature. Screencastify has drawing tools and a blur box, which got the job done, but its editor felt built for lessons rather than bug reports. For this scenario I kept Awesome Screenshot, because annotation and recording live in the same toolbar.

**The Chromebook classroom.** I set this one up the way a teacher would: a Chromebook, a Google Classroom, and a lesson that had to reach students who missed the live class. Screencastify is the only tool here built for this exact room. It saves straight to Google Drive, posts to Classroom, supports the school's compliance requirements, and the questions-and-polls feature turns a passive video into something students answer. Loom works on a Chromebook too, but five-minute recordings do not cover a lesson. Nimbus and Awesome Screenshot record fine, but neither knows what a Classroom is.

**The recording I needed as a file.** I recorded a demo I wanted to keep, edit in another app, and archive on my own drive. Loom failed outright: free users cannot download their videos, so the recording existed only on Loom's servers. Screencastify exported an MP4 without a fight, watermark and all. Nimbus saved a local file with no account and no upload, which is exactly what I wanted from it. Awesome Screenshot saved locally too, once I understood the five-minute local cap and recorded to the cloud instead. If the file matters to you, check the download rules before you record, not after.

## What to Watch Out For: Permissions and Privacy

A screen recorder is the most powerful extension you can install, and you should treat it like one. To do its job it has to see everything on your screen, and the capture tools here ask for broad permissions to match: Nimbus's FuseBase Pro can read and change data on all sites and loads a content script into nearly every page you open, and Awesome Screenshot asks for similar access. That is normal for this category, but read the permission list before you click Add, and remove a recorder you have stopped using instead of leaving it running in every tab.

The deeper issue is what you record. A screen recording captures whatever is on your screen at the time: passwords in a password manager, an API key in a terminal window, a customer's name in a support ticket, a colleague's salary in a spreadsheet you forgot was open. I check my screen before I hit record, every time, and I use the blur tools in Nimbus and Screencastify for anything I missed. Never record a screen that shows something you would not want on someone else's server — and remember that with Loom and Screencastify, your recording is on someone else's server by design.

The cloud question cuts both ways. A share link is the whole point of Loom: the video is ready the second you stop recording. But that also means your recording lives on Loom's infrastructure under Loom's privacy disclosures, which include personally identifiable information, financial and payment information, authentication information, location and user activity. Read what a recording tool collects before you record a client call through it, and never record a meeting or a call without telling the people in it. In several US states and many countries, recording a conversation without the consent of all participants is illegal, and a screen recorder that captures system audio is recording the conversation.

One rule I follow with all four: record the smallest thing that does the job — a tab instead of a full desktop, a five-minute clip instead of an hour, a local file instead of a cloud link when nobody needs to watch it online.

## Two More Worth Knowing About

If you want recordings without an account, a watermark or a time limit, look at [Screenity](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/screenity-screen-recorder/kbbdabhdfibnancpjfhlkhafgdilcnji) first. It is a free, open-source screen recorder with about 200,000 users and a 3.7 out of 5 rating from roughly 765 ratings, and its core extension has no stated time or count cap: you record, annotate, blur sensitive content, and export MP4, GIF or WebM to your own computer or to Google Drive, with no sign-in at all. The trade-off is that there is no hosted share link — you get a file, and sharing it is your job. For anyone whose main complaint about the four tools above is the account, Screenity is the answer.

The other option costs nothing and is already installed: your operating system. Windows Snipping Tool records your screen with Win + Shift + R, and macOS records it with Cmd + Shift + 5, both with no extension, no account and no cloud upload. They are basic — no camera bubble, no annotations, no share link — but for a quick capture on your own machine, they are the fastest tools you own.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which screen recorder extension is actually free?

All four have free plans, but they are not equal. Awesome Screenshot gives you 20 cloud recordings of up to 6 hours each at 720p with no watermark. Screencastify gives you 10 videos of up to 30 minutes each, watermarked. Loom gives you 25 videos of up to 5 minutes each at 720p. Nimbus, now FuseBase Pro, gives you five full-page captures and five-minute recordings. For heavy free use, Awesome Screenshot goes furthest by a wide margin.

### Can I record my screen without creating an account?

Yes, with the capture-first tools. Nimbus lets you take and save screenshots locally without an account, and Awesome Screenshot lets you save screenshots and local recordings without signing in. Screenity goes further: the whole core extension works with no sign-in at all. Loom and Screencastify both require an account before you can record anything.

### Which screen recorder gives me the longest free recording?

Awesome Screenshot, by a long way: its free cloud recordings run up to 6 hours each at 720p. Screencastify is next at 30 minutes per video. Loom and Nimbus both stop free recordings at 5 minutes. Note the trap: local recordings saved to your own computer are capped at 5 minutes each — the 6-hour allowance applies to cloud recordings only.

### Do any of these screen recorders add a watermark?

Screencastify adds a Castify watermark to every export on the free plan, and it is removed only on the paid plans. Loom puts its branding on the free watch page, and free users cannot download videos at all. Awesome Screenshot and Nimbus add no watermark to free recordings, though Nimbus charges for custom watermarks on the paid plan.

### Can I record my whole desktop, or only a browser tab?

All four record a browser tab and can record your full screen, but there are differences in the details. Screencastify and Nimbus both offer desktop recording from the extension, with Nimbus noting that desktop recording drops system sound, the webcam overlay and the drawing tools, which only work when you record a tab. Awesome Screenshot's extension is tab-focused; recording outside the browser needs its separate desktop app. Loom records screen, camera and system audio from the extension.

### Which screen recorder is best for teachers?

Screencastify, and it is not close. It saves straight to Google Drive, posts to Google Classroom, connects to Canvas and Schoology, supports FERPA, COPPA and CSPC compliance, and lets you add questions and polls to a video. The 10-video free limit is the main constraint — a teacher recording daily will need the Starter plan — but no other tool here is built for a classroom at all.

## Verdict

Install **Awesome Screenshot** if you want the best free screen recorder and you do not mind learning where its local limits end and its cloud limits begin. It has the highest rating here at 4.7 stars, it never watermarks your videos, its free plan gives you 20 recordings of up to 6 hours each, and its paid plan is the cheapest here at $5 a month. For most people, it is the one I would install first.

Install **Loom** if you work in a team and want the fastest path from recording to a link your colleagues can comment on. The workflow is the best here — record, stop, paste the link — and Business at $18 a month is fair for what it unlocks. Accept the five-minute free cap and that free users cannot download their videos, and read the recent reviews before you pay, given the rating fell from 4.6 to 2.7 in two months.

Choose **Screencastify** if you teach, train, or live in Google Classroom. Thirty-minute free recordings, Drive integration, and school compliance make it the only real classroom tool in this group, and the Starter plan is good value at $7 a month on annual billing. Accept the ten-video lifetime free limit and the watermark.

And choose **Nimbus — FuseBase Pro** only if you want its editor. The annotation tools are the best in this group, and the free screenshots are genuinely free. But you are adopting a tool rebuilding from a 39-review listing with an unpublished paid price, and Awesome Screenshot does almost everything it does with a far larger user base behind it.

If I had to keep only one, I would keep Awesome Screenshot — the tool whose free plan never once stopped me mid-recording.
