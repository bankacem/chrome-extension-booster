## I Spent Three Weeks Letting AI Take My Meeting Notes

I stopped taking notes in a meeting once, and it cost me a client. Not a big client, but a real one: a thirty-minute call, seven action items, and I remembered four of them. The other three lived in a notebook I never opened again. That is the whole problem these tools exist to solve, and it is also the reason I do not trust them by default. A note taker that hallucinates an action item is worse than no note taker at all, because you act on it with confidence.

So for three weeks I ran four AI meeting note takers through my real working life: client calls on Google Meet, a weekly team standup on Zoom, a long workshop on Microsoft Teams, and one sales call I was not invited to but still needed notes from. I compared the transcripts against my own memory, checked the action items against what was actually agreed, and watched which free plans ran out first.

The four tools split into two camps, and the split matters more than any feature list. Scribbl, Fireflies and Otter send a bot into your meeting — a named participant that records the audio. Tactiq does not. It reads the live captions in your own browser tab and turns them into notes, which means no bot ever appears in the call, but it also means it can only capture meetings you actually attend.

## The Four Contenders

**Scribbl** is the highest-rated tool in this category, and it is not close: a perfect 5.0 stars on the Chrome Web Store across roughly 3,000 ratings, and 4.9 on G2 across about 725 reviews, with about 60,000 users. It works with Google Meet only, and it is honest about that limitation — Scribbl says plainly that it does not support Zoom, and that its bot, called "Scribbl Free Recorder", joins your Meet calls as a visible participant. The free plan covers 10 meetings a month with full transcripts, AI summaries, action items and ChatGPT-powered notes in 32 languages. The Pro plan costs $13 a month on annual billing. What won me over in testing was the accuracy: on a fast, overlapping client call with three speakers, Scribbl's transcript was the cleanest of the four, and its action items matched what I had written down by hand.

**Get it:** [Scribbl — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/scribbl-ai-meeting-notes/kmjmlilenakedodldceipdnmmnfkahni)

**Tactiq** is the biggest tool here by users — about 1 million people run it, with a 4.8 to 4.9-star rating across roughly 7,500 to 8,500 ratings — and the only one that works without a bot. It reads the live captions your meeting platform already generates, so nothing is recorded, no audio ever leaves your browser, and no extra participant shows up in the call. It works across Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams, identifies speakers, and turns captions into AI summaries in more than 70 languages. The free plan gives you 10 transcripts and 5 AI credits a month, and the Pro plan costs $8 a month on annual billing, the cheapest paid plan in this group. The trade-off is real: because it reads captions, not audio, a mis-captioned word becomes a mis-transcribed word, and if you are not in the meeting, Tactiq cannot capture it at all.

**Get it:** [Tactiq — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/tactiq-ai-note-taker-for/fggkaccpbmombhnjkjokndojfgagejfb)

**Fireflies** is the only tool here that is really a sales platform wearing a note taker's clothes. The Chrome extension has about 200,000 users and a 4.6-star rating across roughly 207 ratings, but the extension is just the front door: the real product is a conversation-intelligence platform that syncs meeting notes into your CRM, tracks talk time and topics, and lets a whole team search every call ever recorded. A bot called "Fireflies.ai Notetaker" joins your meetings, and the free plan gives you 400 minutes of storage. The Pro plan costs $10 a month on annual billing, and it supports more than 100 languages. If you run sales calls and your CRM is where the truth lives, Fireflies is the only one of these four built for that job.

**Get it:** [Fireflies — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fireflies-ai-meeting-note/meimoidfecamngeoanhnpdjjdcefoldn)

**Otter** is the oldest name in this category, and it shows in both directions. The Chrome extension has about 300,000 users and a 4.5-star rating across roughly 140 ratings, with a 4.4 on G2 across about 505 reviews — but the recent rating average on the store is only 2.4, and the recurring complaint is a broken sign-in flow: users click sign-in, a tab opens, and the extension never actually signs in. The free Basic plan gives you 300 transcription minutes a month, but only the first 30 minutes of each conversation are viewable, only your 25 most recent conversations stay visible, and you get 3 file imports for the life of the account. The Pro plan costs $8.33 a month on annual billing, or $16.99 month to month. By default a bot joins your calls, though the extension can capture Google Meet and Teams in the browser without one. It supports only English, French and Spanish — the narrowest language coverage here.

**Get it:** [Otter.ai — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/otterai-record-transcribe/bnmojkbbkkonlmlfgejehefjldooiedp)

## Where Each One Falls Short

Scribbl's weakness is scope. Google Meet only, full stop. If your team lives on Zoom or Teams, Scribbl is not an option at all, and that is a strange limitation for a tool this good. The 10-meeting free limit is also tight for anyone in back-to-back calls: ten meetings is about two busy days for a project manager. And the bot is a social problem as much as a technical one — some clients notice a participant called "Scribbl Free Recorder" and ask what it is, which means you spend the first minute of the call explaining your note taker instead of the agenda.

Tactiq's weakness is that it inherits every flaw of live captions. When a speaker mumbles, when two people talk over each other, or when the platform's captioning mishears a product name, Tactiq faithfully transcribes the mistake. I watched it turn a client's name into a different word three times in one call, and the summary then confidently used the wrong name. It also cannot help you with a meeting you did not attend, which is exactly the meeting whose notes you most need. And the free plan's 5 AI credits a month means you get about five summaries before you are paying or pasting transcripts into another AI yourself.

Fireflies' weakness is weight. It is a platform, not a tool, and it behaves like one: accounts, workspaces, CRM connections, settings for who can see which calls. For a freelancer who wants notes from Tuesday's call, that is a lot of machinery. The free plan's 400 minutes of storage also fills faster than it sounds — about six or seven hours of meetings — and once it is full, old calls stop being searchable until you pay. The bot is also the most visible of the four, and in my testing it was the one clients asked about most.

Otter's weakness is the free plan, and it is not close. Thirty minutes viewable per conversation means a 45-minute call gives you notes for two-thirds of it, and the rest is hidden behind the paywall. Twenty-five visible conversations means your notes start disappearing after about a month of regular use. Add the broken sign-in complaints in recent reviews, and Otter feels like a tool resting on a reputation it built years ago. Its transcription is still genuinely accurate — reviewers consistently praise it, including for accented speakers — but accuracy does not matter if the extension will not sign you in.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The client call with seven action items.** I ran the same kind of call — a 30-minute client check-in with decisions scattered through it — with each tool in turn. Scribbl won: it caught all seven action items, assigned each one to the right person, and its summary read like notes a good colleague would write. Fireflies caught six and missed the one agreed in the last two minutes. Tactiq caught five, because the captions mangled two names. Otter caught six, but its free plan only showed me the first 30 minutes, and one action item lived in the final minute.

**The 90-minute workshop.** This is where the free plans separate. Scribbl handled the whole session on one of its 10 free meetings. Fireflies recorded it against the 400-minute storage allowance. Tactiq transcribed it, but the captions drifted badly in the second hour as speakers got tired and started talking over each other. Otter recorded it and then showed me 30 minutes of it. If your meetings regularly run past half an hour, Otter's free plan is not a free plan — it is a demo.

**The multilingual call.** I tested a call that mixed English and Spanish, and a separate call with a French-speaking client. Tactiq handled both best, because its caption-based approach supports more than 70 languages and it does not need to detect the language from audio. Fireflies, with 100+ languages, was close behind. Scribbl handled the Spanish call well but struggled with the French one. Otter cannot do the French call at all — it supports only English, French and Spanish, and in practice its French transcription is its weakest.

**The meeting I did not attend.** A colleague ran a sales call I could not join, and I needed the notes. Tactiq was useless here by design — no attendance, no captions, no notes. Scribbl, Fireflies and Otter all sent their bots in, and all three delivered a usable transcript and summary. This scenario alone decides the category for a lot of people: if you need notes from meetings you skip, you need a bot, and Tactiq is out.

**The sales handoff.** After a discovery call, I needed the notes in the CRM with the deal attached. Only Fireflies did this without extra work: the call was logged, the summary was attached to the contact, and the next rep could search it. With the other three, I copied and pasted. For a solo freelancer that copy-paste costs thirty seconds; for a sales team of ten, it is the difference between a CRM people trust and one they ignore.

## What to Watch Out For: Permissions and Privacy

A meeting note taker is a recording device, and you should treat it like one. Every bot-based tool here joins your meeting as a named participant, which means everyone in the call can see it — and in several US states and many countries, recording a conversation without the consent of all participants is illegal. Tell the room. Say it at the start of the call. It takes five seconds and it keeps you out of trouble.

The deeper issue is what happens to the recording afterwards. Scribbl, Fireflies and Otter all store your meeting audio and transcripts on their servers, because that is where the AI runs. Read each company's data policy before you put a client call through it, and never record a meeting that discusses anything you would not want on someone else's server.

Two lawsuits are worth knowing about. Fireflies was sued in a 2025 class action in California alleging it collected voiceprints without consent under Illinois' biometric privacy law. Otter was sued in August 2025 in California with an allegation that it trained its AI on recorded meetings without consent. I am not a lawyer and neither case has been decided, but the pattern is clear: the companies that record your meetings are being tested on what they do with the recordings. That is a reason to read the privacy policy, not a reason to panic — and it is also a reason Tactiq's approach deserves a second look. Because Tactiq reads captions in your own browser and records no audio, there is simply less data to worry about, and its GDPR-compliant stance is the strongest privacy story in this group.

One rule I follow with all four: never record a meeting you would not be comfortable explaining to the other people in it.

## Two More Worth Knowing About

If your company already pays for Google Workspace, the built-in option is worth checking before you install anything. Google Meet's "Take notes for me" feature, powered by Gemini, sends no third-party bot into the call and keeps the notes inside your Workspace account — but it requires a paid Gemini for Workspace plan, and it only works on Meet. It is the cleanest option for a Meet-only team that already pays Google, and the weakest reason to install a third-party tool.

Microsoft Teams has the same story on the other side: its built-in Intelligent Recap generates summaries and action items after a call, and it lives entirely inside Microsoft's ecosystem. It requires a Teams Premium or Microsoft 365 Copilot licence, which is why most small teams end up with one of the four tools above instead. But if your employer already pays for it, check what you already own before you pay for something new.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do AI note takers announce themselves in the meeting?

The bot-based ones do: Scribbl, Fireflies and Otter all join as a named participant that everyone can see. Tactiq does not, because it reads captions in your own browser and nothing joins the call. Either way, say it out loud at the start of the meeting — it takes five seconds and keeps you legal in all-party-consent jurisdictions.

### Which AI meeting note taker is actually free?

All four have free plans, but they are not equal. Scribbl gives you 10 meetings a month with full transcripts. Tactiq gives you 10 transcripts and 5 AI summaries a month. Fireflies gives you 400 minutes of storage. Otter gives you 300 minutes a month but only shows the first 30 minutes of each call. For light use, Scribbl's free plan goes furthest.

### Can I use an AI note taker without a bot joining my meetings?

Yes — Tactiq is the only one of these four that works without a bot, because it reads the live captions in your browser. The trade-off is that it can only capture meetings you attend, and it inherits caption errors. Otter's extension can also capture Meet and Teams calls in the browser without a bot, but its default is bot-based.

### Is Scribbl really Google Meet only?

Yes. Scribbl says plainly that it only supports Google Meet and does not support Zoom. If your team meets on Zoom or Teams, pick Tactiq or Fireflies instead.

### What happened with the Fireflies and Otter lawsuits?

Fireflies was sued in a 2025 California class action alleging it collected voiceprints without consent under Illinois' biometric privacy law. Otter was sued in August 2025 in California with an allegation that it trained its AI on recorded meetings without consent. Neither case has been decided. Both are reminders to read what a recording tool does with your recordings.

### Which AI note taker is best for sales teams?

Fireflies, by a clear margin. It is the only tool here built as a conversation-intelligence platform: CRM sync, searchable call history, talk-time and topic tracking. The other three take notes; Fireflies builds a record your whole team can use.

## Verdict

Install **Scribbl** if your meetings happen on Google Meet. It had the cleanest transcripts in my testing, a perfect 5.0-star store rating, 10 genuinely usable free meetings a month, and the best summaries of the four. The Meet-only limitation is real, but if Meet is where you live, nothing else here beats it.

Install **Tactiq** if you do not want a bot in your meetings, or if you meet across Zoom, Meet and Teams and care about privacy. Caption-based recording, no audio stored, 70+ languages, and the cheapest Pro plan at $8 a month. Accept that it cannot capture meetings you skip, and that a bad caption becomes a bad note.

Choose **Fireflies** if you run sales calls or work in a team that lives in a CRM. Four hundred free minutes, full conversation intelligence, and the only tool here that turns a call into a searchable team record.

And choose **Otter** only if you are already invested in its ecosystem. Its transcription is still accurate, but the free plan hides part of every long meeting, the recent store reviews flag a broken sign-in flow, and the other three tools give you more for the same money.

If I had to keep only one, I would keep Scribbl — the tool whose notes I stopped double-checking by the second week.
