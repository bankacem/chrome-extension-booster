## I Spent Three Weeks Opening New Tabs on Purpose

I open a new tab about a hundred times a day. Most of the time I know what I want: a site, a search, a document. But a surprising number of those tabs open with no purpose at all — a reflex, a pause between tasks, a moment where my brain reaches for something to look at. What that tab shows me in the first two seconds decides what happens next: a calm photo and one clear task, or a wall of shortcuts and news that eats the next twenty minutes.

For three weeks I ran three new tab dashboards as my daily start page, each for several days at a time, on the same real routine: mornings, research sessions, late-night work, and the aimless tab-opening in between. I tracked which one got me back to work fastest, which one I actually looked at, and which one quietly asked me to pay for the feature I had just started relying on.

The three tools are three different philosophies. Momentum is the inspirational coach: a beautiful photo, a quote, and one thing to focus on today. Bonjourr is the minimalist: an iOS-inspired start page that stays out of your way. Tabliss is the open-source purist: beautiful, free, private, and — as I found out — barely maintained.

## The Three Contenders

**Momentum** is the original new tab dashboard, and still the biggest: more than 3 million users, featured in Tim Ferriss' Tools of Titans, The Wall Street Journal and Apple's Worldwide Developer Conference, and updated as recently as October 19, 2026 (version 2.27.9). Every new tab shows a new background photo, a mantra, a daily quote, the time and weather, and one focus prompt: what is the single most important thing today? The free tier covers all of that, plus a search bar and shortcuts. The Plus plan costs $39.95 a year and adds the features that make Momentum a real productivity tool — a to-do list, Tab Stash for vacuuming up your open tabs, and more customisation. The honest catch, and the one its reviewers keep repeating, is that more and more of Momentum has moved behind the Plus plan over the years, and the free version is now the calm photo and the quote, with the productivity behind a paywall.

**Get it:** [Momentum — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/momentum/laookkfknpbbblfpciffpaejjkokdgca)

**Bonjourr** is the minimalist answer to Momentum, built explicitly as a more modern alternative to it, and the highest-rated tool in this group: 4.9 stars from more than 24,000 ratings, with around 300,000 users. It is an iOS-inspired start page — a clean clock with multiple faces, a search bar, quick links, notes, weather and a Pomodoro timer — on top of high-quality photography that shifts with the time of day. It is completely free, with no paid tier at all, supported by donations. It is open source, it collects no data, it needs no account, and it was featured in Google Chrome's Favorites of 2023. It is also genuinely lightweight at around 1.4 MB, against Momentum's 18 MB. In my three weeks, Bonjourr was the tab I forgot was an extension, which is the highest compliment a start page can get.

**Get it:** [Bonjourr · Minimalist New Tab Page — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/bonjourr-%C2%B7-minimalist-new/dlnejlppicbjfcfcedcflplfjajinajd)

**Tabliss** is the open-source purist's choice: completely free, open source under the GPL-3.0 licence, and it asks for no browser permissions at all — a genuine rarity for a new tab extension. Every new tab is a rotating gallery of Unsplash photography, with a clock, a greeting, and optional widgets for weather, notes and to-do lists, in more than 40 languages. Around 100,000 users run it, with a 4.6 to 4.7-star rating across roughly 387 reviews. And here is the honest problem: the original Tabliss has not been updated since version 2.6.0 on April 25, 2022. In my testing, the Quotes widget had stopped working and a scores widget had fallen over — small breakages that nobody is coming to fix, because the original project is largely in maintenance mode. The community fork TablissNG carries the torch, and it is actively maintained, but the listing most people install is the stalled original.

**Get it:** [Tabliss — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/hipekcciheckooncpjeljhnekcoolahp)

## Where Each One Falls Short

Momentum's weakness is the paywall creep. The free version is beautiful and calm, but the features that make it a productivity tool — the to-do list, Tab Stash, deeper customisation — sit behind the $39.95-a-year Plus plan, and reviewers have noticed more moving behind it over time. The extension is also heavy at 18 MB, and its package contacts outside analytics services, which sits awkwardly next to Bonjourr and Tabliss asking for almost nothing. If you want the daily focus prompt and nothing else, Momentum's free tier is enough. If you want the productivity, you are paying every year for a start page.

Bonjourr's weakness is that minimalism has a ceiling. Its widgets snap to preset slots instead of going wherever you like, and there are fewer of them than on the power dashboards — no Tab Stash, no deep to-do system, no focus-mode timer beyond the Pomodoro widget. If your new tab is your workspace, Bonjourr will feel thin after a week. It is also made by two independent developers, not a company, which is a strength for privacy and a risk for longevity: a two-person project can stop the way a one-person project did with Tabliss.

Tabliss's weakness is maintenance. A new tab extension that has not been updated since April 2022 is a tool running on borrowed time: widgets break, background sources change, and nobody is there to fix them. The privacy story is the best in this group — no permissions, no data, open source — but privacy does not keep the Quotes widget working. If you install Tabliss, install the maintained fork, TablissNG, instead of the original listing.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The Monday morning.** I opened my browser on a Monday with a week of work ahead of me. Momentum won: its focus prompt asked me for the one thing that mattered today, and writing it down — in the tab itself — set the day. Bonjourr showed me a beautiful photo and the time, which was pleasant and useless for planning. Tabliss showed me a mountain and a clock, which was the same.

**The research rabbit hole.** I spent an afternoon across thirty tabs, opening new ones to check facts as I went. Bonjourr won, because it was the fastest to load and the least distracting — a clean page that got out of the way in under a second. Momentum's photo and quote were lovely the first five times and a small delay the next twenty-five. Tabliss was fast too, but its lack of a search bar meant every new tab was a dead end until I typed a URL.

**The 107-tab disaster.** I ended a week with over a hundred tabs open, which is my normal state by Friday. Momentum won, but only on Plus: Tab Stash vacuumed the whole mess into a saved list in one click, and my browser breathed again. On the free tier, Momentum could not help me at all. Bonjourr and Tabliss have no answer to this problem — they are start pages, not tab managers, and they do not pretend otherwise.

**The focus sprint.** I set a Pomodoro timer and worked in twenty-five-minute blocks. Bonjourr won, because its Pomodoro widget lives on the new tab itself — every time I opened a tab, the timer was there, counting down, quietly shaming me back to work. Momentum has no Pomodoro timer. Tabliss has no timer either, and its to-do widget is a list, not a sprint.

**The late-night tab.** I opened a new tab at midnight, tired, with no purpose. Bonjourr won: the photo had shifted to a night image, the page was dark and quiet, and there was nothing on it competing for my attention. Momentum showed me an inspirational quote at midnight, which felt like being coached by a stranger. Tabliss showed me a beautiful photo and nothing else, which was nearly as good — and is the whole point of Tabliss.

## What to Watch Out For: Permissions and Privacy

A new tab extension sees every new tab you open, which is every tab you open. That is a lot of surface area, and the three tools here handle it very differently. Tabliss asks for no permissions at all and stores nothing beyond error reports — the strongest privacy story of any extension I have tested in this whole series. Bonjourr asks only for storage permission, collects no data, needs no account, and an independent safety review found nothing unexplained in its code. Momentum is the outlier: it is a commercial product with analytics, and its package contacts outside analytics services.

The practical rule is simple: a start page does not need to know anything about you to show you a photo and the time. If an extension asks for broad permissions, ask what it needs them for. Tabliss and Bonjourr prove the answer can be "nothing" and "almost nothing".

One more warning, and it matters: there is a copycat listing on the Chrome Web Store called "Momentum Pro" by an unrelated developer, and it is not the official Momentum. It trades on the name, and its reviews and its developer have nothing to do with the real product. Install Momentum only from the official listing linked above, and treat any lookalike with the same name as a stranger wearing a borrowed badge.

## Two More Worth Knowing About

If you install Tabliss, install the fork, not the original. **TablissNG** is the community-maintained fork of the original project, actively updated — version 1.8.0 in September 2026 — with bug fixes and new features, including fixes for the widgets that broke on the original. It is free, open source, and the honest recommendation for anyone who wants what Tabliss promises.

And if you want your new tab to do some good, **Tab for a Cause** replaces your new tab with a page whose ad revenue funds charities, and it publishes how much it has raised. It is not a focus tool and it will not organise your tabs, but it is the only start page here whose business model is giving the money away, and for some people that is the whole point of the tab.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Momentum really free?

Momentum's free tier gives you the daily photo, mantra, quote, focus prompt, weather and search. The productivity features — to-do list, Tab Stash, deeper customisation — need the Plus plan at $39.95 a year. More of the product has moved behind Plus over time, which is the most common complaint in its reviews.

### Is Bonjourr really completely free?

Yes. Bonjourr has no paid tier at all — it is free, open source, and supported by donations. There is nothing to upgrade to, which is also why it has no to-do system or tab manager: the project stays minimal on purpose.

### What happened to Tabliss?

The original Tabliss project is largely in maintenance mode and has not been updated since version 2.6.0 in April 2022. Some widgets, including Quotes, have stopped working. The community fork TablissNG is actively maintained and is the version to install.

### Which new tab extension is best for privacy?

Tabliss, by a clear margin: it asks for no browser permissions and stores nothing beyond error reports. Bonjourr is a close second — storage permission only, no data collected, no account. Momentum is a commercial product with analytics, and the weakest privacy story of the three.

### Do new tab extensions slow down Chrome?

It depends on the size. Bonjourr is about 1.4 MB and Tabliss about 204 KB — both load instantly. Momentum is about 18 MB and noticeably slower on a weak machine, especially when it fetches a new photo. On an old laptop, the lightweight options win.

### Can I use a new tab dashboard and a tab manager together?

Yes, and you should if you open a lot of tabs. A start page decides what a new tab shows you; a tab manager decides what happens to the hundred tabs you already have. They solve different problems, and they work well side by side.

## Verdict

Install **Bonjourr** if you want the best new tab dashboard for most people. It is completely free, it has the highest rating here at 4.9 stars, it collects no data, it needs no account, it weighs 1.4 MB, and it gets out of your way — which is the entire job of a start page.

Install **Momentum** if you want a focus tool, not just a pretty tab. The daily focus prompt is a genuinely good habit, and Tab Stash on the Plus plan is the only real tab cleanup in this group. Just know you are paying $39.95 a year for it, and check that the free tier covers what you need before you pay.

Choose **Tabliss** — the maintained fork, TablissNG — if privacy is your first concern and you want a beautiful, free, open-source start page that asks for nothing. Do not install the original listing: it has not been updated since 2022, and its widgets are breaking.

If I had to keep only one, I would keep Bonjourr — the tab I forgot was an extension, which is exactly what a start page should be.
