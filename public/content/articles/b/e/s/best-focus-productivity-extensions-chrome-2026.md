## I Spent Two Weeks Trying to Stop Distracting Myself

I checked social media 34 times on Monday. I know the number because one of these extensions counted it for me, and the number made me feel slightly ill about my own attention span. Thirty-four times, for an average of 47 seconds each, which is 27 minutes of the working day spent on feeds I did not mean to open. That is the real state of most people's focus, mine included, and it is the reason I stopped trusting my willpower and started testing website blockers properly.

For two weeks I ran four of them through my actual working life: blocking social media during work hours, running Pomodoro timers, locking myself out of news sites with no way to cancel, and trying to cheat on all four to see which ones could be beaten. I also watched what each one costs now, and who owns it, because in 2026 the company behind your focus tool matters as much as the tool itself.

The four tools split into two camps, and the split matters more than any feature list. BlockSite and Forest are polished products with subscriptions and upsells. StayFocusd and LeechBlock NG are free tools built by people who wanted a blocker for themselves. Which camp you belong in decides most of this comparison before you read a single feature — and the free camp, it turns out, wins more of it than you would expect.

## The Four Contenders

**BlockSite** is the biggest focus extension in this group, with about 1,000,000 Chrome users and a 4.5-star rating across roughly 32,400 ratings, and it was updated to version 8.0.0 on November 4, 2026. It is the most complete commercial blocker here: a custom blocklist, Schedule Mode, a Focus Mode with a Pomodoro timer, password protection, uninstall prevention, redirects to a custom block page, blocking by category or keyword, usage Insights, and sync to its Android and iOS apps. It is also the most aggressive about money. The free tier blocks only about 3 sites, which is the most-cited complaint in its reviews, and the upsell pop-ups are constant. Premium costs around $33.50 a year, with a Lifetime Premium option around $49.90 — those figures come from secondary sources that checked the pricing page in October 2026, because the official page renders no prices in its HTML, so treat them as reported and re-check at checkout. The weaknesses beyond price: it requests broad permissions covering all URLs, tabs and cookies, and its privacy is rated a medium risk by independent reviewers; and recent-review sentiment is slipping, with the last-100-reviews average around 3.9 stars against the 4.5 lifetime figure, mostly over incognito and guest-window loopholes that let you slip past the block.

**Get it:** [BlockSite — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/blocksite-block-websites/eiimnmioipafcokbfikbljfdeojpcgbh)

**Forest: stay focused, be present** is the only tool here that tries to make focus feel good instead of punishing. The official extension, by Seekrtech, has about 900,000 Chrome users, and it was updated to version 6.6.1 on November 4, 2026 — its first Chrome extension update in nearly two years. The idea is simple and it works: you plant a virtual tree when you start a focus session, and it grows while you work and dies if you visit a blocked site. The guilt of killing a tree is a better motivator than a block page, and I am not embarrassed to admit it worked on me. It has Deep Focus site blocking, syncs with the iOS and Android apps, shows focus statistics and a visual forest map, and the coins you earn in sessions can plant real trees through Trees for the Future — the vendor reports more than 2 million real trees planted. The core timer and site blocking are free. The paid tier is now Forest Plus, a subscription at $5.99 a month or about $32.49 to $35.99 a year, and the old one-time Pro purchase of $1.99 or $3.99 is discontinued for new users. The weaknesses: it has the lowest rating in this group at 3.0 stars across about 1,400 ratings, with recent reviews complaining about monetisation, restricted features and the redesign; there is no desktop app, so on a computer it is only a Chrome or Firefox extension and cannot block apps outside the browser; and the blocking is voluntary and trivially bypassed — disable the extension, switch browser, tree saved.

**Get it:** [Forest — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/forest-stay-focused-be-pr/kjacjjdnoddnpbbcjilcajfhhbdhkpgk)

**StayFocusd** is the strictest tool here, and the one with the most interesting feature: the Nuclear Option. It has about 700,000 Chrome users and a 4.4-star rating across roughly 8,800 ratings, and it was last updated in October 2026 to version 4.6.15. The core idea is a daily time budget: give social media 30 minutes a day, and when the budget is spent, the sites are blocked for the rest of the day. The Nuclear Option goes further — it blocks your chosen sites for a set number of hours with no way to cancel once it starts, which is the feature I used when I had a deadline and did not trust myself. Blocking is granular: whole sites, subdomains, specific pages or paths, even in-page content such as YouTube Shorts. There is Active Days and Hours scheduling, daily usage statistics, and a newer Gen AI Analytics page that tracks how much time you spend in AI chats. It is completely free, with no paid tier and no premium features. The weaknesses are serious, though. It is Chrome and Edge only, with no mobile app and no cross-device sync. It is easy to bypass — disable the extension, use Incognito, switch browser. And in October 2026 a teardown found that StayFocusd is now published by Sensor Tower, a mobile-analytics company, not the original indie developer, and that version 4.6.15 contains tracker SDKs including Google Analytics and Bugsnag. Recent reviews increasingly report unreliable blocking and lost settings, with the last-100-reviews average around 3.4 to 3.8 stars.

**Get it:** [StayFocusd — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji)

**LeechBlock NG** is the free-forever purist's choice, and the highest-rated tool in this group. It has about 100,000 Chrome users and a 4.9-star rating across about 1,600 ratings — the highest rating here, and holding, with the last-100-reviews average at 4.88 stars. It was last updated on October 12, 2026 to version 1.8. It is free forever: no paid tier, no account, no ads, no caps, open source under the MPL-2.0 licence. It gives you up to 30 block sets, each with its own schedule, time limit and blocking mode, plus a Lockdown mode, a delay-countdown instead of a hard block, wildcards, keywords and exceptions, and a password or random access-code lock on the settings — it can even block the Extensions page itself during a block, so you cannot just turn it off. It runs on Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Brave, Opera and Vivaldi, and an independent audit found zero trackers in the package. The weaknesses: the options page is dense and old-fashioned — times entered as 0900-1700, exceptions with a plus sign, keywords with a tilde — and there is a real learning curve; there is no mobile app and no cross-device sync; it is browser-only, so it can be bypassed by disabling it or using another browser; and it is a single-developer project.

**Get it:** [LeechBlock NG — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/leechblock-ng/blaaajhemilngeeffpbfkdjjoefldkok)

## Where Each One Falls Short

BlockSite's weakness is the free tier, and it is not close. Three sites is not a free tier, it is a demo: the average person has at least five distractions, so you hit the wall on the first day. And once you pay, the upsell pop-ups do not stop — the product keeps selling itself to people who already bought it. The privacy picture is the other problem: broad permissions across every URL, tab and cookie, a medium-risk privacy rating, and a business model built on a subscription that needs your data to show you Insights.

Forest's weakness is that the motivation is the product, and the product is now paywalled. The tree is a wonderful idea, but analytics, custom allowlists and tags, and real-tree redemption are all behind the Plus subscription, and the one-time Pro purchase that long-time users loved is gone for new users. The 3.0-star rating is the visible scar of that change, and the blocking itself is the weakest here — it is voluntary, it only works in the browser, and killing a tree only matters if you care about the tree.

StayFocusd's weakness is trust, and trust is the entire product for a tool that watches everything you browse. A focus extension sees every site you visit, and StayFocusd is now owned by Sensor Tower, an analytics company, with tracker SDKs in the package. That does not mean it sends your browsing history anywhere, but it is the reason I would think twice before installing it now, and it is the reason its recent reviews are sliding. The Nuclear Option is also a double-edged sword: it is the most effective feature here, and the one most likely to lock you out of a site you actually need.

LeechBlock NG's weakness is the learning curve, and it is real. The options page looks like a spreadsheet from 2009, the syntax for exceptions and keywords is cryptic, and setting up 30 block sets properly takes an evening. It has no mobile app, no sync, and no hand-holding. But it is the only tool here with no catch at all — no subscription, no trackers, no owner to worry about, and a 4.9-star rating from people who did the work to learn it.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The social media workday.** I blocked social media from 9 to 5 for a week. BlockSite did it cleanly with Schedule Mode, and the custom block page with a motivational quote was a nice touch — until the upsell popup appeared on the block page itself. Forest's tree died twice, which stung more than any block page. StayFocusd's daily budget was the most honest approach: 30 minutes of social media, then blocked, and the budget made me choose when to spend it. LeechBlock NG's block sets handled it with the most precision — different rules for weekdays and weekends — but took the longest to set up. For a simple workday block, StayFocusd's budget won.

**The deadline lockdown.** I had a report due at 5pm and did not trust myself. StayFocusd's Nuclear Option was the only tool that actually locked me out with no way back — I set it for four hours, and there was genuinely nothing I could do except work. LeechBlock NG's Lockdown came close, and its trick of blocking the Extensions page during a block meant I could not cheat by disabling it. BlockSite's password protection helped, but I knew the password. Forest's tree died and I felt bad, then opened the site anyway. If you need to be locked out of your own habits, StayFocusd and LeechBlock NG are the only two that mean it.

**The Pomodoro afternoon.** I ran four 25-minute focus sessions with 5-minute breaks. Forest was the best experience here by a mile — watching the tree grow is a better timer than a countdown, and finishing four sessions left me with a little forest to look at. BlockSite's Focus Mode worked fine and the timer was clear. StayFocusd has a focus timer but it felt like an afterthought next to the blocking. LeechBlock NG has no Pomodoro timer at all, which is its one real feature gap. For Pomodoro, Forest won.

**The cheating test.** I tried to beat all four: disable the extension, use Incognito, switch browsers, edit the settings mid-block. Every one of them can be beaten by switching browsers — no browser extension can block another browser, and no mobile app is blocked by any of them except BlockSite and Forest through their companion apps. LeechBlock NG was the hardest to beat inside Chrome, because it can block the Extensions page during a block and lock its settings behind a random access code. BlockSite's uninstall prevention helped, but Incognito was still open. The honest conclusion: a browser blocker only works if you want it to work. The tools that admit that — LeechBlock NG and StayFocusd — are the honest ones.

**The phone problem.** I checked my phone 34 times on Monday, and none of these extensions can see a phone. BlockSite and Forest have companion mobile apps that sync your blocklists, which is the only real answer to the phone problem in this group. StayFocusd and LeechBlock NG are desktop-only, so your phone stays unguarded. I ended up using my phone's built-in screen-time limits alongside the extension, which is the practical answer: the extension guards the laptop, the phone's own tools guard the phone, and no single tool covers both unless you pay for BlockSite or Forest's apps.

## What to Watch Out For: Privacy, Ownership and the Manifest V3 Clean-Out

A focus extension sees every site you visit, which means it can see a lot: your banking, your health searches, your late-night browsing. LeechBlock NG is the only tool here with an independent audit finding zero trackers, and it is open source, so anyone can verify that. StayFocusd now contains Google Analytics and Bugsnag tracker SDKs, and it is owned by Sensor Tower, a mobile-analytics company — that is a fact worth knowing before you install a tool that watches your browsing. BlockSite requests the broadest permissions of the four and is rated a medium privacy risk. Forest's privacy is not independently audited. Install only the official extension from the Chrome Web Store, and read the privacy disclosure on the listing before you install.

The other thing to watch is the state of the category after the Manifest V3 clean-out. Google removed the last Manifest V2 extensions from the store on August 31, 2026. The full uBlock Origin is gone from Chrome — only the reduced uBlock Origin Lite remains — which is relevant because many people pair a focus blocker with an ad blocker. All four tools here are Manifest V3 compliant and still installable. The practical impact on focus blockers is small, because they work through declared rules and redirects rather than live traffic inspection, but dynamic per-request filtering is no longer possible on Chrome.

One more warning that applies to all four: there are copycat extensions with similar names. "Forest Focus Timer" and "Focus Forest" are not the official Forest app — the official listing is the Seekrtech one. Check the publisher before you install anything with a familiar name.

## Two More Worth Knowing About

If the four above do not fit, the two alternatives I would look at first are Freedom and Cold Turkey. Freedom is the cross-device pick: its Chrome extension has only about 50,000 users and a 3.3-star rating, because on Mac and Windows it is just a companion to the desktop app, but the service claims more than 3 million users overall and it is the only tool here that blocks apps, websites and the whole internet across Mac, Windows, iOS, Android and Chrome from one session, with a Locked Mode. Pricing is confirmed on its own page: a free tier for sessions up to 2 hours, Premium at $3.33 a month billed yearly or $8.99 monthly, and a Forever plan at $99.50. Cold Turkey is the hardest-to-bypass pick: a desktop app for Windows and macOS with a Chrome extension companion, about 200,000 Chrome users, and a Pro plan around $39 as a one-time purchase. Its blocks are notoriously difficult to circumvent once active, unlike any pure extension.

## Key Takeaways

- LeechBlock NG is the best focus extension for purists: free forever, open source, zero trackers, a 4.9-star rating, and the hardest to cheat — but the setup takes an evening and there is no mobile app.
- StayFocusd has the strictest blocking, including the Nuclear Option with no way to cancel, and it is completely free — but it is now owned by Sensor Tower and contains tracker SDKs, which is a real privacy concern for a tool that watches your browsing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which focus extension is completely free?

StayFocusd and LeechBlock NG. StayFocusd has no paid tier at all. LeechBlock NG is free forever with no account, no ads and no caps, and it is open source. Forest's core timer is free but the good features need Plus, and BlockSite's free tier covers only about 3 sites.

### Can I really not cancel the Nuclear Option in StayFocusd?

Correct. Once the Nuclear Option starts, there is no way to cancel it until the timer ends. That is the point — it is for deadlines you do not trust yourself to keep. Do not start it unless you are sure you will not need the blocked sites, because there is no override.

### Why is Forest rated only 3.0 stars?

Recent reviews complain about monetisation, restricted features and the redesign, and about the one-time Pro purchase being replaced by the Plus subscription. The tree mechanic itself is still loved — the complaints are about what got paywalled around it, not about the timer.

### Is StayFocusd safe to install?

It is functional and it is free, but you should know that it is now published by Sensor Tower, a mobile-analytics company, and that a teardown of version 4.6.15 found tracker SDKs including Google Analytics and Bugsnag in the package. A focus extension sees every site you visit. If that bothers you, LeechBlock NG is the tracker-free alternative.

### Do these extensions block apps on my computer?

No, with one exception. Browser extensions can only block websites inside the browser. Forest and BlockSite have companion mobile apps, and Freedom — the alternative I mention above — blocks apps and websites across devices from one session. If you need to block desktop apps, you need a desktop app like Cold Turkey, not a browser extension.

### Which focus extension works on mobile?

BlockSite and Forest have Android and iOS apps that sync with the extension. StayFocusd and LeechBlock NG are desktop-only. For the phone, your best option is usually the phone's own screen-time or digital-wellbeing tools, used alongside the extension.

## Verdict

Install **LeechBlock NG** if you want the best focus extension and you are willing to spend an evening learning it. Free forever, open source, zero trackers, a 4.9-star rating, and the hardest to cheat in this group. Accept the old-fashioned settings page and the lack of a mobile app.

Install **StayFocusd** if you want the strictest blocking and you do not mind the privacy trade-off. The Nuclear Option is the most effective feature here, and it is completely free. Go in knowing who owns it now and what is in the package.

Install **Forest** if you want the most enjoyable Pomodoro timer and you care about the real trees. The tree mechanic genuinely kept me focused in a way a countdown never has. Accept the Plus subscription and the 3.0-star rating.

Install **BlockSite** if you want mobile sync and the most polished commercial blocker, and you are willing to pay. Skip the free tier — three sites is a demo, not a plan.

If I had to keep only one, I would keep LeechBlock NG — the tool with no subscription, no trackers and no catch, and the one whose block I could not talk my way out of.
