## I Spent Three Weeks With an AI Living in My Browser Sidebar

I used to keep a ChatGPT tab open all day. It was always the tab I lost: buried under fourteen others, reloaded at the worst moment, and every question meant switching context away from the page I was actually reading. Then I installed a sidebar assistant, and the question moved from "should I open the tab" to "should I even leave this page" — because the AI was already there, one click from the toolbar, reading the same page I was reading.

For three weeks I ran three of these sidebar assistants as my daily driver, each for several days at a time, on the same real work: summarising long articles before I decided whether to read them, drafting replies to emails, translating pages, explaining dense documentation, and writing social posts from a link. I tracked where the free plan ran out, which one answered fastest, and which one surprised me with a limit I only found in the fine print.

The three tools look similar from the outside — a sidebar, a chat box, a button to summarise the page — but they are built on very different ideas. Monica is an all-in-one assistant that wants to replace half your extensions. Sider is a chat-first tool that puts the best models side by side. Merlin is the researcher's pick, with dozens of models and a browser automation streak that the others do not have.

## The Three Contenders

**Monica** is the biggest sidebar assistant in this group by users — about 3 million people run it, with a 4.8 to 4.9-star rating across roughly 32,300 ratings. It is a genuine all-in-one: chat with GPT-4o, Claude and Gemini from the sidebar, summarise any page or YouTube video, translate, write, search with an AI agent, and generate images, all without leaving the tab you are on. The free plan gives you 40 basic queries a day, which sounds generous until you realise the advanced models cost more per query — a heavy day of GPT-4o questions burns through the free allowance faster than the number suggests. The Pro plan costs $99 a year, about $8.25 a month. In my testing Monica was the fastest to answer and the best at turning a long page into a short summary you could actually use.

**Get it:** [Monica — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/monica-all-in-one-ai-assi/ofpnmcalabcbjgholdjcjblkibolbppb)

**Sider** is the largest tool in this entire comparison — about 5 million users, with a 4.9-star rating across roughly 114,300 ratings, the highest rating count of any extension in this article. Its idea is simple and it executes it well: every major AI model in one sidebar, side by side, with group chat so you can ask GPT-4o, Claude and Gemini the same question and compare the answers. It also wires AI into your searches, so a Google search comes back with an AI summary next to the results. The free plan gives you 30 basic credits a day, and the Nano plan costs $10 a month, or $8.33 a month on annual billing. What I liked most in testing was the comparison view: when the models disagreed on a technical question, seeing all three answers at once told me more than any single answer would have.

**Get it:** [Sider — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/sider-chatgpt%E2%80%A6/difoiogjjojoaoomphldepapgpbgkhkb)

**Merlin** is the smallest of the three by users — about 900,000, with a 4.4-star rating across roughly 8,800 ratings — and the most ambitious. It gives you access to dozens of AI models from one sidebar, including the ones the other two do not carry, and it goes beyond chat: Merlin can summarise pages, write, translate, and run multi-step research tasks in the browser. The free plan is the most generous on paper, at roughly 51 to 102 queries a day, which is why it is popular with students and researchers. The Pro plan costs $19 a month on annual billing, or $228 a year — the most expensive paid plan in this group by a wide margin. And here is the catch I only found by reading the fine print and then hitting it: there is a hidden fair-use cap of roughly $100 of model usage a month, even on the paid plan. Heavy users hit a wall that the pricing page does not advertise.

**Get it:** [Merlin — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/merlin-ai/camppjleccjaphfdbohjdohecfnoikec)

## Where Each One Falls Short

Monica's weakness is that the free plan is thinner than the headline number. Forty basic queries a day sounds like a lot, but the good models — GPT-4o, Claude — are not basic queries, and they consume your allowance several times faster. I ran out of useful queries by mid-afternoon on a heavy research day, while the counter still showed basic queries remaining. The all-in-one ambition is also a double-edged sword: Monica does so many things that its sidebar can feel crowded, and its search agent and image tools are good but not best-in-class at any one job.

Sider's weakness is that it is a chat tool first and everything else second. Its page summariser works, but it is not as good as a dedicated summariser, and its writing tools are thinner than Monica's. The 30 free credits a day are also the smallest free allowance in this group, and the credit system is confusing: different models cost different numbers of credits, and the extension does not always make that clear before you ask. I burned a day's credits on two long GPT-4o conversations before lunch, which is a fast way to learn the pricing page by heart.

Merlin's weakness is the price and the hidden cap. Nineteen dollars a month is nearly two and a half times Monica's Pro plan, and the roughly $100-a-month fair-use cap means the "unlimited" feeling of the paid plan stops being unlimited exactly when you use it hardest. The 4.4-star rating is also the lowest here, and the reviews that drag it down are consistent: users who hit the cap, users confused by the credit system, and users who found the interface busier than the other two. Merlin is the most powerful tool in this group on paper, and the one most likely to frustrate you in practice.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The "should I read this?" test.** I opened a 6,000-word article and asked each tool to summarise it before I decided whether to read it. Monica won: its summary was the shortest and the most honest about what the article actually said, including a line flagging that the article's central claim was weaker than its headline. Sider's summary was accurate but twice as long. Merlin's summary was good, but it took noticeably longer to generate, and it added a "key takeaways" section that restated the same points.

**The email reply sprint.** I had eleven emails to answer in an hour, and I drafted each reply with the sidebar open on Gmail. Sider won this one, because its group-chat view let me compare a short GPT-4o draft against a warmer Claude draft for the two emails that mattered, and pick the better one. Monica drafted good replies faster, but I had to switch models manually to compare. Merlin drafted well too, but its replies were consistently longer than I wanted, and I spent the saved time editing them down.

**The dense documentation.** I fed each tool a piece of API documentation I did not understand and asked it to explain it like I was a junior developer. Merlin won: with access to dozens of models, it gave the clearest explanation, with a worked example that actually ran. Monica was close behind. Sider's answers were correct but terse, and it took a follow-up question to get the example.

**The foreign-language page.** I opened a page in a language I do not read and asked each tool to translate and summarise it. All three handled it, but Monica's translation was the most natural, and it kept the page's structure — headings, lists — in the summary. Sider translated accurately but flattened the structure. Merlin translated well and added useful context about the topic, which I had not asked for but appreciated.

**The research rabbit hole.** I spent an afternoon researching a topic across ten tabs, asking follow-up questions as I went. This is where Merlin's hidden cap bit me: by late afternoon the paid plan stopped answering advanced-model questions, and I only found out why when I read the fair-use note in the settings. Monica handled the whole afternoon on the Pro plan without a hitch. Sider handled it too, but I burned through the daily credits faster than I expected and ended the day on the basic model, which was noticeably weaker on the last two questions.

## What to Watch Out For: Permissions and Privacy

A sidebar assistant is not a website. It reads the page you are on — that is the whole point — which means it can see whatever you can see: your emails, your bank balance, your medical results, your private documents. Every tool here asks for broad permissions, and you should assume that anything you summarise is sent to the AI company's servers. That is how the feature works, and it is not a secret, but it is a decision you should make consciously.

Do not summarise a page you would not paste into a chatbot. That is the rule I follow, because functionally that is what you are doing. A payslip, a medical record, a confidential contract: read those yourself. The convenience of a one-click summary is not worth the data leaving your browser.

The pricing trap to watch is the credit system. All three tools sell you a plan and then measure your usage in a second currency — basic queries, credits, fair-use dollars — and the conversion between the two is never as generous as the headline suggests. Before you pay, use the free plan for a full working week, including your heaviest day, and see where it actually runs out. That one week tells you more than the pricing page does.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Monica really free?

Monica's free plan gives you 40 basic queries a day, which covers light use. The advanced models — GPT-4o, Claude — cost more per query, so a heavy day burns through the free allowance faster than the headline number suggests. The Pro plan costs $99 a year.

### Which AI sidebar assistant has the most models?

Merlin, with access to dozens of AI models from one sidebar. Monica and Sider both offer the major models — GPT-4o, Claude, Gemini — but Merlin's catalogue is wider, which is why researchers like it.

### Can Sider really compare AI models side by side?

Yes. Sider's group-chat feature lets you ask GPT-4o, Claude and Gemini the same question and see all three answers together. It is the fastest way I found to check whether an answer is solid or just confident.

### What is Merlin's hidden fair-use cap?

Merlin's paid Pro plan, at $19 a month on annual billing, carries a fair-use cap of roughly $100 of model usage a month. Heavy users hit a wall that the pricing page does not advertise, and the extension stops answering advanced-model questions until the allowance resets.

### Do sidebar assistants read everything I browse?

They read the page you ask them about, and they send that content to the AI company's servers to generate the answer. They do not read pages you never invoke them on, but the permission they hold is broad. Do not summarise pages containing sensitive personal or confidential information.

### Which sidebar assistant is best for students?

Merlin's free plan is the most generous, at roughly 51 to 102 queries a day, which is why students like it. Monica is the better all-rounder if you also want writing, translation and video summaries, and its Pro plan is the cheapest here.

## Verdict

Install **Monica** if you want the best all-round sidebar assistant. It was the fastest in my testing, it wrote the best summaries, and at $99 a year its Pro plan is the cheapest here. The free plan's 40 basic queries a day cover light use honestly.

Install **Sider** if you want to compare models before you trust an answer. Five million users and a 4.9-star rating are not an accident, and the group-chat view is genuinely useful for anything you need to get right. Just know that 30 free credits a day go quickly.

Choose **Merlin** if you need the widest choice of models and you mostly work on the free plan, where it is the most generous of the three. Think twice before paying $19 a month for it — the hidden fair-use cap means the paid plan stops being unlimited exactly when you use it hardest.

If I had to keep only one, I would keep Monica — the one I stopped noticing, because it was just there, answering, every time I needed it.
