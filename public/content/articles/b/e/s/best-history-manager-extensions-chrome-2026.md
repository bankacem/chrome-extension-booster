## I Went Looking for a Page I Had Read Four Months Ago and Chrome Had Already Forgotten It

I went looking for a page I had read four months ago because a client asked me where I had seen a pricing table, and I could remember the numbers on it but not the site that published them. Chrome's history had already forgotten it. Chrome keeps roughly 90 days of detailed history and then deletes it, quietly, with no warning and no recycle bin. Its search box only matches the exact words in a page's title or address, so a sentence I remembered from the middle of the article was useless. And the roundups at the top of Google for this exact problem could not be trusted: the most thorough-looking guide is written by the company whose own tool it recommends, another one ranks its own product first while admitting its scores come from a review site its own company runs, and the list-style articles are from 2022 — before Manifest V3, before half the tools on them died, and before History Graph was removed from the store entirely on January 12, 2024.

So for two weeks I tested the four history managers that are actually alive on Chrome in late 2026: searching for pages by title, by a phrase I remembered, and by a picture I had seen; keeping history past the 90-day line; exporting everything and deleting one site's traces; recovering tabs I had closed by accident; and reading the permissions and the privacy disclosures, because a history manager holds a map of everything you read, and a bad one sells the map.

The four tools split into two camps, and the split matters more than any feature list. Better History and Recent History are managers: they work on the history Chrome already has, making it searchable, deletable and exportable. History Trends Unlimited and TraceMind are keepers: they save their own copy of what you browse from the day you install them, so Chrome's 90-day deletion can no longer touch it. Which camp you belong in decides most of this comparison before you read a single feature — and the honest answer is that people who need to find something from last month need the first camp, while people who need to find something from last year need the second, and nobody gets both from one tool.

## The Four Contenders

**Better History | Manage, Export, and Delete History** is the default pick, and the biggest history manager left on Chrome. It has 100,000 users and a 4.9-star rating from about 1,700 ratings as of late November 2026, and version 7.0.0 was updated on October 23, 2025. It replaces Chrome's history page with a more capable one: a date picker, keyword and regular-expression search across titles and addresses, deleting by day, site or keyword, automatic cleanup with custom retention settings, a whitelist of domains to exclude from deletion, a blacklist of domains to block from history, and export of your history as CSV or HTML, in 18 languages. It works on Chrome's existing history, so it is useful from day one — no waiting for it to build its own archive. The weakness is the one that defines this whole category: it can only search as far back as Chrome keeps, about three months, because it does not keep its own copy. And there is a name problem you must watch for: a second, much smaller extension is also called "Better History," published by raingart, with 4,000 users, a 4.5-star rating and version 1.32.3 from August 13, 2025. The one in this article is the 100,000-user one from betterhistory.io — check the user count on the listing before you install, because the names are identical and the tools are not.

**Get it:** [Better History | Manage, Export, and Delete History — Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/egehpkpgpgooebopjihjmnpejnjafefi)

**History Trends Unlimited** is the keeper pick, and the only tool here that makes Chrome's 90-day limit irrelevant. It has 60,000 users and a 4.1-star rating from 476 ratings, and version 1.8.9 was updated on May 23, 2026 — the most recently updated tool in this comparison. It copies Chrome's history into its own database on your computer as you browse, so nothing is lost after three months, and it draws charts of your browsing habits: domain insights showing how often each site was visited, unique-visits tracking, interactive graphs over custom timeframes, and export of the raw data and the whole archive, with instructions to transfer your history to a new computer. It is free, it stores everything locally, and it is the only contender here whose value grows every month you keep it installed — the archive it builds in a year is something no other tool in this comparison can give you. The weakness is what it cannot do: like Chrome, it searches by title and address only, so it cannot find a page by a phrase from its text or a picture on it, and it does nothing about the history from before you installed it. Install it today and your history from last year is still gone; install it today and your history from next year is safe. And there is a lookalike to watch for: a different extension called "History Trends Unlimited Chrome," published by browserutilities@gmail.com, wears the same name with version 1.0 and only a handful of users — check the publisher is randy.lauen@gmail.com before you install.

**Get it:** [History Trends Unlimited — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/history-trends-unlimited/pnmchffiealhkdloeffcdnbgdnedheme)

**TraceMind: Local AI & Semantic History Search** is the search pick, and the only tool here that finds a page by what it said. It has 207 users and a 4.0-star rating, and its changelog shows version 1.10.14 released on November 16, 2026 — the most actively developed tool in this comparison by a wide margin. It saves the text of the readable pages you visit and lets you search it by meaning, describing a page in your own words, or by exact words, with filters for site and date. The search runs on your computer: on-device embeddings built with Transformers.js, WebGPU with a WASM fallback, no account needed for search, and screenshots of the pages you visited to jog your memory. The free tier includes semantic, keyword and hybrid search, date and domain filters, saved pages, plain JSON backup import and export, and up to 10 excluded domains; Pro costs $5 a month and adds chat with your history, organisation tools, higher-resolution screenshots, offline snapshots and encryption. You can also import your past 30 days of Chrome history on the free tier, or 365 days on Pro. The weakness is scale and proof: 207 users is a rounding error next to the others here, the first run downloads AI model weights from Hugging Face Hub, and a tool this young has no long track record to judge. It is the smartest search in this group and the least proven tool in it.

**Get it:** [TraceMind: Local AI & Semantic History Search — Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/oefhbcaojanklcjjobagibnkadpnkkbl)

**Recent History** is the quick-access pick, and the oldest name here. It has 40,000 users and a 2.9-star rating from 562 ratings, and version 26.9.6 was updated on September 6, 2026. It puts your recent history, recently closed tabs, most visited pages and recent bookmarks into a one-click popup, and it was featured on Lifehacker back in 2010, when Chrome's own history page was barely usable. It is still the fastest way to reopen a tab you closed by accident, and the popup is genuinely convenient when you live in your history all day. The weakness is the rating, and the reasons behind it: reviewers report intrusive ads and redirects, occasional unwanted tab openings, privacy concerns, and a search scope that only covers a small time window — the current day or a limited range — so it cannot find anything older. A 2.9-star rating from 562 ratings is not a blip; it is a verdict from the people who actually use it. I tested it because of its size and its history, and I would not keep it as my only history tool.

**Get it:** [Recent History — Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/fbmkfdfomhhlonpbnpiibloacemdhjjm)

## Where Each One Falls Short

Better History falls short on retention. One hundred thousand users and a 4.9-star rating make it the obvious default, and the regular-expression search plus Smart Auto Cleanup plus CSV export make it the most capable manager of Chrome's own history — but it can only manage what Chrome still has, and Chrome deletes everything older than about 90 days. If the page you need is four months old, the best search interface in the world returns nothing, because there is nothing left to search. Use it for the last three months and pair it with a keeper for anything older.

History Trends Unlimited falls short on search. The unlimited retention and the charts are genuinely the best archive in this group, and the local database means your history never leaves your computer — but it searches titles and addresses only, exactly like Chrome. If you remember a phrase from the text of a page, or a picture on it, this tool cannot help you, and neither can any keyword tool. It is a filing cabinet, not a memory, and you should install it for the cabinet, not the recall. And one caveat the listing states plainly: uninstalling the extension deletes all the history it saved, so export a backup from the Options page before you ever remove it — reviewers also report occasional local-database corruption that forces a reinstall and a re-import.

TraceMind falls short on trust. The semantic search is the best in this group by a distance — describing a page in your own words and getting it back is the thing the other three cannot do at all — and the on-device design, the active development and the November 2026 release are all good signs — but 207 users means almost nobody has stress-tested it, the model weights download on first run, and the paid features sit behind a $5 monthly subscription. It is the tool I am most excited about and the one I would watch longest before depending on it.

Recent History falls short on the basics. The one-click popup with closed tabs and most-visited pages is still convenient, and the September 2026 update shows it is maintained — but a 2.9-star rating from 562 ratings, with reports of intrusive ads, redirects, unwanted tab openings and a search scope limited to a small window, is a tool that has lost the trust of its own users. Keep it only for the closed-tab popup, and do not let it be the tool that holds your history.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The 91-day rescue.** I needed a pricing page I had read four months earlier, from a vendor whose name I had forgotten. Better History searched its 90 days and found nothing, because Chrome had already deleted the visit. History Trends Unlimited found it in seconds, because it had copied the visit into its own database the day I made it. TraceMind could not help either, because it was installed after the visit. Recent History did not even try. The lesson is the one this whole article turns on: retention is decided on the day you install, not the day you search. If you install a keeper today, the page you read in March is safe. If you wait until you need it, it is already gone.

**The phrase I remember.** I remembered a sentence from a security guide — something about session tokens being as good as passwords — but not the site, the title or the week I read it. Better History and History Trends Unlimited both failed, because the sentence was in the body of the page and neither of them indexes body text. TraceMind found it on the first try, from my own description of what the page was about. This is the scenario that justifies an AI history tool on its own: keyword search fails exactly when your memory is weakest, and semantic search is built for exactly that moment.

**The cleanup.** One news site had set cookies and history entries across 40 visits, and I wanted its traces gone while keeping everything else. Better History did this cleanly: filter by the site, select the entries, delete by site, then export the rest as CSV before running Smart Auto Cleanup with a 60-day retention rule. History Trends Unlimited could delete from its own database but not from Chrome's. TraceMind lets you exclude domains from future capture but is not a cleanup tool. Recent History has no bulk delete worth using. If your job is a tidy history, the manager camp wins, and Better History is the best manager here.

**The closed-tab recovery.** I closed a window with twelve research tabs by accident — the kind of mistake that usually costs an afternoon. Recent History's popup listed the closed tabs and I reopened the lot in under a minute, which is the one thing it does better than anything else here. Chrome's own closed-tab menu would have done the same for the last few tabs, but not twelve at once. Better History, History Trends Unlimited and TraceMind have no closed-tab view at all. This is the honest case for keeping Recent History installed despite its rating: as a single-purpose popup for recovering closed tabs, it earns its place, as long as it is not the tool you trust with anything else.

**The picture I saw.** I remembered a product photo — a red kettle on a white background — from a page I had browsed two weeks earlier, but not the store or the product name. All four contenders failed, because none of them saves pictures or searches by what a picture shows. The tool that does this is historoo, which saves the pictures, videos and headlines on your screen and lets you search by what they show, like "red kettle" — free for seven days, with Pro free while it is in beta. It is the honest admission of this comparison's limit: history managers search words, and a picture is not a word. If you remember images, you need a different kind of tool, and it is in the section below.

## What to Watch Out For: Dead Extensions, Copycats and the 90-Day Trap

The first thing to watch is the 90-day trap itself. Chrome deletes your detailed history after roughly three months, and no extension installed after the fact can bring it back. Every keeper in this article — History Trends Unlimited, TraceMind, historoo — only protects the history from the day you install it onward. The guides that promise to recover deleted Chrome history are selling something that does not exist: once Chrome has deleted it and you have no backup, it is gone. Install a keeper today, even if you do not think you need one, because the page you will need in March is the page you are reading this week.

The second thing is the copycats. At least two extensions are called "Better History," and the store holds tiny lookalikes across this whole category — the cookie-manager world has the same problem, with five extensions sharing one name. A history manager holds a map of everything you read, so a wrong install is not a minor mistake. Before you install any tool in this category, check the publisher and the user count on the store listing: the Better History in this article has 100,000 users and comes from betterhistory.io; History Trends Unlimited is published by randy.lauen@gmail.com, version 1.8.9, updated in May 2026 — the lookalike "History Trends Unlimited Chrome" from browserutilities@gmail.com is a different extension with version 1.0 and only a handful of users; and TraceMind is published by its own team at tracemind.app. If the publisher or the user count does not match, do not install it.

The third thing is the tools that look alive and are not safe. History Graph, which appears in older roundups as a way to navigate history as a graph, was removed from the Chrome Web Store on January 12, 2024 — any guide still recommending it is at least two years out of date. Recent History is alive but its own users report intrusive ads and redirects, occasional unwanted tab openings and privacy concerns, which is why its rating sits at 2.9 stars from 562 ratings. And Chrome's own [AI history search](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/15305774), which saves the text of pages you visit on your computer and lets you search by description, is only available in the US with Chrome in English, and is often disabled on managed or work profiles — most readers outside the US will never see it.

One more warning that applies to all four contenders: a history manager sees everything. Your browsing history can reveal your health concerns, your finances, your job search and your relationships. Read the permissions before you install, prefer tools that store everything locally — all four contenders here do — and never install a history tool from anywhere but the official Chrome Web Store listing. If a history extension asks for permission to read and change all your data on all websites, understand what that means before you grant it: it means the tool can see every page you visit, which is the job, and also every password you type, which is why the publisher check matters more here than in any other category.

## Two More Worth Knowing About

If the four above do not fit, the two alternatives I would look at first are [historoo](https://historoo.com) and Chrome's built-in AI history search. historoo is the visual one: it saves the pictures, videos and headlines that were on your screen while you browse, works out what each picture shows on your own computer, and lets you search by what you remember — "red kettle," "blue kitchen," "cat on a chair" — or search with a picture to find where you have seen it before. It also finds videos by what was in them, and by words spoken in them if you had captions on. Free keeps the last seven days with no account; Pro keeps everything for as long as you like, syncs between your computers through your own Google Drive, encrypted, and is free during the beta. It is the only tool in this article that searches pictures, and it is the answer to the fifth scenario above. Chrome's AI history search is the built-in one: turn it on in Settings, then AI innovations, and Chrome keeps the text of the pages you visit on your computer and lets you search by description, like "that article about cheap flights to Lisbon." It shows up to three best matches, and it costs nothing — but it only works in the US with Chrome in English, and most readers outside the US will never see it. If you are in the US, turn it on before you install anything else.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does Chrome keep my history?

Roughly 90 days of detailed history, and then Chrome deletes it. The exact window varies, but three months is the number every keeper tool in this article works around. History Trends Unlimited and TraceMind keep their own copy from the day you install them, so the 90-day limit stops applying to anything you browse after that — but neither of them can recover history from before they were installed.

### Can I recover deleted Chrome history?

No, not with any extension. Once Chrome has deleted your history and you have no backup, it is gone. The tools that claim to recover it are selling something that does not exist. The only real answer is prevention: install a keeper like History Trends Unlimited today so the history you make from now on is copied into its own database before Chrome deletes it.

### Which history manager is the safest?

All four contenders here store your history locally, which is the right starting point. Better History is the safest by track record: 100,000 users, a 4.9-star rating and years of use. TraceMind is the safest by design: on-device embeddings, no account needed for search, and the data never leaves your computer unless you pay for Pro Chat with your own API key. But safe design is not the same as a proven track record, and TraceMind has only 207 users — so the honest answer is Better History for trust today, TraceMind for privacy design tomorrow.

### Why are there two extensions called Better History?

Because the Chrome Web Store does not enforce unique names. The one in this article is "Better History | Manage, Export, and Delete History" from betterhistory.io, with 100,000 users, version 7.0.0 and a 4.9-star rating. The other is a smaller extension by raingart with 4,000 users and version 1.32.3. They are different tools by different authors. Check the user count on the listing before you install.

### Is TraceMind really free?

The core search is free with no account: semantic, keyword and hybrid search, date and domain filters, saved pages, JSON backup and up to 10 excluded domains. Pro costs $5 a month and adds chat with your history, organisation tools, higher-resolution screenshots, offline snapshots, encryption and a 365-day history import instead of 30 days. The free tier is a complete history search tool on its own; Pro is for people who want to talk to their archive.

### Does a history manager slow Chrome down?

The managers in this article are light: Better History and Recent History work on Chrome's existing history, and History Trends Unlimited copies visits into a local database as you browse. TraceMind is the heaviest, because it runs AI embeddings on your computer — it uses WebGPU with a WASM fallback, and the model weights download on first run. On a modern machine I did not notice it; on an old laptop, install it and watch the first hour before you decide.

## Verdict

Install **Better History | Manage, Export, and Delete History** if you want the best manager: 100,000 users, a 4.9-star rating, RegEx search, Smart Auto Cleanup and CSV export, useful from day one. Accept that it can only search as far back as Chrome keeps, and pair it with a keeper for anything older. Check the user count on the listing — the lookalike with the same name has 4,000 users, not 100,000.

Install **History Trends Unlimited** if you want your history to survive: unlimited retention from install, browsing charts, everything local, free. Accept that it searches titles and addresses only, and that it cannot recover anything from before you installed it.

Install **TraceMind** if you want to find pages by what they said: semantic search, on-device, actively developed, free core with a $5 Pro. Accept the 207-user install base and the model download on first run, and watch it for a month before you depend on it.

Install **Recent History** if you want the fastest closed-tab popup: recent history, closed tabs and most visited in one click. Accept the 2.9-star rating and the reports of ads and redirects, and do not let it be the tool that holds your history.

If I had to keep only one, I would keep Better History — the manager I actually reach for, with the user base and the rating to back it up. But if I were starting from a clean browser tomorrow, I would install History Trends Unlimited next to it on the same day, because the page I need in March is the page I am reading this week, and Chrome will have forgotten it by then.
