## The Invisible Threat: How to Spot a Fake AI Assistant

This is the section that makes you an expert. Security researchers have repeatedly documented campaigns where hackers clone popular ChatGPT and Gemini extensions and publish convincing lookalikes. The trick is simple: the clone requests permission to "read and change all your data on mail.google.com," then injects an invisible frame that quietly copies your drafts, received mail, and session cookies to an attacker's server in real time.

**The golden rule of AI extension safety:**

- **Safe:** extensions that request only `activeTab` or narrowly scoped APIs, with a verified publisher badge and a plausible reason for every permission.
- **Dangerous:** extensions requesting broad host permissions like `*.google.com` without verification, or "free" tools that proxy everything through an unknown backend.

If you want to verify what a tool is allowed to do, the <a href="https://support.google.com/chrome_webstore/answer/2664769?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Web Store Help page on installing and managing extensions</a> explains how to read permission warnings and remove a suspicious add-on completely. ## How to Choose the Best AI Email Assistant Chrome Extensions (The 5-Second Test)

Do not read feature lists. Ask these three questions instead:

1. **What model does it use?** If the listing says "GPT-3.5" or just "AI," skip it. For professional replies you want GPT-4o or GPT-4.1 class models that follow tone instructions reliably.
2. **Can I edit the reply before sending?** A "human in the loop" editor is non-negotiable — you do not want an assistant hallucinating a discount into a client email while you watch.
3. **Is the API key mine?** If the extension forces you through its own free backend, you are the product. Tools that let you bring your own OpenAI or Gemini key keep your data on a path you control.

Answer all three correctly and you have found one of the best AI email assistant Chrome extensions for your specific workflow — not just the one with the best landing page.

## Frequently Asked Questions

![FAQ illustration for picking the best AI email assistant Chrome extensions](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1655720828018-edd2daec9349?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

### Are AI email assistant Chrome extensions safe to use?

Reputable ones are, but the category attracts clones. Check the publisher badge, read every permission request, prefer tools that use `activeTab` instead of blanket host permissions, and remove anything that starts behaving oddly after an update.

### Do the best AI email assistant Chrome extensions work with Outlook as well as Gmail?

Most do. NovaTexter and GPT Workspace both operate inside any web text box, which covers Gmail, Outlook on the web, and most CRM inboxes. Always test in your actual client before rolling it out to a team.

### Will an AI assistant send emails without my approval?

Not if you pick correctly. Every recommendation above keeps a human in the loop: the AI drafts, you review and press send. Treat any tool that auto-sends as an immediate uninstall.

### Can I use these AI email assistants for free?

Yes, with limits. Several offer free tiers or accept your own API key, which shifts usage costs to your OpenAI or Google account instead of a subscription. Free tiers are fine for personal volume; heavy users usually end up paying for tokens directly.

### How do I remove an AI email extension that no longer feels safe?

Open chrome://extensions, click Remove on the extension, then review the permissions list of every remaining add-on. If you suspect credential exposure, change your email password and sign out of all sessions as a precaution.
