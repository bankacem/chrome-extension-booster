## I Spent Three Weeks Watching My Own Emails Get Opened

I sent the same kind of emails I always send. A proposal to a client who had gone quiet. A follow-up to a recruiter. An invoice to a customer who swore they never got the first one. And for three weeks, I sent each of them four times — once through each tracker — because I wanted to know which tool tells the truth about what happens after you hit Send.

Email tracking is a strange category. The whole product is a tiny invisible image hidden in your message. When the recipient opens the email, the image loads, and the tracker records the open. That is the entire trick, and every tool in this comparison is a variation on it. The differences are what they do around that trick: how much they charge, how much of your inbox they touch, whether they slow Gmail down, and whether the recipient can tell you are watching.

I tested four tools that dominate the Chrome Web Store for Gmail tracking: Mailtrack, Streak, Mixmax and Yesware. I used each one as my daily tracker for several days, sent tracked emails to test accounts I control on Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail, and checked which opens were recorded, which were missed, and which were false alarms. I also watched what each extension did to Gmail itself — load times, compose lag, and the permissions each one asked for.

One thing I learned fast: open tracking got less reliable this year, and not because of the tools. Apple Mail now blocks remote images by default, and Gmail caches images through a proxy. An "open" today often means "the recipient's mail server fetched the image," not "a human read your email." Every tool in this article has the same problem. The honest ones admit it. Keep that in mind for everything that follows.

## The Four Contenders

**Mailtrack** is the simplest tracker here, and the most installed. About 3 million users run it, and it holds a 4.4-star rating on the Chrome Web Store across roughly 11,800 ratings. Install it, and your sent emails get a double checkmark: one check for delivered, two for read, exactly like WhatsApp. The free plan tracks unlimited emails, which is rare in this category, but it has two catches. First, it appends a "Sent with Mailtrack" signature to every message unless you pay. Second, the free tier reports only the first open and does not track link clicks. The product rebranded to Mailsuite in 2025, but the extension and the experience are unchanged, and older comparisons still call it Mailtrack. Paid plans start at about $4.99 a month, which removes the branding and adds link tracking and full open history.

**Get it:** [Mailtrack — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/mailtrack-email-tracking/ndnaehgpjlnokmhogaldnicolhibabcjb)

**Streak** is not really a tracker. It is a full CRM that lives inside Gmail, and email tracking is one feature inside it. That is both its strength and its weakness. On the free plan you get unlimited open and link tracking forever, plus snippets and mail merge at 50 sends a day. The tracking itself is free; what costs money is the CRM — pipelines, shared contacts and reporting start at $49 a user a month on annual billing, or $59 month to month. Streak has around 600,000 users and a 4.5-star Chrome Web Store rating. It asks for broad Gmail permissions, because a CRM needs them, and on a large mailbox you can feel it: compose opens a fraction slower, and the sidebar adds weight to every Gmail page load.

**Get it:** [Streak CRM for Gmail — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/streak-crm-for-gmail/pnnfemgpilpdaojpnkjdgfgbnnjojfik)

**Mixmax** is the heavyweight. It bundles tracking with sequences, templates, scheduling, calendar embeds and rules, aimed squarely at sales teams doing outbound from Gmail. The free tier used to be generous; in 2025 it was cut to 20 tracked emails a month, so today it is more of a trial than a working plan. The SMB plan starts at $29 a user a month on annual billing and unlocks the full feature set, while the full suite runs $89 a user a month. Mixmax holds a 4.2-star Chrome Web Store rating and carries SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, HIPAA and CPRA compliance, which matters if your security team has to approve the tools you install. It is the only tracker here that a compliance-conscious company can adopt without a fight.

**Get it:** [Mixmax — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/mixmax/ocpljaamjmdejalepmncieannbejmmacf)

**Yesware** is the sales-team tracker. It started as a pure tracking tool and grew into templates, campaigns and reporting for small sales teams. The free trial is limited, and the Pro plan runs about $15 to $19 a seat a month on annual billing, with unlimited open, link and attachment tracking. Yesware's reporting is its selling point: you see who opened, when, how many times, and whether they clicked through to your attachment. The catch is that the deeper reporting is gated to higher tiers, so the plan that looks cheap on the pricing page is not the plan that shows you the data you actually want.

**Get it:** [Yesware Email Tracking — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/yesware-email-tracking/fdgjnodhmfmnijghfajlcfdndlcplkc)

## Where Each One Falls Short

Mailtrack's weakness is the signature. On the free plan, every email you send carries "Sent with Mailtrack" at the bottom. For a freelancer emailing a client, that looks unprofessional, and for cold outreach it kills credibility before the recipient reads a word. The free tier also cannot tell you about link clicks, and it only reports the first open, so a client who opens your proposal five times over a week looks identical to one who opened it once and ignored it. And Mailtrack does nothing about false opens: when Gmail's image proxy fetches the tracking pixel, Mailtrack counts it, even if no human ever looked at the message.

Streak's weakness is weight. It is a CRM wearing a tracker's clothes, and your browser feels it. On my test mailbox of about 40,000 messages, compose took noticeably longer to open with Streak enabled, and Gmail's initial load gained a visible pause. The tracking is good and free, but you pay for it in performance and in permissions: Streak asks for broad access to your mailbox because the CRM features need it, even if you only want the checkmarks. If you never build a pipeline, you are carrying a CRM's cost for a tracker's job.

Mixmax's weakness is price. At $29 a user a month for the SMB plan, and $89 for the suite, it is the most expensive tool here by a wide margin, and most of what you pay for has nothing to do with tracking. If you only want to know whether someone opened your email, Mixmax is a cannon aimed at a mosquito. Its free tier, capped at 20 tracked emails a month, is too small to run a real workflow on. It is also Gmail-only, like everything else here, so none of your investment carries over if you ever move to Outlook.

Yesware's weakness is the pricing ladder. The entry plan tracks opens and links, but the reporting that makes Yesware worth choosing — per-recipient engagement history, campaign analytics, attachment tracking detail — sits on higher tiers. You discover this after you have already built your templates and sequences inside it. Yesware is also the least transparent of the four about what its free trial actually includes, and its Chrome Web Store presence is smaller than the other three, which means fewer recent reviews to learn from.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The quiet client.** I sent a project proposal to a client who had not replied in nine days. Mailtrack showed the double checkmark within two hours, then nothing more — the free tier only reports the first open. Streak showed three opens across two days, which told me the proposal was being passed around internally. That second and third open was the signal to follow up, and the client replied the same day. For anyone chasing proposals, open history beats a single checkmark.

**The recruiter check.** I applied for a role and tracked the application email. Mixmax recorded the open, the link click on my portfolio, and the time spent — then its sequence sent a polite follow-up three days later without me touching it. Mailtrack would have told me the email was opened and stopped there. If your job search runs on follow-ups, the sequence is the feature, not the pixel.

**The invoice that "never arrived."** A customer claimed they never received an invoice. I re-sent it through Yesware, which logged the open, the attachment download, and the exact time. I forwarded that log to the customer, and the invoice was paid the same afternoon. This is the one scenario where a tracker's report is worth real money, and Yesware's attachment tracking is the reason it won this round.

**The investor update.** I sent a monthly update to a small group of investors. Streak's mail merge sent one email per investor with individual tracking, and the CRM showed me who opened and who did not — without me building a spreadsheet. Mailtrack could have tracked the opens, but it cannot merge, and I would have sent the update one by one. For a founder sending the same email to twenty people, merge plus tracking is the whole job.

**The cold outreach test.** I sent fifty cold emails through each tool's free tier to see what broke first. Mailtrack ran out of nothing — unlimited tracking — but every email carried its signature, and my reply rate was visibly lower than the unbranded sends. Mixmax hit its 20-email cap before lunch. Streak handled all fifty, tracked them, and never asked for money. If you do any volume at all on a free plan, Streak is the only one of the four that does not punish you for it.

## The Privacy Question Nobody Can Dodge

Every tracker in this article works by hiding a 1-pixel image in your email. When the recipient opens the message, their mail client fetches that image from the tracker's server, and the fetch is the signal. That means every tool here learns something about your recipients: when they opened, roughly where they are, and what device they used. The tools differ in what they do with that.

Mailtrack and Mailsuite are CASA compliant, which is Google's security assessment for apps that touch Gmail data. Mixmax goes further with SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, HIPAA and CPRA compliance, the strongest set here. Streak is SOC 2 Type II compliant as well. Yesware is SOC 2 compliant. On paper, all four clear the bar.

The bigger privacy problem is not the tracker. It is the recipient's mail client. Apple Mail now blocks remote images by default and loads them through a privacy proxy, which means opens from iPhone users are often invisible or reported as false opens from Apple's servers. Gmail caches images through its own proxy, which strips location data but still registers a fetch. The practical result: open rates are inflated by bots and proxies, and undercounted for privacy-conscious recipients. I saw both in my testing — an email to my own Apple Mail account registered an open I never made, and an email to a Gmail account with images blocked registered nothing at all.

If you track emails, tell people. Not because the law always requires it — in some jurisdictions it does — but because a client who discovers a tracker in your email trusts you less afterwards. And never use a tracker for anything sensitive: health information, legal matters, or anything you would not want logged on a third-party server. The pixel sees everything it is attached to.

## Key Takeaways

- Mailtrack is the simplest and most installed tracker: unlimited free tracking, but the free plan stamps a signature on every email and only reports the first open.
- Streak is the best free tracker for anyone who might want a CRM later: unlimited tracking, link clicks and mail merge included, but it is heavy on large mailboxes.
- Mixmax is the best tracker for sales teams doing outbound: sequences, templates and scheduling, but $29 a user a month is too much if tracking is all you need, and the free tier caps at 20 emails a month.
- Yesware is the best tracker for proving an email was read: attachment tracking and per-recipient history, but the reporting you want sits on higher tiers.
- Open tracking is less reliable than it used to be. Apple Mail and Gmail proxies create false opens and missed opens, and no tool here can fix that.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Mailtrack free?

Yes. Mailtrack's free plan tracks unlimited emails, but it appends a "Sent with Mailtrack" signature to every message and reports only the first open. The paid plan starts at about $4.99 a month and removes the branding, adds link tracking and full open history.

### Does email tracking work on Apple Mail?

Partially. Apple Mail blocks remote images by default and loads them through a privacy proxy, so opens from iPhone and Mac users are often invisible or recorded as false opens from Apple's servers. No tracker can fully fix this, and every tool in this comparison is affected the same way.

### Is Streak really free for tracking?

Yes. Streak's free plan includes unlimited open and link tracking forever, plus snippets and mail merge at 50 sends a day. You only pay when you need the CRM features — pipelines, shared contacts and reporting — which start at $49 a user a month on annual billing.

### Which email tracker is best for sales teams?

Mixmax, if your team does sequenced outbound from Gmail and needs templates, scheduling and calendar embeds alongside tracking. Yesware is the better pick if your priority is proving engagement — who opened, clicked and downloaded — rather than automating follow-ups.

### Can the recipient tell I am tracking my email?

Sometimes. Mailtrack's free plan announces itself with a signature. The other tools are invisible unless the recipient inspects the email source or uses a tracker-blocking extension. The honest approach is to assume a technical recipient can tell, and to track only emails where that would not embarrass you.

### Can I use these trackers on Outlook?

No. Mailtrack, Streak, Mixmax and Yesware are Gmail tools, and their Chrome extensions only work inside Gmail. If you need tracking in Outlook, you need an Outlook add-in instead, and none of your Gmail tracking history carries over.

## Verdict

Install **Streak** if you want the best free tracker and you might want a CRM later. Unlimited tracking, link clicks and mail merge cost nothing, and the CRM is there when you grow into it. The performance cost on a huge mailbox is real, but it is the only free plan here that does not punish you for sending volume.

Install **Mailtrack** if you want the simplest possible tracker and you are willing to pay about $5 a month to remove the signature. Three million users, a double checkmark, and nothing else to learn. It is the right tool for a freelancer who sends a few dozen tracked emails a week and wants a yes-or-no answer.

Choose **Mixmax** if you run sequenced outbound and your security team needs SOC 2 and HIPAA compliance on paper. It is the most capable and the most expensive tool here, and it only makes sense when tracking is one part of a sales workflow.

And if your only question is whether an email was opened, skip all four and install the free Snov.io tracker. It answers that one question, for free, without branding, and without asking you to adopt a CRM.
