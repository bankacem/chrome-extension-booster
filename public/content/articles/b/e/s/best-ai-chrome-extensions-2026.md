Meanwhile Google shipped Gemini directly into Chrome — see our [complete Gemini in Chrome guide](/blog/gemini-in-chrome-2026-complete-guide) — which raises a fair question: which third-party AI extensions are still worth installing?

The twelve picks below survived that process. ## The 12 best AI Chrome extensions of 2026

### Gemini in Chrome — the built-in baseline

OUR PICK FOR MOST PEOPLE · FREE TIER

Google's native assistant now handles what most people installed extensions for: page-aware chat, five-bullet summaries, multi-tab comparisons and, on AI Pro/Ultra plans, the Auto Browse agent that completes tasks across sites. Start here before installing anything — our [full guide](/blog/gemini-in-chrome-2026-complete-guide) covers enabling every feature.

**Pros:** free, zero setup, deepest page context, full mobile rollout. **Cons:** agentic features gated behind subscription; no specialized writing or video tools.

### Sider — the strongest all-in-one sidebar

SUBSCRIPTION

Sider remains the most complete third-party assistant: one sidebar that opens ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini models side by side, with page summarization, PDF chat, translation and a library of prompt tools layered on top. In our tests its group-chat mode — asking two models the same question — was the fastest way to sanity-check AI answers on important topics.

**Pros:** model variety, mature PDF and webpage chat. **Cons:** credit system adds up; overlaps heavily with built-in Gemini.

### Grammarly — still the writing king

BEST WRITING ASSISTANT · FREE TIER

Grammarly's 2026 generation goes far beyond comma policing: tone rewriting, full-sentence generations from short prompts, and context-aware suggestions that respect the field you are typing in — Gmail, LinkedIn, a CMS editor or a support desk. No native browser assistant matches its inline, keystroke-level integration, which is why it stays installed on every test machine we own.

**Pros:** works in virtually every text field, excellent free tier. **Cons:** premium suggestions can be aggressive; adds input lag on weak hardware.

### QuillBot — paraphrasing and summarizing on demand

FREE TIER

QuillBot remains the reference for rewriting. Select any paragraph, choose a mode — fluency, formal, concise — and get an alternative in place. Students and non-native writers are the obvious audience, but our editors use its summarizer daily for condensing background reading before reviews.

**Pros:** best-in-class paraphrase quality, generous free quota. **Cons:** premium modes locked; no agentic features.

### Monic AI — the research copilot

SUBSCRIPTION

Monic AI is what happens when a note app and an AI assistant have a child: you feed it pages, PDFs and your own files, and it answers questions with citations back to the source. For thesis research, legal reviews or competitive analysis, it turned multi-hour reading sessions into focused Q&A. Its workspace syncs across devices, so your research context follows you.

**Pros:** cited answers, real knowledge base, great for long projects. **Cons:** heavier learning curve; overkill for casual browsing.

### Immersive Translate — bilingual reading without friction

BEST TRANSLATION · FREE TIER

While Chrome's native translation works well, Immersive Translate transforms how you read foreign content: it renders the translation directly under each original paragraph, preserving layout and letting you compare both languages at a glance. It also handles PDFs, subtitles and input boxes. If you regularly read sources in a second language, this is transformative — try it on a Japanese news site or a German research paper once and you will never go back.

**Pros:** unique inline layout, many translation engines, PDF and subtitle support. **Cons:** best engines require API keys or subscription.

### Video summarizers — the YouTube time machine

3 VIDEOS/DAY FREE

The Eightify-style category — timestamped YouTube summaries with key-point bullets — matured into one of the most reliable AI utilities of 2026. Paste nothing: open a lecture or podcast video and the extension displays a structured outline you can click to jump to the relevant minute. Our tests on two-hour conference talks cut "watch time" by roughly 80 percent with no loss of the important content. See our dedicated roundup of [YouTube extensions that save time](/blog/youtube-extensions-that-actually-save-you-time).

**Pros:** massive time savings, clickable chapters. **Cons:** accuracy varies with audio quality; free daily limits.

### Speechify-style readers — turn pages into podcasts

SUBSCRIPTION

AI text-to-speech reached near-human quality in 2026, and the Speechify-style extensions let any article, PDF or Google Doc flow into your ears at adjustable speed while you walk or commute. Natural voices in dozens of languages, highlight tracking and speed listening up to 4.5x make this the accessibility tool of the year as much as a productivity one.

**Pros:** excellent voices, speed listening, cross-device sync. **Cons:** best voices subscription-only; power drain on laptops.

### Bardeen-style automations — no-code AI recipes

SUBSCRIPTION

Bardeen-style tools connect your browser actions into pipelines: scrape a LinkedIn search into Sheets, summarize each result and post the digest to Slack — without writing code. With 2026's AI steps built in, the recipes now understand unstructured pages, which previously required custom scripts. Power users get a genuine employee-that-never-sleeps; casual users should start with the template library instead of building from scratch.

**Pros:** real automation, deep app integrations. **Cons:** runs quota limited; recipes need maintenance when sites change.

### Merlin-style hubs — many models, one shortcut

LIMITED QUERIES

Merlin-style hubs put a dozen models — GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama and image generators — behind a single keyboard shortcut on any page. The value is comparison shopping for answers: generate with one model, refine with another, all without leaving the site you are on. For writers who hit rate limits on a single service, the aggregated quota alone justifies installation.

**Pros:** one subscription covers many models. **Cons:** quality per model slightly behind native apps; UI can feel busy.

### Clipboard AI pickers — instant rewrite in any text box

FREE TIER

The minimalist end of the spectrum: a small AI button that appears in any input field, ready to fix grammar, change tone or translate the draft you just typed. Where Grammarly watches your typing, clipboard pickers act on demand — a calmer model that many users now prefer for focus. Ideal for occasional senders who want correction without a resident assistant.

**Pros:** lightweight, unobtrusive, works everywhere. **Cons:** no continuous protection; features vary by developer.

## How we tested

Every candidate went through the same three-week protocol. First, installation and permission audit — we flag anything requesting broad access without justification, as detailed in our [extension security guide](/ar/blog/chrome-extension-security-risks-permission-audit-guide). Second, daily real usage across four scenarios: research digestion, email and writing, foreign-language reading, and video learning. Third, performance measurement: memory footprint with the sidebar open and closed, startup impact, and interference with other extensions. Tools that degraded browsing or burned RAM without clear value were cut regardless of features.

> RAM reality check: Chrome AI-era browsing is memory-hungry. If your machine struggles after installing assistants, enable Memory Saver and pair it with a tab suspender — the combination recovers most of the overhead.

## Which AI extensions should you actually install?

Resist the urge to install all twelve. The 2026 stack that tested best for us is deliberately small: the built-in **Gemini sidebar** for questions and summaries, **Grammarly** for writing, one **video summarizer** if you learn from YouTube, and the **ExtensionTo protection stack** to keep the environment clean. Add a research copilot like Monic AI only if your work involves sustained projects, and an automation tool only if you have identified a repetitive workflow worth an hour of setup.

That is four to six extensions doing the work of the twenty-plus tools articles like this one listed in 2024 — consolidation is the real trend of 2026, driven by browsers absorbing general-purpose AI. The winners are specialists and guardians: tools that do one thing better than the native assistant, or protect you while it works.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best AI Chrome extension in 2026?

For most users the Gemini sidebar built into Chrome is now the default starting point, because it is free and page-aware. Among third-party extensions, Sider and Monic AI lead for all-in-one assistance, Grammarly remains the strongest writing assistant, and video summarizers are the fastest way to digest long content.

### Are AI Chrome extensions safe to install?

Reputable AI extensions from established developers are generally safe, but audit permissions before installing, avoid tools that request access to all sites without a clear reason, and prefer extensions with visible privacy policies.

### Do AI Chrome extensions cost money?

Most use a freemium model: a daily quota of free actions with paid plans for higher limits and premium models. Expect free tiers to cover casual use and subscriptions of roughly $8–$20 per month for heavy use.

### Will AI extensions slow down Chrome?

AI sidebars are mostly idle until opened, so their impact is small, but stacking many assistants causes Chrome RAM creep. Pair your setup with a tab suspender and audit extensions monthly.

### How do AI extensions differ from Gemini in Chrome?

Gemini in Chrome covers general page-aware chat and agentic tasks natively. Third-party extensions differentiate with specialized workflows: better writing corrections, video summaries, inline translation overlays, voice reading and automation recipes.
