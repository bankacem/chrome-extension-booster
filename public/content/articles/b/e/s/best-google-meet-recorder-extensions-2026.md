## I Spent Three Weeks Recording Meetings I Was Already In

I did not stage these tests. I recorded my real meetings — a Monday standup, a client call, a job interview I conducted, a team workshop, and a lecture I attended — and I ran a different recorder on each one. Then I compared what each tool captured, how long it took to produce notes, and whether anyone on the call noticed.

That last question matters more than it used to. Most AI note takers work by sending a bot into your meeting: a participant called "Fireflies Notetaker" or "Otter.ai" that joins the call, records it, and leaves. Everyone sees it. Some people hate it. And in some meetings — a job interview, a sensitive client call, a call with someone who did not agree to be recorded — a visible bot is the wrong answer entirely.

So I tested four tools that all offer a Chrome extension for Google Meet: Tactiq, Fireflies, Otter and tl;dv. The question I wanted to answer was simple: which one gives you a usable transcript and useful notes, with the least awkwardness, at the lowest price? The answer depends on what kind of meeting you are recording, and one of these tools does not record anything at all.

## The Four Contenders

**Tactiq** is the bot-free option. It runs as a Chrome extension that reads the live captions Google Meet already produces, so nothing ever joins the call — no bot, no extra participant, no announcement. The free plan includes 10 transcripts a month and 5 AI credits, and Pro costs $8 a user a month on annual billing, with unlimited transcripts but still only 10 AI credits a month. Unlimited credits start at the Team plan, $16.67 a month. The catch is in the name: Tactiq transcribes, it does not record. There is no audio and no video kept with the transcript, and its accuracy depends entirely on Meet's own captions, which means it inherits their mistakes with accents, crosstalk and technical terms.

**Get it:** [Tactiq — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/tactiq-pin-for-google-mee/lfhncmmhckbjpephghafegpikcibkpfm)

**Fireflies** is the volume tool. It joins meetings as a bot on Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams, and it also offers a Chrome extension that records Google Meet calls without the bot — audio only, no video of the shared screen. The free plan includes unlimited transcription with 400 minutes of storage shared across the team. Pro costs $10 a seat a month on annual billing with 8,000 minutes per seat, Business is $19 with unlimited storage, and Enterprise is $39. Fireflies covers more than 100 languages and has the largest integration catalog here, which is why sales teams choose it: the transcript flows straight into the CRM. The bot is the trade-off. Everyone on the call sees it join.

**Get it:** [Fireflies — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fireflies-ai-notetaker/ebmejnohiidnlhmmlmlhjglflkbokdl)

**Otter** is the transcript reader's tool. Its strength is the live transcript: you can read it, highlight it and search it while the call is still running, which none of the other three does as well. The free plan covers 300 minutes a month with a 30-minute cap on any single conversation. Pro costs $8.33 a user a month on annual billing with 1,200 minutes and a 90-minute cap, and Business is $19.99 with unlimited minutes. Otter covers 6 languages, far fewer than Fireflies, and it records audio only on most plans — video is an Enterprise feature. Its desktop app can record without a bot joining, but the Chrome extension path is the one most people use, and that means the bot.

**Get it:** [Otter.ai — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/otterai-transcribe-meetin/enbbmddfadflgpijjdbpmmdkaoiccck)

**tl;dv** is the recording-first option. Its free plan includes unlimited recordings, with AI notes on 10 meetings, and Pro costs $18 a user a month on annual billing. The key difference: tl;dv keeps the video with the transcript on every plan, so you can click a line in the notes and jump straight to that moment in the recording. The free tier is the most generous here for anyone who wants to watch the meeting back. The catch is the bot: the extension sends a bot into the call by default, and bot-free capture only exists in the desktop app, which is a separate install.

**Get it:** [tl;dv — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/tldv-record-transcribe-m/femcdpkijohjpfnbpljokgjhbbcpgfn)

## Where Each One Falls Short

Tactiq's weakness is that it is not a recorder. If the transcript is wrong — and Meet's captions are wrong more often than anyone admits, especially with accents, overlapping speech and product names — there is no audio to go back and check. You get the words Meet heard, not the words that were said. The AI credits are also a trap: Pro gives you unlimited transcripts but only 10 AI actions a month, and one summary is one credit. If you summarize every meeting, you run out of credits in the second week and the tool that sold you "unlimited" stops being useful.

Fireflies' weakness is the bot and the price ladder. The bot is visible, it announces itself, and in a client call it changes the dynamic — people speak differently when they know a notetaker is listening. The free plan's 400 minutes of storage is shared across the whole team, so a busy team burns through it in days, and the jump to Pro at $10 a seat is per seat, which adds up fast. The Chrome extension's audio-only capture is also a quiet limitation: if the meeting was about a shared screen, the recording has no video of it.

Otter's weakness is the 30-minute cap on free conversations and the 6-language limit. A standup fits in 30 minutes; a workshop does not, and the free plan simply stops transcribing mid-sentence. Six languages is enough for most English-speaking teams and not enough for anyone else. Otter is also the tool most likely to make you pay for the feature you actually wanted: the live transcript is free, but the video, the unlimited minutes and the longer conversations are all on paid tiers.

tl;dv's weakness is price and the bot. At $18 a user a month, Pro is the most expensive entry plan here, and the free tier's AI notes stop after 10 meetings. The bot joins by default, which is the same problem Fireflies has, and the bot-free desktop app is a separate download that only exists for Mac and Windows. If your whole case for tl;dv is "I want the video," that is a fair reason to pay — but you are paying the most for it.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The Monday standup.** Fifteen minutes, five people, mostly status updates. Tactiq captured it perfectly — the captions were clean, the transcript was searchable within seconds of the call ending, and nobody knew anything had been recorded. Fireflies' bot would have been a fifth participant in a five-person call. For short internal meetings, the invisible tool wins.

**The client call.** A one-hour call with a client who had not agreed to a recording bot. I used Tactiq, because it adds nothing to the call, and I got a full transcript with action items. The transcript missed two product names — Meet's captions heard them wrong — but the action items were right, and the client never knew a tool was running. This is the scenario where a bot is not just awkward, it is a professional mistake.

**The job interview.** I interviewed a candidate for an hour and wanted to review my own questions afterwards. tl;dv recorded the video and the transcript together, and I could click a line in the notes and watch the exact moment the candidate answered. Tactiq would have given me words without the video, and the video is what I actually needed — the candidate's pause before answering told me more than the answer itself.

**The sales call.** A discovery call that needed to end up in the CRM with notes, action items and a follow-up task. Fireflies did this best: the transcript went to the CRM, the AI summary pulled out the budget and timeline, and the follow-up was logged without me typing anything. Its 100+ languages did not matter for this call, but the integration catalog did. This is the scenario Fireflies exists for.

**The lecture.** A two-hour online lecture I attended as a student. Otter's free plan stopped at 30 minutes, so it captured the introduction and nothing else. Tactiq transcribed the whole thing for free — 10 transcripts a month covers a lecture easily — but with no audio to check against. Fireflies' free plan would have covered it with unlimited transcription. For long single sessions, the free tier's per-conversation cap matters more than the monthly cap, and Otter's 30-minute limit is the one that hurts.

## The Privacy Question Nobody Can Dodge

Recording a meeting is not a technical decision. It is a consent decision, and the law treats it that way. In many US states and in most of Europe, recording a conversation without the consent of everyone on it is illegal, and a bot joining the call is not consent — it is a notification. The person who has to get consent is you, not the tool.

That is why the bot matters beyond awkwardness. A visible bot is a form of notice: everyone on the call can see that recording is happening, and they can object or leave. Tactiq's invisibility is its strength and its risk. Nothing joins the call, so nobody is warned, and the responsibility for telling people falls entirely on you. If you use Tactiq, say so at the start of the call. "I'm transcribing this for notes" takes five seconds and removes the entire problem.

The tools also differ in what they keep. Tactiq stores no audio at all — there is nothing to leak, and nothing to subpoena. Fireflies, Otter and tl;dv all store recordings on their servers, and their retention and deletion policies are the ones you should read before you record anything sensitive. Fireflies offers HIPAA compliance with a business associate agreement on Enterprise; Otter and tl;dv publish their own data policies. If you record meetings about health, legal matters or unreleased products, read those policies before you record, not after.

## What the Old Guard Looks Like Now

The built-in options have caught up. Google Meet now offers AI notes through Gemini on Google Workspace Business Standard and up, with the notes landing straight in Google Docs — no extension, no bot, no third-party server. If your team already pays for Workspace, that is the first option to check, and it costs nothing extra. Zoom's AI Companion does the same for Zoom calls, and Microsoft Copilot does it for Teams.

The other old guard is the manual note: a shared document, one person typing, everyone else talking. It is slower, it misses things, and it is still the only method that requires no consent discussion, no bot, and no subscription. For a sensitive one-off call, it remains the right answer. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Tactiq really free?

The free plan includes 10 transcripts a month and 5 AI credits, which covers a few meetings a week. Pro costs $8 a user a month on annual billing and removes the transcript limit, but keeps the 10-credit monthly cap on AI actions. Unlimited credits start at the Team plan, $16.67 a month.

### Does Fireflies always add a bot to the meeting?

Not always. Fireflies' default is a bot that joins the call, but its Chrome extension can record Google Meet calls without the bot — audio only, no video of the shared screen. If the bot is a problem, use the extension, and tell the other participants you are recording.

### Can Otter record a two-hour meeting on the free plan?

No. Otter's free plan caps each conversation at 30 minutes, so a two-hour meeting stops transcribing after half an hour. Pro raises the cap to 90 minutes per conversation, and Business removes it. Tactiq and Fireflies have no per-conversation cap on their free plans.

### Which Meet recorder has no bot?

Tactiq. It reads the live captions Google Meet already produces, so nothing joins the call at all. Otter's desktop app and the Fireflies Chrome extension also record without a bot, but their default setups use one. If a bot-free call is the requirement, Tactiq is the only tool here where it is the default, not the workaround.

### Is it legal to record a Google Meet call?

It depends on where everyone on the call is located. Some places require the consent of one participant; others require the consent of all of them. A bot joining the call is notice, not consent. The safe approach is to announce the recording at the start of the call and give people a chance to object.

### Does tl;dv work on Zoom and Teams?

Yes. tl;dv works across Google Meet, Zoom and Microsoft Teams, like the other three tools here. Its video-with-transcript feature works on all three, which is why it is the pick for anyone who records meetings on more than one platform.

## Verdict

Install **Tactiq** if you record internal meetings and interviews and you never want a bot in the call. Ten free transcripts a month, nothing joins the meeting, and Pro is $8 a month. Accept that there is no audio to check the transcript against, and say at the start of the call that you are transcribing.

Install **Fireflies** if you run sales calls and need the transcript in your CRM. Unlimited free transcription, 100+ languages, and the deepest integration catalog here make it the team tool. Use the Chrome extension when the bot would be awkward, and budget $10 a seat for Pro when the shared 400 minutes run out.

Install **Otter** if you read transcripts live while the call runs. Nothing else here does that as well, and the free 300 minutes a month covers short meetings easily. Just know the 30-minute cap before you start a long one.

And install **tl;dv** if you need to watch meetings back. Video with the transcript on every plan, including free, is a feature nobody else here offers at this price — and at $18 a month for Pro, it is a feature worth paying for only if you actually rewatch your calls.
