## I Spent Two Weeks Living Inside Four Gmail Extensions

I get about 60 emails a day. I counted them, because counting them was the first thing that made me feel slightly ill about my own inbox. Of those 60, roughly a third need an answer, a third are newsletters I signed up for in a moment of optimism, and a third are things I should have dealt with last week. That is the real state of most people's email, mine included, and it is the reason I stopped trusting Gmail's default interface and started testing the extensions that turn it into a proper email client.

For two weeks I ran four of them through my actual inbox: checking mail without opening Gmail, scheduling sends for the morning, chasing replies that never came, tracking whether anyone opened what I sent, and sharing an inbox with a colleague to see which tool actually works for a team. I also watched what each one costs now, because the free tiers in this category are thinner than they look, and the prices quoted in last year's roundups are wrong.

The four tools split into two camps, and the split matters more than any feature list. Checker Plus and Boomerang are for individuals who want Gmail to do more. Mailtrack and Gmelius are for people who send email as part of their job — sales, support, teams. Which camp you belong in decides most of this comparison before you read a single feature.

## The Four Contenders

**Checker Plus for Gmail** is the free notifier, and the only tool here with no subscription at all. It has more than 1,000,000 Chrome users and a rating around 4.3 stars across roughly 12,700 ratings, and it was last updated on October 14, 2026 to version 36.6. It is made by Jason Savard, an independent developer, and it shows: the tool does one job obsessively well. It shows unread counts and desktop notifications across multiple Gmail and Workspace accounts without opening Gmail at all, and you can read, reply, archive and delete from the popup or from Chrome's side panel. It works in the background even when Chrome is closed, it can read mail aloud with voice notifications, and there is an optional AI reply-draft feature using Gemini, off by default. All core features are free; an optional contribution of any amount unlocks a few extras. The weaknesses: it is desktop Chrome only, with nothing on mobile; it is a single independent developer with no team or service agreement behind it; and the optional AI features send thread content to Google's Gemini API, which is worth knowing before you turn them on.

**Get it:** [Checker Plus for Gmail — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/checker-plus-for-gmail/oeopbcgkkoapgobdbedcemjljbihmemj)

**Boomerang for Gmail** is the scheduling and follow-up tool, and the one I used most for actual work. It has roughly 900,000 to 1,000,000 Chrome users and a rating around 4.2 stars, and it was last updated on October 14, 2026. Its core features are Send Later with recurring messages, follow-up reminders that return an email to your inbox if nobody replies, a Respondable AI writing score inside compose, Inbox Pause, a Magic Live Calendar for one-click meeting scheduling, and read and click tracking. The pricing is on [Boomerang's subscriptions page](https://www.boomeranggmail.com/subscriptions.html): Free Basic gives you 10 message credits a month; Personal costs $4.98 a month billed annually and covers Gmail only; Pro costs $14.98 a month billed annually and adds Workspace; Premium costs $49.98 a month billed annually. There is a 30-day free Pro trial with no card required. The weaknesses: the free tier is very thin — 10 credits a month is exhausted fast; recurring messages, advanced Respondable and advanced Inbox Pause are paywalled; the Personal plan excludes Google Workspace addresses, which is a nasty surprise for anyone on a work account; and it requests broad permissions, including managing other extensions, with occasional lag reported in reviews.

**Get it:** [Boomerang for Gmail — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/boomerang-for-gmail/mdanidgdpmkimeiiojknlnekblgmpdll)

**Mailtrack, now Mailsuite,** is the tracking tool, and the one with the most users in this group. It has 3,000,000 Chrome users and a 4.4-star rating across roughly 11,800 ratings, and it was last updated on October 14, 2026. It is made by Mailsuite in Barcelona, it is GDPR-compliant, Google-audited and ISO certified, and the vendor claims more than 116,000 paying subscribers. It puts double-checkmark read receipts inside Gmail, sends real-time open notifications, tracks per-recipient opens in group emails, and adds link and PDF tracking, mail merge and campaigns, plus a self-organised CRM and a mobile Gmail add-on. The pricing is on [Mailsuite's pricing page](https://mailsuite.com/en/pricing): Free costs nothing; the Mailtrack plan is $6.99 per user a month; the Mailsuite plan is $19.99 per user a month. The free plan gives you unlimited open tracking, but it stamps a "Sent with Mailtrack" signature on every email you send, keeps only first-open history, caps link tracking at 10 emails and campaigns at 100 emails a month. Paid removes the signature and lifts the caps, up to 60,000 campaign emails a month. The weaknesses: the free signature is advertising on your own emails, and it is the first thing recipients notice; pixel-based tracking fails when recipients block remote images or use privacy proxies, so you get false "unread" results; tracking recipients in the EU carries consent obligations; and the rebrand from Mailtrack to Mailsuite confuses people, because mailtrack.io now redirects and older reviews cite a $4.99-a-month PRO plan that no longer exists.

**Get it:** [Mailtrack — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/email-tracker-by-mailtrac/ndnaehgpjlnokgebbaldlmgkapkpjkkb)

**Gmelius** is the team tool, and the only one here that is not really for individuals at all. It has about 50,000 Chrome users and a 4.6-star rating across roughly 691 ratings, and it was last updated on October 1, 2026 to version 9.1.47. It is made by Gmelius SA in Switzerland, and it turns Gmail into a shared workspace: shared inboxes, shared labels, Kanban boards, assignments, collision detection so two people do not answer the same email, internal notes and mentions, automation rules, SLA timers and analytics, and integrations with Slack, HubSpot and Trello. In March 2026 it launched Meli, an AI assistant that drafts replies, sorts and classifies mail, schedules meetings and follows up. The pricing is on [Gmelius's pricing page](https://gmelius.com/pricing): Meli costs $19 per user a month on annual billing; Growth is $25; Pro is $40; Enterprise is custom with a minimum of 50 users. There is a 7-day trial and no free tier. The weaknesses are significant for individuals: there is no free tier at all; per-user pricing gets expensive for a team; the cheapest plan has no shared inbox — it is AI-assistant-only — which is a surprise for a tool sold as a shared-inbox product; users complain of slowness on large inboxes, with 10-plus seconds to open a mail; it is Gmail and Workspace only, with no Outlook or IMAP; and email tracking and campaigns are discontinued for new users, citing GDPR, so older reviews praising those features are outdated.

**Get it:** [Gmelius — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/gmelius-shared-inboxes-ai-email-assistants/dheionainndbbpoacpnopgmnihkcmnkl)

## Where Each One Falls Short

Checker Plus's weakness is that it is one person. Jason Savard has maintained it for years and it is excellent, but there is no team, no service agreement and no mobile app, and if he stops, it stops. The desktop-only limitation is the practical one: the tool that tells you about your mail does not exist on the phone where you actually read most of it. And the AI reply drafts, while optional and off by default, send your thread content to Google's Gemini API when you turn them on, which is a privacy decision worth making deliberately.

Boomerang's weakness is the free tier, and it is not close. Ten message credits a month is not a free plan, it is a demo: one scheduled email a day for ten days, and then you are done for the month. The paywalling is aggressive — recurring messages, the good version of Respondable, the good version of Inbox Pause — and the Personal plan excluding Workspace addresses means the people most likely to need it, at work, have to pay for Pro at three times the price. The broad permissions, including managing other extensions, are also worth a pause.

Mailtrack's weakness is the signature. A free plan that stamps "Sent with Mailtrack" on every email you send is advertising on your own correspondence, and it is the first thing a recipient sees. It is the price of free tracking, and for personal email it is a bad trade. The tracking itself is also fragile: if the recipient blocks remote images or uses a privacy proxy, the pixel never fires and the email shows as unread when it was read. And in the EU, tracking opens without consent is a legal question, not just an etiquette one.

Gmelius's weakness is the price and the pivot. There is no free tier, the cheapest plan is $19 per user a month, and that plan does not include the shared inbox that is the product's whole point — you need Growth at $25 a user a month for that. The AI pivot in March 2026 repositioned it as an agent platform, and in the process email tracking and campaigns were closed to new users. For a team that lives in Gmail, it is still the best tool here. For an individual, it is the wrong product at the wrong price.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The multi-account morning.** I have two Gmail accounts and a Workspace account, and I check all three every morning. Checker Plus was the only tool that handled this without opening Gmail at all: unread counts for all three in the toolbar, a popup that let me read, reply, archive and delete without opening a tab, and notifications that arrived even when Chrome was closed. Boomerang, Mailtrack and Gmelius all live inside Gmail, so they cannot help you before you open it. For the morning triage, Checker Plus won outright.

**The email that had to go out at 9am.** I wrote an email at 11pm that needed to arrive at 9am, when the recipient starts work. Boomerang's Send Later handled it in two clicks, and its recurring-message feature scheduled the weekly version of the same email. None of the other three does send-later: Checker Plus is a notifier, Mailtrack is a tracker, Gmelius's Meli can schedule meetings and follow-ups but not a plain send-later. If you write emails at night and send them in the morning, Boomerang is the only tool here that does it.

**The reply that never came.** I sent a proposal on Monday and heard nothing by Thursday. Boomerang's follow-up reminder returned the email to my inbox on Thursday morning with a nudge to chase it, and the proposal closed the next day. Mailtrack told me the email had been opened twice, which was useful in a different way — I knew it had been seen, so the silence was a decision, not an oversight. Checker Plus could not help, and Gmelius's follow-up features are part of the Meli assistant on the paid plans. For chasing replies, Boomerang's reminder and Mailtrack's open data are complementary, and I ended up wanting both.

**The shared support inbox.** I shared an inbox with a colleague for a week, answering customer mail together. Gmelius was the only tool that made this work properly: shared inbox, shared labels, Kanban boards, assignments, and collision detection that warned us when we were both typing a reply to the same customer. Boomerang and Mailtrack have no shared inbox at all. Checker Plus can show multiple accounts, but it cannot share one. For a team inbox, Gmelius won, and it was not close — but it is also the only tool here that charges $25 a user a month for the privilege.

**The tracked pitch.** I sent a pitch to a potential client and wanted to know if it was read. Mailtrack's double-checkmark appeared within the hour, and the real-time notification told me exactly when it was opened. It was genuinely useful — I timed my follow-up for the next morning instead of waiting a week. But the free plan stamped "Sent with Mailtrack" on the pitch, which is not the impression you want to make on a potential client, and the recipient was in the EU, where tracking opens without consent is a legal grey area. I paid for the $6.99 plan to remove the signature, which is exactly how the free tier is designed to work.

## What to Watch Out For: Permissions, Tracking Law and the Manifest V3 Clean-Out

An email extension sees your mail, which means it can see a lot: your conversations, your attachments, your contacts. All four of these request broad Gmail permissions, because that is how they work — the extension has to read your inbox to notify you, schedule for you, track for you or share it. That is normal, and it is also the reason you should install only the official extension from the Chrome Web Store, published by the company itself, and never a lookalike with a similar name.

The deeper question is what happens to your mail. Checker Plus is made by one developer and sends nothing anywhere unless you turn on the AI drafts, which use Google's Gemini API. Boomerang processes your mail on its own servers to provide scheduling and tracking. Mailtrack's tracking pixel is the product: it tells the sender when you open an email, and if you are the recipient, you may not know it is there. Gmelius's AI assistant reads your mail to draft replies and classify it. Read the privacy policy of whichever one you install, and do not install an email extension casually — it is the most intimate permission you will ever grant a browser tool.

The tracking question has a legal side too. Mailtrack is GDPR-compliant and Google-audited, but tracking the opens of EU recipients carries consent obligations, and its own tracking can fail when recipients block remote images or use privacy proxies. Gmelius discontinued tracking and campaigns for new users, citing GDPR. If you send tracked email to people in Europe, know the rules before you do it.

The other thing to watch is the state of the category after the Manifest V3 clean-out. Google removed all remaining Manifest V2 extensions from the Chrome Web Store on August 31, 2026, and all four tools above are Manifest V3 survivors. That is a useful filter: if an email extension you are considering has not been updated in 2026, do not install it.

## Two More Worth Knowing About

If the four above do not fit, the two alternatives I would look at first are Sortd for Gmail and Right Inbox. Sortd turns Gmail into Trello-style Kanban boards with drag-and-drop columns, and it is the best option if you want to manage your inbox like a project board. It has a Starter plan from $10 per user a month on annual billing. Right Inbox bundles send-later, reminders, recurring emails, sequences, templates and open tracking into one lightweight extension, with about 100,000 users and a 4.5-star rating. Its free Limited plan caps each feature at 5 uses a month, and Professional costs $7.95 a month on annual billing. One caution from November 2026 reviews: multiple Trustpilot complaints report charges continuing after cancellation, and its store privacy label contradicts the company's own privacy policy, so check the billing terms before you pay.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which Gmail extension is actually free?

Checker Plus, for individuals. Its core features — multi-account notifications, reading and replying from the popup — are free, with no subscription. Boomerang's free tier is 10 credits a month, Mailtrack's free tier stamps an ad on your emails, and Gmelius has no free tier at all.

### Is Mailtrack's free plan worth it?

Only if you do not mind the signature. Unlimited open tracking for free is a good deal, but every email you send carries "Sent with Mailtrack" at the bottom, which is advertising on your own correspondence. For personal email it is a bad trade; for sales outreach, the $6.99 plan that removes it is the honest price.

### Can Boomerang schedule emails to send later?

Yes, that is its best feature. Send Later lets you write an email now and send it at a chosen time, and recurring messages repeat it on a schedule. The free tier gives you 10 message credits a month, so regular use needs the Personal plan at $4.98 a month billed annually.

### Is it legal to track whether someone opened my email?

It depends where they are. Mailtrack is GDPR-compliant, but tracking opens of EU recipients carries consent obligations, and Gmelius discontinued tracking for new users citing GDPR. Pixel tracking also fails when recipients block remote images. Treat open data as a signal, not proof, and do not rely on it for anything important.

### Which Gmail extension is best for a team?

Gmelius, by a clear margin. Shared inboxes, shared labels, Kanban boards, assignments, collision detection and SLA timers are built for teams. But it is expensive — Growth is $25 per user a month on annual billing — and the cheapest Meli plan at $19 a user a month does not include a shared inbox.

### Do these extensions work on my phone?

Checker Plus is desktop Chrome only. Boomerang, Mailtrack and Gmelius all have mobile support through the Gmail app or an add-on, but the full experience is on desktop. If you do most of your email on your phone, test the mobile experience before you commit to a paid plan.

## Verdict

Install **Checker Plus** if you want the best free Gmail tool. Multi-account notifications, read and reply from the popup, no subscription, and it works in the background even when Chrome is closed. Accept that it is one developer and desktop-only.

Install **Boomerang** if you live by scheduling and follow-ups. Send Later, recurring messages and the follow-up reminder that brings an unanswered email back to your inbox are the best in this group. Accept the 10-credit free tier and pay for Personal at $4.98 a month if you use it daily.

Install **Mailtrack** if you need to know whether your emails were opened. Three million users, double-checkmark receipts and real-time notifications. Pay the $6.99 a month to remove the signature — the free plan's ad on your emails is not worth it.

Install **Gmelius** if you run a team inbox in Gmail. Shared inboxes, Kanban, collision detection and the Meli AI assistant are the best team features here. Accept that there is no free tier and that the shared inbox starts at Growth, $25 per user a month.

If I had to keep only one, I would keep Checker Plus — the tool that told me about my mail before I opened Gmail, cost nothing, and never once asked me to upgrade to do its job.
