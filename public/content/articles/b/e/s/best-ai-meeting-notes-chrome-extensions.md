## The Privacy Reality Check

![The privacy reality check for AI meeting notes extensions — glowing padlock over a laptop keyboard](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1614064641938-3bbee52942c7?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

To work on Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams, meeting assistants need permissions that most extensions never touch:

- `Host permissions: <all_urls>` — the ability to read every website you visit
- `activeTab` — access to your current tab
- `tabCapture` — the ability to record audio from your browser

Fireflies.ai, one of the most popular meeting assistants, requests `<all_urls>` — the broadest permission Chrome offers. The justification is legitimate (meetings can happen on any meeting-platform URL, so the extension must see enough to detect them), but the same permission could theoretically access any other site you visit. Privacy researchers at Incogni flagged transcription and meeting assistant extensions with the highest privacy risk scores in their 2026 extension report for exactly this reason.

✅ **Safe practice:** only install meeting note extensions on a dedicated "work browser profile" used just for meetings, or use a tool that processes audio locally. The full safe-setup routine appears at the end of this guide.

## Fireflies.ai — The Most Popular (But Riskiest)

**Free tier: Unlimited transcriptions | 100,000+ users | Updated March 2026**

![Fireflies.ai features and risks — microphone on a desk in front of a conference screen](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1655720828018-edd2daec9349?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

Fireflies is the 900-pound gorilla of meeting notes. It works on Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, and even Webex.

### What Makes It Popular

- **Unlimited free transcriptions** — most competitors cap free usage
- **CRM integrations** — automatically logs notes to Salesforce and HubSpot
- **Searchable database** — find that one comment from three months ago
- **Action item detection** — the AI flags "John will send the proposal"
- **Real-time transcription** — live captions during the meeting

### User Reviews Summary

**Pros users report:** reliable and consistently working, seamless CRM integrations and workflow controls, a genuine productivity boost as a "second brain," and instant sharing that shortens post-meeting workflows.

**Cons users report:** the AI struggles with attendee identification and accents, sometimes misattributes who said what, and shows occasional stability issues.

### Privacy Risk Analysis

Fireflies requests `Host permissions: <all_urls>` — permission to read and change data on every website you visit. The justification: it needs to detect when you're in a meeting, which can be on any URL. The concern: that same permission could theoretically access your email, bank, or medical portal if you visit them while it's installed. Incogni's 2026 report flagged Fireflies as "high risk impact" for this reason.

**Verdict: the best free option, but only if you** (1) use a separate browser profile solely for meetings, and (2) keep email, banking, and sensitive sites out of that profile entirely.

## How to Use the Best AI Meeting Notes Chrome Extensions Safely

![How to use AI meeting notes extensions safely — browser profile screen with privacy settings](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1607252650355-f7fd0460ccdb?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

**The 2026 safe setup for meeting transcription:**

1. **Create a second Chrome profile** called "Work Meetings Only"
2. **Only log into meeting apps** on this profile
3. **Never open email, banking, or sensitive sites** in this profile
4. **Install Fireflies (or your chosen tool) only in this profile**
5. **Delete the profile if you stop using the extension**

This isolates the extension's broad permissions to only your meeting tabs. Before installing anything, read its permissions page on the store — the <a href="https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official Chrome for Developers extension documentation</a> explains what each permission like `tabCapture` or `<all_urls>` actually allows, and the <a href="https://support.google.com/chrome_webstore/answer/2664769?hl=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Google Web Store Help guide to installing and managing extensions</a> shows how to review and revoke permissions on installed items.

A well-rounded remote-work stack keeps the meeting transcript alongside other AI helpers — a [free AI content summarizer](/blog/free-ai-content-summarizer-extension-2026) for the documents that come out of meetings, a [voice input extension](/blog/chatgpt-voice-input-chrome-extension) for drafting the follow-up emails, and a [conversation search extension](/blog/chatgpt-conversation-search-extension-2026) so answers from past AI sessions stay findable.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which is the best AI meeting notes chrome extension for free users?

Fireflies.ai, because its free tier offers unlimited transcriptions with no monthly minute cap — unmatched among the major tools. Fathom also offers unlimited free transcriptions but only works with Zoom. Just remember that Fireflies requests the broadest permissions, so pair it with the separate-profile setup.

### Do these extensions work with Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams?

Fireflies.ai supports all three plus Webex. Otter.ai supports all three but through its own app rather than a Chrome extension. Tactiq is strongest on Google Meet with weaker Zoom support and no Teams. Fathom is Zoom-only.

### Is it legal to record and transcribe meetings?

Laws vary by jurisdiction: many places require only one party's consent (yours), while others require all participants to consent. Announce that you're recording, check your company policy, and never assume the built-in "recording" banner satisfies local law — the responsibility sits with the person initiating the transcription.

### Do AI meeting notes tools put my conversations on their servers?

Typically yes — transcription runs in the cloud for Fireflies, Otter, Tactiq, and Fathom. That's how they achieve accuracy and searchability, but it means confidential discussions are processed by a third party. For sensitive topics, use a local-only transcription tool or manual notes.

### Can the AI really detect action items and who said what?

Action-item detection works well on clearly stated commitments ("I'll send the report by Friday"). Speaker identification is more fragile: Otter handles it best after learning voices, while Fireflies users report mixed results, especially with accents, overlapping speech, or unfamiliar names. Treat attribution as a draft to verify, not ground truth.
