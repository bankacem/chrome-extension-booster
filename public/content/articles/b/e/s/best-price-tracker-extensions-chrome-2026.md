## I Spent a Month Watching Prices That Were Never What They Seemed

I put the same ten products on four trackers on the same day. A television I wanted for the living room. A laptop for work. A pair of headphones, a coffee maker, a book, a phone case, a board game, a pair of running shoes, a monitor, and a kitchen knife set. Then I watched for a month, and I wrote down what each tracker told me — and what it did not tell me.

Every price tracker promises the same thing: never overpay again. The trick is a chart. A line that shows what a product cost last month, last year, and yesterday, so you can tell whether today's "50 percent off" is a real deal or a price that was doubled last week and cut back to normal. That trick only works if the chart is complete, current, and honest about what it is showing. And the four tools in this article are not equally honest.

I tested Keepa, CamelCamelCamel, PriceHistory and BuyHatke side by side for a month, on Amazon in the US and India, checking which charts showed the most, which alerts arrived first, and which extension had quietly stopped being maintained. One of them has not been updated in over two years. Another one tracks more than 7 billion products. The gap between them is the whole story.

## The Four Contenders

**Keepa** is the deepest tracker here, and the most used. Its Chrome extension has around 4 million users and holds 4.67 stars from more than 5,100 ratings. It injects an interactive price-history chart directly into Amazon product pages, drawing five lines on one graph: Amazon's own price, the Buy Box price, third-party New, third-party Used, and Sales Rank. It covers 11 Amazon marketplaces and says it tracks more than 7 billion products. The free tier covers charts, a Deals feed, price-drop and availability alerts, and the browser extension. The paid Pro plan costs €29 a month or €290 a year and adds the full seller stack: sales rank history, Buy Box history, offer counts and a product finder. Free users report that tracking now stops at around 200 products; Pro raises that to 10,000. Keepa's extension was last updated in July 2026, so it is actively maintained.

**Get it:** [Keepa — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/keepa-amazon-price-track/neebplgakaahbhdphmkckjjcegoiijjoo)

**CamelCamelCamel** is the oldest tracker here, and the simplest. Its browser extension, called the Camelizer, has around 800,000 users and holds 4.30 stars from just over 1,000 ratings. It shows three price lines — Amazon, third-party New and third-party Used — with high, low and average values, and it sends free email alerts when a product hits the price you set. There is no paid plan at all: CamelCamelCamel is free, funded by affiliate commissions, and you can view charts and set alerts without creating an account. It covers 8 Amazon marketplaces, including Australia, which Keepa does not. The problem is maintenance: the Camelizer's last update landed in June 2024, more than two years ago, and its data refreshes less often than Keepa's on many products.

**Get it:** [The Camelizer — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/the-camelizer-price-track/milckebjkfgnlmdoejhmmklfknlmgoep)

**PriceHistory** is the tracker for shoppers in India. It injects price-history charts into Amazon.in and Flipkart product pages, shows the price trend over time, and sends alerts when a tracked product drops to your target price. It is free, and it is built specifically for the Indian market, where the two big trackers above have thinner coverage and where Flipkart — a store neither Keepa nor CamelCamelCamel covers — matters as much as Amazon. If you shop on Amazon.in, PriceHistory shows you the history that the global tools miss.

**Get it:** [PriceHistory — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/price-history/jahefcahofjbkjncokdokfgghpggpjlf)

**BuyHatke** is the other India-focused option, and the broader one. It started as a price-comparison site for Indian shoppers and grew into a Chrome extension that tracks prices on Amazon.in and Flipkart, compares prices across Indian stores, and surfaces coupons alongside the price history. It is free, and for an Indian shopper it answers a question the global tools never ask: is this the cheapest price across all Indian stores, or just the cheapest on Amazon today?

**Get it:** [BuyHatke — Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/buyhatke-the-online-shopp/ofbhffddfbgnlpflfbgblmmmpakphcbe)

## Where Each One Falls Short

Keepa's weakness is the free tier's ceiling. Two hundred tracked products sounds like a lot until you are watching a whole wishlist, and then the cap arrives without warning — users report that tracking simply stops. The Pro plan at €29 a month is priced for Amazon sellers, not shoppers, and most of what it adds — sales rank history, Buy Box history, offer counts, a data API from €49 a month — is useless to someone who just wants to know whether a television is cheap this week. Keepa is also the most complex tool here: five lines on one chart is powerful, but it is also the chart most likely to confuse a first-time user.

CamelCamelCamel's weakness is that it is fading. A browser extension that has not been updated since June 2024 is a risk in a browser that changes every month, and the data behind it refreshes more slowly than Keepa's. It shows no sales rank, no Buy Box history, no offer counts and no seller stock levels, so it cannot tell you whether a product actually sells or whether the seller you are buying from is reliable. It has no mobile app and no API. For a casual shopper who checks a price once a month, none of that matters. For anyone who tracks prices seriously, it is the reason to switch.

PriceHistory's weakness is scope. It is an India-only tool: Amazon.in and Flipkart, and nothing else. If you shop on Amazon.com, Amazon.co.uk or Amazon.de, it does nothing for you. It is also a smaller operation than Keepa, with a smaller user base and less public documentation about how often its data refreshes. For its audience it is the right tool; outside that audience it is invisible.

BuyHatke's weakness is the same as PriceHistory's, with a twist: it is a comparison tool first and a tracker second. Its price history is thinner than Keepa's, and its chart shows less detail. What it does well — comparing across Indian stores and finding coupons — is genuinely useful, but if your only question is "what did this cost last month," BuyHatke's answer is less complete than the chart Keepa draws.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**The television before Prime Day.** I tracked a 55-inch TV for three weeks before a sale event. Keepa's chart showed the price had been $499 for months, spiked to $649 the week before the sale, then "dropped" to $499 for the event — the classic fake discount. CamelCamelCamel showed the same shape, a day later. The chart saved me from celebrating a discount that did not exist. This is the scenario price trackers exist for, and both tools passed it.

**The laptop I needed now.** I needed a laptop for work and could not wait for a sale. Keepa showed the price had been stable for four months, with no dip in sight, and the Buy Box line showed only one seller at that price. I bought it. The tracker did not save me money — it saved me three weeks of waiting for a drop that was never coming. A flat chart is also an answer.

**The book that kept changing price.** I tracked a hardcover book that moved between $18 and $27 five times in a month. CamelCamelCamel's email alert caught the $18 dip and I bought it. Keepa's alert arrived two hours later — the same drop, slower notification. For fast-moving small items, the alert speed matters more than the chart depth, and the simpler tool won this round.

**The phone on Amazon.in.** I tracked a phone on Amazon.in, where Keepa's coverage is thin and CamelCamelCamel does not operate the same way. PriceHistory showed a clean six-month chart with two clear dips, and its alert caught a festival-season drop to the lowest price in that window. BuyHatke showed the same phone cheaper on Flipkart the same week — a fact neither global tool could have told me. For Indian shoppers, the local tools are not a compromise. They are the only tools that see the whole market.

**The used headphones.** I wanted a pair of headphones and was willing to buy used. Keepa's Used price line showed the third-party Used price had been falling for two months, while the Amazon price was flat. I bought used and saved $60. CamelCamelCamel also shows a Used line, so it would have worked too — but Keepa's chart made the trend obvious at a glance, while CamelCamelCamel's took a minute of staring. When you are comparing four lines against three, the extra line earns its keep.

## The Privacy Question Nobody Can Dodge

A price tracker watches what you shop for. Keepa's extension reads the Amazon pages you visit so it can inject its chart, and its free tier requires no account — which means it does not tie your browsing to an identity, but it still sees the products you look at. CamelCamelCamel is the lightest touch here: you can set a price watch from a confirmation email alone, with no account at all, and the extension only activates on Amazon pages.

The real privacy question is the data itself. Keepa says it tracks more than 7 billion Amazon products, and that database is its business — the Pro subscriptions and the paid data API are built on it. Your individual browsing is a rounding error in that database, but the database exists because millions of people let the extension watch their shopping. That is a fair trade for a free chart, as long as you know it is happening.

The practical advice is the same as for any shopping tool. Do not install two price trackers that do the same job — each one reads your Amazon pages, and the second one adds nothing but another company watching. Turn off the extension on stores you do not track, if your browser lets you limit site access. And remember that a price alert is an email from a company that knows what you want to buy. That email is worth having, but it is not free.

## What the Old Guard Looks Like Now

You do not need an extension at all to check a price history. CamelCamelCamel's website shows the same charts without installing anything, and Keepa's website does too — the extensions just put the chart on the product page so you do not have to open another tab. If you only check prices occasionally, the website is the lighter option, and it leaves nothing running in your browser.

The other old guard is the deal community: sites like Slickdeals, where users post price drops they found, often before any tracker alerts you. A tracker tells you the history; a deal forum tells you what is cheap right now. I use both — the tracker to judge whether a deal is real, the forum to find the deal in the first place. Independent comparisons back up the numbers in this article: the [Keepa vs CamelCamelCamel comparison](https://revenuegeeks.com/compare/keepa-vs-camelcamelcamel) confirms the user counts, ratings and the July 2026 update date I found, and the [CamelCamelCamel review](https://oabeans.com/camelcamelcamel-reviews/) covers the free plan and the missing seller data in detail.

## Key Takeaways

- Keepa is the deepest tracker: five chart lines including Sales Rank and Buy Box, 11 marketplaces, 4 million users, and the only one actively maintained — but the free tier caps at around 200 tracked products and Pro costs €29 a month.
- CamelCamelCamel is the best free tracker for casual shoppers: fully free, no account, unlimited watches, email alerts — but the Camelizer extension has not been updated since June 2024 and its data refreshes more slowly.
- PriceHistory is the right tracker for Amazon.in and Flipkart shoppers: free, local, and the only tool here that sees Flipkart prices.
- BuyHatke is the best comparison tool for Indian shoppers: price history plus cross-store comparison plus coupons, all free.
- The chart is the product. A "50 percent off" badge means nothing without the history behind it, and the tracker that shows you the most history is the one that saves you the most money.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Keepa free?

Yes, for shoppers. Keepa's free tier includes price-history charts, the Deals feed, price-drop and availability alerts, and the browser extension. The Pro plan at €29 a month adds seller data — sales rank history, Buy Box history, offer counts and a product finder — which most shoppers never need. Free tracking reportedly stops at around 200 products.

### Is CamelCamelCamel still working in 2026?

Yes, the website and the Camelizer extension still work, and the price alerts still arrive. But the extension has not been updated since June 2024, and its data refreshes less often than Keepa's on many products. It remains the best fully free option for casual use, but it is no longer the best-maintained one.

### Which price tracker works for Amazon India?

PriceHistory and BuyHatke are built for Amazon.in, and both also cover Flipkart. Keepa covers Amazon.in as one of its 11 marketplaces, but its coverage there is thinner than in the US, and CamelCamelCamel's Indian coverage is limited. For Indian shoppers, the local tools see more.

### Do price trackers work on Flipkart?

Keepa and CamelCamelCamel do not cover Flipkart. PriceHistory and BuyHatke do — they inject price-history charts into Flipkart product pages and track prices there the same way they do on Amazon.in.

### Can a price tracker tell me the best time to buy?

It can tell you the price history, which is most of the answer. If the chart shows a product drops every few months and is currently near its high, waiting is the smart move. If the chart is flat for a year, there is no dip coming and you should buy now. No tracker can predict the future — it can only show you the past clearly enough to decide.

### Why do some "deals" show a fake discount?

Because the seller raised the price before the sale. A product that costs $499 for months, jumps to $649 for a week, then "drops" to $499 is not on sale — it is back to its normal price. A price tracker exposes this in one glance, which is exactly why retailers dislike them.

## Verdict

Install **Keepa** if you track prices seriously. Five chart lines, 11 marketplaces, 4 million users and active maintenance make it the deepest and most reliable tracker here. The 200-product free cap is the only real limitation, and for most shoppers 200 watched products is more than enough.

Install **CamelCamelCamel** if you want a tracker that costs nothing and asks for nothing. No account, no cap, no paid tier, and email alerts that still work. The stale extension is a real concern, but for checking a price once a month it remains the simplest honest tool in this category.

Install **PriceHistory** or **BuyHatke** if you shop on Amazon.in or Flipkart. The global tools do not see the Indian market properly, and these two do — PriceHistory for the cleanest chart, BuyHatke if you also want cross-store comparison and coupons.

And whatever you install, install only one. Two price trackers reading the same Amazon pages is not twice the information. It is twice the watching.
