## [Understanding](/blog "Understanding CORS Chrome: A Comprehensive Guide to Web Development") the Problem

Before we dive into the solutions, let's understand why Google Chrome might be slow on your old laptop. There are several reasons for this, including:

- Outdated hardware: Older laptops often have slower processors, less RAM, and outdated graphics cards, which can struggle to keep up with modern web applications.
- Resource-intensive extensions: Some Chrome extensions can consume a lot of system resources, slowing down the browser and affecting overall performance.
- Cached data and cookies: Over time, cached data and cookies can accumulate and slow down Chrome, especially if you don't regularly clean them up.
- Malware and viruses: Malicious software can infect your laptop and slow down Chrome, making it essential to run regular virus scans and keep your antivirus software up to date.

## Optimizing Chrome for Better Performance

To **speed up Google Chrome on old laptop**, you'll need to optimize the browser and your laptop for better performance. Here are some tips to get you started:

### Update Chrome to the Latest Version

Ensuring you're running the latest version of Chrome is essential for optimal performance. To check for updates, follow these steps:

1. Open Google Chrome on your old laptop.
2. Click the three vertical dots in the top right corner.
3. Click "Help" and then "About Google Chrome."
4. Chrome will automatically check for updates and prompt you to install the latest version if available.

### Disable Resource-Intensive Extensions

Some Chrome extensions can consume a lot of system resources, slowing down the browser. To disable resource-intensive extensions, follow these steps:

1. Open Google Chrome on your old laptop.
2. Click the three vertical dots in the top right corner.
3. Click "More tools" and then "Extensions."
4. Review your installed extensions and disable any that you don't use or that are consuming excessive resources.

Consider using our [ProTab Suspender](/extension/protab-suspender) extension, which can help suspend inactive tabs and free up system resources.

### Clear Cached Data and Cookies

Cached data and cookies can accumulate over time and slow down Chrome. To clear them, follow these steps:

1. Open Google Chrome on your old laptop.
2. Click the three vertical dots in the top right corner.
3. Click "More tools" and then "Clear browsing data."
4. Select the types of data you want to clear, including cached images and files, cookies, and browsing history.
5. Choose a time range and click "Clear data."

### Use a Lightweight Theme

Using a lightweight theme can help reduce Chrome's resource usage and improve performance. To change your theme, follow these steps:

1. Open Google Chrome on your old laptop.
2. Click the three vertical dots in the top right corner.
3. Click "Settings" and then "Appearance."
4. Choose a lightweight theme from the available options.
