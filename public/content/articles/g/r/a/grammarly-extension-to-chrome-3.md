Grammarly began as a simple grammar checking tool but has evolved into a comprehensive writing assistant powered by advanced natural language processing (NLP) algorithms. At its core, Grammarly scans your text in real-time, comparing it against a vast database of linguistic rules and patterns to identify errors and suggest improvements. The Chrome extension specifically works by intercepting text input across websites and web applications, providing immediate feedback without requiring you to copy text between applications.

The extension operates through a sophisticated multi-layered approach. First, it performs basic spell checking, catching obvious typos and misspellings. Second, it analyzes grammar rules, subject-verb agreement, proper punctuation, and sentence structure. Third, it evaluates writing clarity, conciseness, and tone. Finally, in [its premium version](/blog/the-latest-idm-extension-for-chrome-free), it checks for plagiarism and provides style suggestions tailored to your specific writing goals.

What sets Grammarly apart from basic spell checkers is its contextual understanding. For example, it can distinguish between "their/there/they're," "your/you're," and other commonly confused words based on context. In my testing, this contextual accuracy proved particularly valuable in professional communications where such mistakes could undermine credibility.

The Chrome extension specifically works by creating an overlay in text fields across websites, allowing it to analyze your writing as you type. This real-time functionality is one of its greatest strengths, enabling immediate corrections and suggestions without interrupting your workflow. However, this constant monitoring does come with performance considerations that we'll explore later in this guide.

## Installation and Setup: Getting Started with Grammarly {#installation-and-setup}

Installing the Grammarly extension to Chrome is a straightforward process that takes less than two minutes. Here's a step-by-step guide based on my testing across different Chrome versions:

1. Open Chrome and navigate to the [Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/grammarly-grammar-checker-sp/klbibkeccnjlkjkiokjodocebajanakg)
2. Search for "Grammarly" in the store's search bar
3. Click on the official Grammarly extension (look for the green checkmark indicating it's the verified publisher)
4. Click "Add to Chrome" and confirm the installation
5. After installation, click the Grammarly icon in your toolbar and select "Sign Up" to create an account
6. Choose between the free or premium version (we'll compare these later)
7. Complete the onboarding process, which includes selecting your writing goals (academic, business, casual, etc.)

Once installed, Grammarly will automatically begin working across websites where text input is available. The extension appears as a green double underline beneath text it's analyzing, with suggestions appearing in a sidebar when you click on underlined text.

During setup, you'll be asked to connect Grammarly to your Google account for easier access. This integration allows Grammarly to sync your settings across devices, though you can also create a separate Grammarly account if you prefer. In my testing, the Google account integration worked seamlessly, with settings properly syncing between my Chrome installations on different devices.

One important note during installation: Chrome may show a [permission](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/permission-api) request for Grammarly to "read and change all your data on the websites you visit." This is normal and necessary for the extension to function properly, as it needs access to text fields to provide real-time suggestions. However, Grammarly emphasizes that it doesn't store or transmit your personal content—only the text being analyzed is processed for grammar checking.

## Performance Considerations: Memory Usage and Optimization {#performance-considerations}

As a Chrome power user who runs multiple extensions simultaneously, I've noticed that Grammarly can impact browser performance, particularly memory usage. In my testing across different Chrome configurations, I found that Grammarly typically adds between 50-100MB of memory usage, with occasional spikes up to 150MB when processing lengthy documents.

This impact varies based on several factors:
- Text length and complexity being analyzed
- Number of open tabs and extensions
- Chrome version and system resources
- Grammarly settings (real-time checking frequency)

To optimize Grammarly's performance while maintaining its benefits:

1. **Adjust real-time checking frequency**: In Grammarly settings, you can disable continuous real-time checking and instead activate it manually with a keyboard shortcut. This reduces constant background processing.

2. **Use the free version for basic needs**: The free version uses fewer resources than the premium version while still providing solid grammar and spell checking.

3. **Disable on specific sites**: Grammarly allows you to disable the extension on specific websites where you don't need it, such as video streaming platforms or image-heavy sites.

4. **Regular browser maintenance**: Clearing Chrome's cache and cookies periodically can help maintain overall browser performance when using resource-intensive extensions like Grammarly.

5. **Consider tab management**: Grammarly analyzes text in every open tab, so keeping only necessary tabs open reduces its workload.

In my experience, the performance impact is generally worth the benefits for most users, especially those who do significant writing in Chrome. However, users with very limited RAM or older computers might want to use Grammarly selectively rather than keeping it active at all times.

## Troubleshooting Common Grammarly Issues {#troubleshooting-common-issues}

Even the most reliable tools encounter issues occasionally. Based on my experience and common user reports, here are the most frequent Grammarly problems and their solutions:

### Extension Not Working
If Grammarly stops functioning:
1. Check that the extension is enabled in Chrome's extensions page
2. Try reloading the current webpage
3. Remove and reinstall the extension
4. Clear Chrome's cache and cookies
5. Update Chrome to the latest version

### Real-Time Checking Not Working
For suggestions that don't appear as you type:
1. Verify that real-time checking is enabled in Grammarly settings
2. Check if the website uses an unusual text input field
3. Try using the manual checking shortcut (Ctrl+Alt+L on Windows, Cmd+Shift+L on Mac)

### Performance Issues
If Chrome becomes sluggish with Grammarly:
1. Reduce the number of open tabs
2. Disable Grammarly on non-essential sites
3. Adjust real-time checking frequency in settings
4. Consider using the free version temporarily

### Sync Problems
When settings don't sync across devices:
1. Ensure you're logged into the same Grammarly account
2. Check internet connectivity
3. Verify sync is enabled in account settings
4. Try logging out and back in

### Incorrect Suggestions
If Grammarly provides inaccurate recommendations:
1. Add the word to your custom dictionary
2. Adjust your writing goals and formality settings
3. Provide feedback directly through the extension
4. Check for conflicting extensions that might interfere

In my experience, most issues can be resolved by simply reloading the page or temporarily disabling and re-enabling the extension. Keeping Chrome and Grammarly updated also prevents many potential problems.

## Frequently Asked Questions {#frequently-asked-questions}

### Is Grammarly free to use with Chrome?
Grammarly offers a free version with essential grammar and spell checking features. The premium version, which includes advanced suggestions, plagiarism detection, and style analysis, requires a subscription.

### Does Grammarly work on all websites?
Grammarly works on most websites with text input fields, including Gmail, Google Docs, social media platforms, and content management systems. However, it may not function properly on some highly customized or secure sites with unusual text input implementations.

### How much memory does Grammarly use?
In my testing, Grammarly typically uses between 50-100MB of memory, with occasional spikes during intensive document analysis. This can vary based on system configuration and usage patterns.

### Can I use Grammarly offline?
The Grammarly extension requires an internet connection to function, as it processes text through Grammarly's servers. However, the desktop version offers limited offline functionality.

### Does Grammarly store my writing?
Grammarly's privacy policy states that it stores text temporarily for processing purposes but doesn't retain personal content permanently. Users can control data retention through their account settings.

### Is Grammarly accurate for non-native English speakers?
Grammarly is particularly helpful for non-native English speakers, as it can identify common mistakes and provide explanations for corrections. However, it's most effective when used alongside language learning resources.

### How does Grammarly compare to human proofreading?
While Grammarly catches approximately 95% of common errors, it can't match the nuance and contextual understanding of a human proofreader, especially for highly specialized or creative content.

### Can Grammarly be used for languages other than English?
The free version primarily supports English, but premium subscriptions include additional languages like Spanish, French, German, and others, with varying levels of support.
