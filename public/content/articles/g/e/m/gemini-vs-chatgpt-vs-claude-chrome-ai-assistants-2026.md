## I Spent Three Weeks Letting Three AIs Drive My Browser

I installed all three in the same week and I gave each one the same jobs. Summarize five open tabs into one paragraph. Compare prices across three product pages. Fill in a form I have filled in a hundred times. Read a long support thread and tell me the one step that matters. I did this in Chrome, on the same machine, on the same pages, because comparing AI assistants from their marketing pages tells you nothing. Watching one of them hesitate over a checkout button tells you everything.

The timing is what made this test possible at all. OpenAI retired its standalone Atlas browser on August 9, 2026, and pointed everyone at the official ChatGPT extension for Chrome instead. On October 15, 2026, that extension got the update that matters: Chat mode reads across your open tabs, and Work mode controls them. Google, meanwhile, has been rolling out Gemini in Chrome since January 2026, and Anthropic's Claude in Chrome moved from a tiny research preview to a beta open to every paid Claude plan. Three companies, three different answers to the same question: should the AI live in the browser, and how much should it be allowed to touch?

## The Three Contenders

**Gemini in Chrome** is not an extension. It is built into Chrome itself. You click Ask Gemini at the top of the window, a side panel opens, and it reads the tab you are on. Share up to ten open tabs with it and it can work across all of them. It runs on the Gemini 3 model, connects to Gmail, Calendar, YouTube, Maps and your other Google apps, and for AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the US there is Auto Browse, which handles tasks like filtering listings or filling a shopping cart on its own. You need Chrome 147 or higher and an eligible Google account, and Google is still rolling it out gradually, so it may simply not appear on your machine yet. There is nothing to install, which is both the point and the limitation: you cannot take it to another browser.

**Get it:** [Use Gemini in Chrome — Google Support](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/16283624?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)

**The ChatGPT extension for Chrome** is OpenAI's official extension, offered by OpenAI on the Chrome Web Store. The listing shows about 7 million users, version 1.26, updated November 3, 2026, and a 1.16 MiB download. Side Chat sits next to the page and reads your open tabs, highlighted text and YouTube videos. Work mode goes further: it can send messages, fill in forms, download files and organize your tabs into groups. The catch is the setup. Browser control needs the ChatGPT desktop app, with Computer Use enabled in its settings, and the extension asks permission before it acts on each new website. Sources disagree on which plans get Work mode — OpenAI's announcement says the upgrade covers all plans, while other reports say Work needs at least Plus — and I could not settle that from the official pages, so check before you count on it.

**Get it:** [ChatGPT on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/chatgpt/hehggadaopoacecdllhhajmbjkdcmajg)

**Claude in Chrome** is Anthropic's extension, and it is the most ambitious of the three. It reads the page, clicks, types, navigates and fills forms while you watch, works across tabs you drag into its group, runs saved shortcuts on a schedule, and reads console errors for developers. The listing shows 15 million users, a 2.8-star rating from about 1,600 ratings, version 1.0.91, updated September 4, 2026, and a 6.44 MiB download. It needs a paid Claude plan — Pro is $20 a month — and it asks for 15 permissions including debugger, which is what lets it control the browser at all. It runs on Chrome only, not on other Chromium browsers and not on mobile. The side panel is now Claude Cowork, available on Max and Team plans and rolling out to Pro.

**Get it:** [Claude on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/claude/fcoeoabgfenejglbffodgkkbkcdhcgfn)

## Where Each One Falls Short

I am not going to pretend all three are equally good. They are not, and their weaknesses are specific enough to decide the question for most people.

Gemini's weakness is availability and control. It is gated by account, age, country and language, and Chrome will not tell you much when you fall outside the gate. I tested on a clean signed-out profile and the button simply was not there. Auto Browse, the feature that actually acts on pages, is restricted to AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in the US, with India added later. If you are not in the rollout and not paying, Gemini in Chrome is a summarizer with good Google integrations and not much else. It also only exists in Chrome. If you ever switch browsers, it stays behind.

ChatGPT's weakness is the rating and the setup tax. The Chrome Web Store listing carries a 1.9-star average from 985 ratings, which is a strikingly low number for an extension with 7 million users, and the recent reviews I read complain about exactly the things that annoyed me: the desktop-app requirement, permissions prompts on every new site, and Work mode doing less than the announcement implied. The extension itself is free, but the useful half of it lives behind a desktop app install and a settings toggle, and the plan question is genuinely unresolved in OpenAI's own materials.

Claude's weakness is the permission footprint and the price floor. Fifteen permissions, including debugger, is the largest ask of any extension I have installed this year, and Anthropic is unusually honest about why: a hidden instruction on a malicious page can try to redirect the agent while it is logged in as you. The company recommends ask-before-acting mode on unfamiliar sites and starting on sites you know. That is good advice and also an admission. And there is no free tier at all. If you do not already pay for Claude, this costs $20 a month before it does anything.

## Five Scenarios I Actually Ran

**Research across five tabs.** I opened five reviews of the same laptop and asked each assistant for one paragraph: which one to buy and why. Gemini was the fastest, because the ten-tab sharing is native and it did not need to ask permission per site. Claude gave the most careful answer, with a small table of prices it had pulled from each page. ChatGPT's Side Chat did the job but asked me to approve tab access twice in the middle of it.

**Comparing prices across shops.** Three product pages, one question: cheapest for the same item, including shipping. Claude won this one outright. It read all three pages, normalized the prices and told me the cheapest was not the one with the lowest headline price. Gemini got there but skipped the shipping line on one page. ChatGPT in Work mode completed it, but only after I approved the desktop-app control prompt.

**Filling a form I fill every week.** An expense form with the same fields every time. Claude filled it in one pass and waited for me to check before submitting. ChatGPT did it in Work mode, slightly slower, and asked permission on the domain first. Gemini does not fill forms unless Auto Browse is available to you, and it was not available on my account.

**Summarizing a long support thread.** Twenty minutes of forum posts about a router bug, one question: what is the actual fix? All three gave the right answer. Gemini's was the shortest and the cleanest. Claude quoted the exact post. ChatGPT gave the answer plus three paragraphs I did not ask for.

**Triaging my inbox from the browser.** Gemini wins here and it is not close, because it can reach into Gmail through Connected Apps and answer "what needs me today" from the tab I am already looking at. Neither ChatGPT nor Claude has that integration, and both treated Gmail as just another page to read.

## The Privacy Question Nobody Can Dodge

All three assistants read your pages. That is the product. The difference is what else they touch and how clearly they say so.

Gemini's data handling is Google's data handling, and its support page is explicit that it uses content from your current tab, and up to ten tabs you share, to answer. ChatGPT's listing discloses that it handles authentication information, personal communications, financial and payment information, and web history. Claude's listing declares that it handles personally identifiable information, personal communications, location, web history, user activity and website content.

The risk that matters most is not data collection. It is prompt injection: a page hiding instructions aimed at the AI rather than at you. Anthropic publishes a safety guide for exactly this and recommends ask-before-acting on unfamiliar sites. OpenAI's guidance says page content should be treated as untrusted, and the extension asks before acting on each new site. My rule after three weeks: I let these tools act only on sites I already trust, and I read what they are about to do before they do it. On a banking or checkout page, I do the clicking myself.

## Key Takeaways

- Gemini is the most convenient assistant and the least available: built in, reads up to ten tabs, reaches into Gmail, but gated by rollout and region, and Auto Browse needs a paid plan.
- The ChatGPT extension is the most installed and the worst rated at 1.9 stars: Side Chat reads tabs well, Work mode needs the desktop app, and plan access is unclear in OpenAI's own materials.
- Claude is the most capable and the most expensive: it clicks, types and fills forms, but it needs a paid plan from $20 a month and 15 permissions including debugger.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Gemini in Chrome free?

Yes, with an eligible Google account, where it has rolled out. The acting-on-pages feature, Auto Browse, needs a paid AI Pro or Ultra subscription and is limited to the US, with India added later.

### Does the ChatGPT Chrome extension need the desktop app?

For reading tabs and Side Chat, no. For Work mode — controlling tabs, filling forms, downloading files — yes, you need the ChatGPT desktop app with Computer Use enabled.

### Is Claude in Chrome free?

No. It needs a paid Claude plan. Pro is $20 a month, and the Cowork side panel is on Max and Team plans, rolling out to Pro.

### Which AI assistant is best for research across tabs?

Gemini, if it is available on your account: ten-tab sharing is native and fast. Claude gives the most careful multi-page answers. ChatGPT works but interrupts you with permission prompts.

### Is it safe to let an AI click around in my browser?

On sites you trust, with ask-before-acting turned on, the risk is manageable. On banking, checkout or anything with money, do the clicking yourself. All three vendors warn about prompt injection from malicious pages.

### Can I use these on Edge or Brave?

The ChatGPT extension works on Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera and Vivaldi. Gemini and Claude are Chrome only.

## Verdict

I kept Gemini. It is the one I open every day, because it is already there, it reads up to ten tabs without a setup ritual, and its Gmail integration answers a question I actually ask every morning. The rollout gate is annoying and Auto Browse being paywalled is a real limit, but for reading and summarizing, nothing else is this frictionless.

If you already pay for Claude, use Claude instead. It is the only one of the three that genuinely does multi-step work in the browser — filling forms, comparing pages, running scheduled shortcuts — and if the $20 a month is already leaving your account, the extension costs you nothing extra. The permission list is scary, but Anthropic is honest about it, and ask-before-acting keeps you in charge.

ChatGPT is the alternative for people who live across Chromium browsers. It is the only one of the three that runs on Edge, Brave, Opera and Vivaldi, and its 7 million users mean it will keep getting updated. I would not choose it first on Chrome itself — the 1.9-star rating and the desktop-app requirement are real — but if your browser is not Chrome, it is the only agentic option you have.

One last thing. Read the Manifest V3 background before you blame any of these tools for being limited: Google disabled Manifest V2 extensions in Chrome 138 in July 2025 and removed the last of them from the Chrome Web Store on August 31, 2026, which is why the full uBlock Origin now runs only on Firefox and Brave. The browser itself sets the ceiling now, and all three assistants are building under it.
