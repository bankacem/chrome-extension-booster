## I Asked Three Extensions to Stop Me From Wasting My Own Time

I know my own numbers. Left alone, I will open a social feed "for a second" at least a dozen times a day, and a second is never a second. So I tested the three website blockers everyone recommends, one at a time, for a month, with the same block list: the three social feeds, two video sites, and one news site that I check the way other people check the weather. Same list, same hours, same honest question — which one actually keeps me out?

The three could hardly be more different. LeechBlock NG is free, open source, and built by one developer. StayFocusd is free, owned by Sensor Tower, and built around a daily time budget. BlockSite is the biggest name in the category, with a million users, and the only one of the three that charges money. I ran each for at least a full working week, including the days when I actively wanted to cheat, because a blocker you can defeat in ten seconds is decoration.

## LeechBlock NG: The One You Configure Like an Engineer

LeechBlock NG is version 1.8, updated October 12, 2026, by James Anderson. It has about 100,000 users on the Chrome Web Store and a 4.9-star rating from 1,598 ratings — the highest rating of the three, and it has held steady across the last 500 reviews. It is free, and not in the freemium sense. There is no paid tier, no account, no advertising, and the code is public on GitHub under the Mozilla Public License 2.0. The listing declares that no data is collected or used.

The idea is block sets. You sort distracting sites into groups — up to 30 of them — and give each group its own rule. Blocked between 9am and 5pm. Allowed for ten minutes in every hour. Both at once. A countdown delay instead of a hard block, so you wait before the site loads. A password or a random access code on the settings page, so changing the rules takes effort. Wildcards, exceptions, keywords. It runs on Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Brave, Opera and Vivaldi, which matters if you ever switch browsers.

Its weakness is the options page. It is a dense form with its own notation, and the first time I opened it I closed it again and made tea. Setting up a simple 9-to-5 block takes five minutes once you know where everything is; learning where everything is takes longer. There is also an honest limit that applies to all three: a browser extension only blocks the browser it is installed in. Open another browser, or your phone, and the block is not there. LeechBlock can block the Extensions page during a block, which helps, but anyone determined to cheat will find the door.

**Get it:** [LeechBlock NG on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/leechblock-ng/blaaajhemilngeeffpbfkdjjoefldkok)

## StayFocusd: The One With a Daily Allowance

StayFocusd works differently. Instead of schedules, it gives you a daily time budget per site. You get, say, twenty minutes of a social feed a day, and when the budget is gone the site is gone until tomorrow. Version 4.4.1, updated January 19, 2026, by Sensor Tower. It has 700,000 users and a 4.4-star rating from 8,796 ratings. It is free, with no paid tier.

The feature everyone talks about is the Nuclear Option. You set a list and a duration, turn it on, and it cannot be cancelled once it is running. That sounds dramatic, and it is, and it is also the only feature in this test that I never once defeated, because there is nothing to defeat. I set it for a four-hour writing block on my worst site, and the site stayed dead for four hours.

StayFocusd's weaknesses are real and recent. Its average rating across the last 100 reviews has fallen to 3.35, down from 4.65 in December 2025, and the reviewers blame a block-list bug and privacy concerns. The privacy part deserves a straight answer: StayFocusd's listing declares that it collects web history, user activity and website content, and one reading of its disclosure lists network monitoring, clicks, mouse position, scroll and keystroke logging. That is the listing's own declaration, not my interpretation, and it is the heaviest data collection of the three. It is also, in my testing, the least flexible: a time budget is a good model for social feeds and a poor model for a site you need for ten minutes at 3pm and never again that day.

**Get it:** [StayFocusd on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/stayfocusd-%E2%80%93-website-bloc/laankejkbhbdhmipfmgcngdelahlfoji?hl=en-US)

## BlockSite: The Biggest Name, and the Paywall

BlockSite is the best-known blocker in the category and the most installed by far. Version 8.0.0, updated November 4, 2026, by BlockSite LP. It has 1,000,000 users and a 4.5-star rating from 32,432 ratings — the biggest numbers here, though the last 100 reviews average 3.86, down from 4.20 in July. Its feature list is the longest: a block list by site, category or keyword, scheduled blocking, a focus timer, an adult-content filter, a password lock, a custom redirect, and insights into how often you tried to open a blocked site. It syncs to Android and iOS, which neither rival does.

Its weakness is the free tier. Since 2024, the free version is capped at three blocked sites. Three. My test list had six. That cap is the whole business model — the full feature set needs the paid Premium tier — and it means the free version is a trial in everything but name. The second weakness is polish over control: BlockSite is the easiest of the three to set up and the hardest to make behave exactly the way you want. You get categories and schedules, but not LeechBlock's thirty rule sets, and not StayFocusd's unbreakable Nuclear Option. It is the blocker for someone who wants to install, block three sites, and never open the options page again.

**Get it:** [BlockSite on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/blocksite-block-websites/eiimnmioipafcokbfikbljfdeojpcgbh)

## Five Scenarios That Decided It For Me

**The 9-to-5 workday.** I block the feeds from 9 to 5, free after. LeechBlock does this exactly, per set, per day of the week. BlockSite does it with schedules. StayFocusd cannot do it at all — a budget is not a schedule, and twenty minutes at 9am is twenty minutes gone by 9:20.

**The ten-minutes-an-hour allowance.** LeechBlock again: allow ten minutes in every hour between 9 and 5. This is the rule that actually changed my behavior, because it teaches rationing instead of abstinence. Neither rival offers it in this form.

**The deadline lockdown.** Four hours, one site, no escape. StayFocusd's Nuclear Option is the only feature here that is genuinely unbreakable, and on deadline day that is worth more than every other feature combined. LeechBlock's lockdown plus a random access code comes close. BlockSite's password lock does not, because I know my own password.

**The phone problem.** BlockSite is the only one of the three with Android and iOS apps, so the block follows you off the desktop. If your distraction lives on your phone — and for most people it does — this is BlockSite's strongest card, and it is a real one.

**The shared computer.** A family machine, where I want the block on and someone else wants it off. LeechBlock's password-protected options page handles this. StayFocusd has no equivalent. BlockSite's password lock works, but only on the paid tier's full feature set.

## The Privacy Angle, Stated Plainly

A website blocker reads your browsing by definition. It has to know which site you just opened. The question is what it does with that knowledge.

LeechBlock's listing declares no data collection, its code is public, and it needs no account. StayFocusd's listing declares collection of web history, user activity and website content, and it is owned by Sensor Tower, an analytics company. BlockSite's listing declares web history collection. I am not going to tell you what to feel about that. I will tell you what I did: I kept the blocker that collects nothing, and I accepted that the one with the unbreakable mode collects the most. That is a trade, and you should make it with your eyes open.

## Setup: What Each One Takes

LeechBlock took me twenty minutes to set up properly, including reading the documentation. The documentation is good — the developer publishes examples — and once the sets are built, I have not touched them in weeks. StayFocusd took three minutes: install, set a budget, done. BlockSite took five: install, add three sites, hit the cap, sigh, pay or stop. If setup time is your deciding factor, the order is StayFocusd, BlockSite, LeechBlock. If control is your deciding factor, the order reverses.

One practical note that applies to all three: give the extension "On all sites" access in its Details page, and turn on "Allow in Incognito", or the block has a hole in it exactly where you will look for one at 11pm.

## Key Takeaways

- LeechBlock NG is the strongest free blocker: 30 rule sets, schedules plus time allowances, no data collection, open source, 4.9 stars — but its options page takes twenty minutes to learn.
- StayFocusd's Nuclear Option is the only genuinely unbreakable block here, but its recent reviews have fallen to 3.35 and its listing declares the heaviest data collection.
- BlockSite is the easiest to set up and the only one with mobile apps, but its free tier caps you at three blocked sites.
- A lifetime rating hides a recent slide: check the last 100 reviews, not the headline number.
- No browser extension blocks your phone or a second browser. Turn on Incognito access or the block has a hole.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which website blocker is completely free?

LeechBlock NG and StayFocusd. LeechBlock has no paid tier at all. BlockSite's free tier caps you at three blocked sites.

### Can I block sites on a schedule?

LeechBlock NG and BlockSite both support scheduled blocking. StayFocusd uses a daily time budget instead of a schedule.

### What is StayFocusd's Nuclear Option?

You set a list and a duration, turn it on, and it cannot be cancelled until the time runs out. It is the strongest single feature in this comparison.

### Do these blockers work in Incognito mode?

Only if you allow it. Go to Extensions, open the blocker's Details page, and turn on Allow in Incognito.

### Can I block sites on my phone too?

BlockSite has Android and iOS apps that sync with the extension. LeechBlock NG and StayFocusd are desktop-browser only.

### Is LeechBlock NG hard to set up?

It takes about twenty minutes the first time, including reading the documentation. After that, the rule sets run themselves.

## Verdict

I kept LeechBlock NG. It is free forever, it collects nothing, its code is public, and its thirty rule sets let me build exactly the shape of focus I need — 9-to-5 blocks, ten minutes an hour, a lockdown with a random code I cannot guess in a weak moment. The options page is ugly and it took me twenty minutes to learn. I have not thought about it since.

If you have one deadline and one site that ruins it, install StayFocusd instead and use the Nuclear Option. Nothing else here is unbreakable, and on deadline day that is the only feature that matters. Accept the data collection with your eyes open, and uninstall it when the deadline passes.
