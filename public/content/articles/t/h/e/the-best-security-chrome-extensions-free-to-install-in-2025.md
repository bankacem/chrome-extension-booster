<ol class="wp-block-list"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>uBlock Origin: The King of Content Filtering</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<p><!-- /wp:list --> <!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Let&rsquo;s get one thing straight. If you are using "AdBlock Plus," delete it. Right now. It&rsquo;s resource-heavy and lets companies pay to show you ads.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --> <!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>uBlock Origin is the only answer here. It is efficiently coded, entirely open-source, and ruthless. It doesn't just kill annoyance; it kills "malvertising"&mdash;those nasty ads that execute drive-by downloads the moment they load on your screen.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --> <!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Why it&rsquo;s a non-negotiable install:<br />Most people think ad blockers are just for convenience. They are wrong. They are a security layer. By blocking the scripts that load ads, you are essentially closing the biggest door malware uses to get in. uBlock Origin uses practically zero CPU power, so your fan won't sound like a jet engine while you browse.</p>

<p>The <a href="/blog/best-memory-saver-extension-for-chrome-4" class="internal-link" title="Best Memory Saver Extension for Chrome: Boost Performance and Reclaim Your RAM"><a href="/blog/fast-screenshot-extension-alternatives-1" class="internal-link" title="Discover the Best Fast Screenshot Extension Alternatives for Seamless Browsing">extension</a></a> sits quietly in Chrome. <ol class="wp-block-list" start="3"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Privacy Badger: The EFF&rsquo;s Watchdog</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<p><!-- /wp:list --> <!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>You know when you look at a pair of boots on Amazon, and then those boots follow you to Facebook, Instagram, and a random news site? That&rsquo;s cross-site tracking. It&rsquo;s creepy.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --> <!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Privacy Badger comes from the Electronic Frontier Foundation (EFF). These guys are the heavyweights of digital rights. This extension isn't working off a stagnant list of "bad guys." It actually learns.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --> <!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>If Privacy Badger notices a domain tracking you across three different websites, it snaps into action and blocks it. It&rsquo;s automated vigilance. You install it, you forget it, and suddenly the internet feels a lot less like a surveillance state.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --> <!-- wp:list {"ordered":true,"start":4} --></p>
<ol class="wp-block-list" start="4"><!-- wp:list-item -->
<li>Netcraft: The Phishing Killer</li>
<!-- /wp:list-item --></ol>
<p><!-- /wp:list --> <!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Phishing is getting sophisticated. The emails look real. The login pages look identical to your bank&rsquo;s. Even smart people get tricked.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --> <!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Netcraft is a veteran in the security space. Their extension is essentially a giant community watch program for the web. If you stumble onto a site that looks suspicious or is known for hosting malware, Netcraft blocks it before the page even renders.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --> <!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>It also gives you a "risk rating" for every site you visit. It&rsquo;s particularly good at spotting those nasty homograph attacks&mdash;where hackers use foreign characters that look like English letters (like replacing a Latin 'a' with a Cyrillic 'a') to trick you into thinking you are on apple.com when you aren't.</p>

<p>Click&amp;Clean puts a big red button on your browser. One click, and it flushes your cache, cookies, history, and download logs. It can even delete the terrifying "Local Shared Objects" (Flash cookies) that stick around like gum on a shoe. It&rsquo;s instant digital amnesia.</p>
