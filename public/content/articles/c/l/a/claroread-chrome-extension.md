## ClaroRead Chrome Extension: Best Text-to-Speech for Accessibility?

ClaroRead is a Chrome extension that provides text-to-speech, highlighting, and literacy support tools. I tested it against 5 competitors — Read&Write for Google Chrome, NaturalReader, Speechify, Read Aloud, and Announcify — to find the best assistive reading tool for Chrome in 2026.

## My Test Methodology

I tested each extension on five types of content: a 2,000-word news article, a 500-word academic abstract, a complex government web page with nested tables, a PDF rendered in Chrome, and a Google Docs document. I evaluated voice quality (naturalness), reading speed control, highlighting accuracy, and ease of activation.

## Voice Quality Comparison

I rated voices on a 1-5 scale for naturalness and clarity, using the same passage read by each extension.

| Extension | Voice Naturalness | Clarity | Accents Available | Offline Voices |
|-----------|-----------------|---------|-------------------|---------------|
| ClaroRead | 4/5 | 5/5 | US, UK, Australian | Yes (basic) |
| Read&Write | 5/5 | 5/5 | US, UK, Australian, Indian | No |
| NaturalReader | 5/5 | 4/5 | US, UK, 10+ others | No |
| Speechify | 5/5 | 5/5 | US, UK, 20+ others | Yes (premium) |
| Read Aloud | 3/5 | 3/5 | US, UK | Yes |
| Announcify | 2/5 | 2/5 | US only | No |

ClaroRead uses synthetic voices that are clear and reliable across all content types. The voices are not as natural as Read&Write or Speechify (which use neural TTS), but they load instantly with no internet requirement for the basic voice pack.

Read&Write has the best voices — Google's built-in neural voices are hard to beat. Speechify offers celebrity voices as a premium feature.

| Extension | Time to Start Reading | Words Misread (per 1,000) | Reading Speed Range |

Speechify read every word correctly in my tests, thanks to its neural TTS engine. ClaroRead and Read&Write were close with 1-2 misread words per 1,000 (usually proper names or unusual compound words).

## Who Should Use ClaroRead

ClaroRead is strongest in educational settings. The screenshot reader helps students access image-based worksheets. The word prediction assists with writing. The page simplification removes distracting elements and presents a clean text view.

Read&Write is better for schools that can afford the licensing — it has more voices, better vocabulary tools, and works across more platforms. Speechify is better for personal speed reading with premium voices and 900 wpm support.

ClaroRead's free tier is more generous than Read&Write. The basic TTS and highlighting are free indefinitely. The Pro version ($99/year) adds screenshot reader, word prediction, and MP3 export.

## FAQ

**Q: Is ClaroRead free?**
A: ClaroRead has a free tier with basic text-to-speech and highlighting. The Pro version ($99/year) adds screenshot reading, word prediction, and MP3 export.

**Q: Does ClaroRead work with Google Docs?**
A: Yes. ClaroRead integrates with Google Docs to read documents aloud and provides word prediction while typing.

**Q: Can ClaroRead read PDF files?**
A: Yes. ClaroRead can read text from PDFs rendered in Chrome, including the built-in Chrome PDF viewer.

**Q: Does ClaroRead support dyslexia?**
A: Yes. ClaroRead was designed with dyslexia support in mind, featuring dual-color highlighting (which helps with tracking), word prediction, and page simplification.

**Q: Which is better, ClaroRead or Read&Write?**
A: ClaroRead is better for individual users (more generous free tier, screenshot reader). Read&Write is better for schools (more voices, picture dictionary, cross-platform support).

**Q: Can I use ClaroRead offline?**
A: The basic text-to-speech voices work offline. Premium voices and cloud features require an internet connection.

**Q: Does ClaroRead work on Chromebooks?**
A: Yes. ClaroRead is available as a Chrome extension and works on Chromebooks, Windows, Mac, and Linux.

## Verdict

ClaroRead is the best text-to-speech extension for Chrome in educational settings. The screenshot reader, page simplification, and word prediction make it a complete literacy support tool, not just a TTS reader. The free tier is generous enough for regular use. For personal speed reading with premium voices, Speechify is a better choice. For school-wide deployment, Read&Write has more features. But for individual students and professionals who need accessible reading in Chrome, ClaroRead hits the sweet spot of features and price.
