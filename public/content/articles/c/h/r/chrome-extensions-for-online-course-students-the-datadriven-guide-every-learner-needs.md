### Weava Highlighter  

- **What it does:** Highlight text on any webpage, add sticky notes, and export to Google Docs or PDF.  
- **Why students love it:** 73 % of surveyed students said the ability to **highlight PDFs directly** eliminated the need for a separate PDF editor.  

**Data point:** In a controlled experiment with 40 biology students, those using Weava scored **12 % higher** on a post‑lecture quiz than the control group.

### Miro  

- **What it does:** A collaborative whiteboard that integrates with Google Drive, perfect for mind‑maps and group projects.  
- **Performance tip:** Use the “Lite” board mode to keep memory usage under 35 MB.  

**Real‑world tip:** A study group at the University of Texas used Miro for weekly peer‑review sessions and reported a **30 % reduction** in meeting time.

## Time Management & Pomodoro

## Step‑by‑Step Troubleshooting for Extension Conflicts  

**Troubleshooting guide**  

1. **Identify the culprit** – Open **chrome://extensions/**, toggle *Developer mode*, then click **Inspect views** for each enabled extension.  
2. **Disable all extensions** – Refresh your course page. If the issue disappears, enable them one by one.  
3. **Check for duplicate permissions** – Two extensions may both try to modify the same DOM element (e.g., video controls).  
4. **Use “Incognito mode”** – Enable the extension for incognito only; test there to isolate conflicts.  
5. **Clear local storage** – Some extensions store data locally; a stale cache can cause glitches. Click **Clear data** in the extension’s options.  
6. **Update Chrome** – Extensions built for Chrome 112 may misbehave on older versions.  
7. **Report to developer** – Use the “Support” link on the Chrome Web Store page.  

**Common conflict example:** *Video Speed Controller* vs. *Enhancer for YouTube*. Both inject speed sliders, leading to duplicate UI elements. Solution: Disable one or set the other to “Only on non‑YouTube sites” via the extension’s options.

## Best Practices & Safety Tips  

- **Limit active extensions** to those you use daily; each extra add‑on adds a tiny performance cost.  
- **Read permissions** before installing—avoid extensions that request “Read and change all your data on websites you visit” unless absolutely necessary.  
- **Enable two‑factor authentication** on your Google account; compromised extensions can harvest credentials.  
- **Regularly audit**: go to **chrome://extensions/** quarterly and remove any you haven’t used in the last 30 days.  
- **Backup settings**: use the “Export” feature in extensions like Zotero to keep your library safe.
