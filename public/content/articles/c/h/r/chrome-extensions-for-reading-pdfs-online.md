Reading PDFs in the browser should not require downloading files and opening a separate app. The right Chrome extensions for reading PDFs online let you highlight passages, add sticky notes, fill forms, and even convert documents to editable formats without ever leaving the tab. Chrome's built-in viewer handles basic viewing and printing, but the moment you need to do anything interactive, the default workflow breaks down: download, open a desktop app, edit, re-upload, repeat.

This guide covers the extensions that genuinely fix that workflow, organized by what they do best. Some replace the default viewer entirely, some specialize in annotation and collaboration, and others focus on conversion and form filling. By the end you will know exactly which tool matches how you actually work with documents — and if you want to understand what the built-in viewer can and cannot do first, our [Chrome built-in PDF viewer guide](/blog/chrome-pdf-viewer-guide) is the natural starting point.

## The Problem with PDFs in Chrome

![A PDF annotation workflow with browser tools arranged around a document](/content/images/chrome-extensions-for-reading-pdfs-online/pdf-annotation-workflow.webp "PDF annotation workflow")

Chrome ships with a competent PDF viewer, but "competent" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. You can view, scroll, zoom, and print. You cannot annotate, highlight with saved notes, add text boxes, fill interactive forms, sign, or convert. Every time a document demands more than passive reading, you are forced into the download-edit-re-upload loop — a workflow that becomes genuinely painful when you are reviewing contracts, grading papers, or cross-referencing multiple reports in one session.

Extensions solve this by adding full-featured PDF tooling directly in the browser tab. The six tools below are the ones that actually deliver on that promise rather than wrapping a crude viewer in a marketing page.

## Chrome Extensions for Reading PDFs Online: Full-Featured Editors

![Chrome extensions for reading PDFs online — laptop with an open document beside a coffee cup on a desk](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1611162616305-c69b3fa7fbe0?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

### Kami

Kami is the most comprehensive PDF extension for Chrome. It opens PDFs directly in the browser with a full annotation toolbar: highlight, underline, strikethrough, text boxes, freehand drawing, stamps, and threaded comments. Where Kami pulls ahead of the pack is collaboration — multiple people can annotate the same document simultaneously, with changes appearing live for everyone. Its Google Drive and Classroom integrations make it the default choice in schools, but freelancers and teams get the same value when reviewing drafts together.

### PDF Viewer by PDF4WEB

This extension replaces Chrome's default viewer with a more capable engine that supports form filling, digital signatures, and basic annotations. Its biggest practical advantage is performance: the default viewer tends to lag on documents over 50 pages, while PDF4WEB keeps scrolling smooth even on lengthy contracts and manuals. If your PDF life is mostly "open, interact, sign, move on," this is the least invasive upgrade — it changes the viewer, not your habits.

## Annotation and Highlighting Specialists

![Highlighting and annotation tools for browser-based PDF reading — stylus resting on a tablet displaying text](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1574717024653-61fd2cf4d44d?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

### Hypothesis

Hypothesis is an open-source annotation layer that works on both PDFs and web pages. You highlight text, attach notes, and choose between public and private annotations. The distinguishing feature is the shared layer: on public documents you can see annotations from other users, which turns a static PDF into a conversation. Research groups, seminar classes, and journalist source-review workflows get the most out of it. For readers who need extra support while studying, pairing Hypothesis with an assistive tool such as [ClaroRead for Chrome](/blog/claroread-chrome-extension) covers both annotation and read-aloud comprehension.

### Lumin PDF

Lumin PDF offers a clean annotation interface — highlight, comment, draw — plus light editing like page deletion, reordering, and rotation. Its cloud-first design means you open files straight from Google Drive, Dropbox, or OneDrive without downloading them first, and edits save back to storage automatically. For people who live inside Drive, that seamless round-trip is worth more than any single feature.

## PDF Conversion and Form-Filling Tools

![A browser-based PDF conversion workflow from document source to editable output](/content/images/chrome-extensions-for-reading-pdfs-online/pdf-conversion-workflow.webp "PDF conversion workflow")

### Smallpdf

Smallpdf is a suite of PDF utilities that runs as a Chrome extension. For reading workflows, its most useful trick is conversion: PDF to Word, Excel, or PowerPoint, so you can extract content and edit it natively. Researchers quoting a study, analysts pulling tables out of a report, and anyone repurposing PDF content save real time compared with retyping or launching desktop conversion software.

### DocHub

DocHub concentrates on form filling and signing. When you hit a fillable PDF in the browser, DocHub lets you complete text fields, add checkmarks, insert dates, and apply digital signatures, then save or share the finished document. For contracts, applications, tax forms, and permission slips, it removes the entire download-open-print-scan cycle that used to define paperwork.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are these Chrome extensions for reading PDFs online safe for sensitive documents?

Kami, Hypothesis, and DocHub all offer options to keep documents private, and Hypothesis is open source so its handling can be audited. Still, check each extension's privacy policy before uploading confidential files. For highly sensitive material, a desktop application with local-only processing may be more appropriate.

### Can I edit existing PDF text with these extensions?

Most browser-based PDF tools focus on annotation rather than text editing. If you need to modify the underlying text in a PDF, you will need a more advanced tool like Adobe Acrobat. The extensions here add annotations on top of the PDF without changing its content.

### Do these extensions work with password-protected PDFs?

Yes, in almost every case you enter the password to unlock the document first, after which the extension interacts with the PDF normally. Some tools remember unlocked documents for the session only, so expect to re-enter passwords in new tabs or sessions.

### Do I still need a PDF extension now that Chrome's viewer keeps improving?

For pure viewing, no — the built-in viewer is fine. Extensions earn their place the moment you need saved highlights, shared annotations, signatures, form data, or format conversion, none of which the default viewer offers.

### Will these extensions slow Chrome down with large PDF files?

The extensions only activate when you open a PDF, so everyday browsing is unaffected. For very large documents, PDF4WEB's viewer is specifically built to stay responsive where the default viewer can lag, making it the safest pick for 100+ page files.
