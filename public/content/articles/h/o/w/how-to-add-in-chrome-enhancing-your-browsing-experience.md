Are you looking to **add in Chrome** new features, functionality, or tools to enhance your browsing experience? Extensions are the easiest way to do it: a two-minute install can block popups, capture screenshots, manage passwords, or translate any page on the fly. This guide walks through exactly how to **add in Chrome** the tools you want, from finding a trustworthy extension in the Web Store to keeping your collection organized long after installation day.

Before installing anything, it helps to understand what you are actually adding. A browser extension is a small software program that plugs into Chrome and gains the ability to read or modify the pages you visit. That power is what makes extensions useful — and what makes it worth spending a minute checking ratings, permissions, and the developer's reputation first. If you have ever wondered what is technically going on under the hood, the overview of [what a browser extension is in 2026](/blog/what-is-a-browser-extension-2026) is a solid primer.

## What Can You Add in Chrome?

Chrome extensions cover nearly every browsing task you can name. Some popular types include:

- **Productivity extensions** like [ProTab Suspender](/extension/protab-suspender), which suspends inactive tabs to save memory, and [Quick Screenshot Lite](/extension/quick-screenshot-lite), which captures full-page screenshots instantly.
- **Security extensions** like [Redirect Shield](/extension/redirect-shield), which stops automatic and potentially malicious redirects, and [SecuraKey Pro](/extension/securakey-pro), an encrypted password manager.
- **Convenience extensions** like [Cookie Banner Blocker](/extension/cookie-banner-blocker) for dismissing consent banners, [Offline Reader Pro](/extension/offline-reader-pro) for saving articles, and [Auto Dark Mode Switcher](/extension/auto-dark-mode-switcher) for scheduled theme changes.

You can also **add in Chrome** things that are not extensions at all — themes to restyle the interface, and web apps that open in their own window. The categories overlap less than you might expect: themes change how Chrome looks, while extensions change what it can do.

## How to Add in Chrome: Step-by-Step

![How To Add In Chrome Enhancing Your Browsing Experience Overview](/content/images/how-to-add-in-chrome-enhancing-your-browsing-experience/how-to-add-in-chrome-enhancing-your-browsing-experience-overview.webp "How To Add In Chrome Enhancing Your Browsing Experience Overview")

Adding an extension is a straightforward process that takes about a minute:

1. **Open the Chrome Web Store.** Type `chromewebstore.google.com` in the address bar, or search for the store from Google.
2. **Search for the extension you want to add in Chrome.** Use a specific phrase — "full page screenshot" beats "screenshot" — and check that the result is the tool you actually meant.
3. **Evaluate the listing.** Read the rating, the number of users, recent reviews, and the "Last updated" date before trusting it.
4. **Click "Add to Chrome".** A permission dialog appears; confirm by clicking **Add extension** only if the requested permissions make sense for the tool's function.
5. **Pin it to your toolbar.** Click the puzzle-piece icon and pin the extensions you use daily so they are always one click away.

Google documents the full [install and management flow in Web Store Help](https://support.google.com/chrome_webstore/answer/2664769?hl=en) if you want the official version of these steps. The permissions dialog in step 4 deserves real attention: a calculator extension has no business reading data on every website you visit, and declining a suspicious permission list costs you nothing.

## Managing Extensions After You Add Them in Chrome

![How To Add In Chrome Enhancing Your Browsing Experience Features](/content/images/how-to-add-in-chrome-enhancing-your-browsing-experience/how-to-add-in-chrome-enhancing-your-browsing-experience-features.webp "How To Add In Chrome Enhancing Your Browsing Experience Features")

Installing is the easy part; a tidy extension setup is what keeps Chrome fast. After you **add in Chrome** a few tools, manage them from `chrome://extensions`:

- **Toggle on/off** to disable an extension temporarily without uninstalling it — useful when debugging site problems.
- **Enable Developer mode** to view an extension's ID and inspect what it is actually running, something the <a href="https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">official extension documentation</a> explains in depth.
- **Click "Remove"** for anything you have not used in a month. Fewer extensions mean less memory use and a smaller attack surface.
- **Let Chrome auto-update.** Updates install automatically in the background, but you can force one with the "Update" button on the extensions page.

If a page misbehaves after a new install, disable your extensions one by one from this page to find the culprit — it is the fastest diagnostic in the browser.

## Tips for Choosing What to Add in Chrome

![How to add in Chrome — person comparing browser tool options on a desktop setup](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1633356122544-f134324a6cee?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

With thousands of options, a little selectivity goes a long way:

- **Prefer tools that solve a problem you have daily.** A niche extension you need twice a year does not justify a permanent toolbar slot.
- **Favor recently updated extensions** with a steady review history, and be skeptical of tools whose ratings spike overnight.
- **Check the permissions against the function.** Screenshot tools need page access; they do not need to change your data on all websites.
- **Avoid duplicates.** Two ad blockers or two screenshot tools will fight each other and waste memory.

For readers who want curated suggestions rather than building a list from scratch, the [Google Chrome addons guide](/blog/pro-google-chrome-addons-guide) groups the strongest tools by task. And if you browse mostly on a phone, note that the process differs on mobile — the guide to [adding extensions to Chrome on mobile](/blog/how-to-add-extensions-to-chrome-mobile) covers the Android workarounds.

## Frequently Asked Questions

![How to add in Chrome — user browsing extension listings before installing a new tool](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1555066931-4365d14bab8c?auto=format&fit=crop&w=1200&q=80)

### How do I add an extension in Chrome?

Open the Chrome Web Store, search for the extension, open its listing, and click "Add to Chrome". Confirm the permission dialog by clicking "Add extension", then pin the icon to your toolbar from the puzzle-piece menu.

### Are Chrome extensions safe?

Most are, especially popular ones from established developers. Safety comes from vetting: install only from the official Web Store, read recent reviews, check that requested permissions match the tool's stated function, and remove extensions you no longer use.

### Can I use Chrome extensions on multiple devices?

Yes. Sign in to Chrome with the same Google account and enable sync, and your extensions install automatically on every desktop where you sign in. Mobile devices work differently, as covered in our mobile extension guide.

### How do I remove an extension I added in Chrome?

Go to `chrome://extensions`, find the extension card, and click "Remove". You can also right-click the extension's toolbar icon and choose "Remove from Chrome" for a one-step uninstall.

### Why is "Add to Chrome" grayed out for some extensions?

This usually means the extension is incompatible with your browser or ChromeOS version, or that your device is managed by an organization with install restrictions. Check the listing's requirements, or ask your administrator if the device is managed.

### How do I keep the extensions I add in Chrome up to date?

Chrome updates extensions automatically by default. To force an update, open `chrome://extensions`, enable Developer mode, and click "Update" — handy right after a site change that might be tied to an extension fix.
