## I Went Looking for the Extensions Nobody Recommends

Every "best Chrome extensions" list I read in October 2026 recommended the same ten tools. Todoist, Grammarly, uBlock Origin Lite, OneTab. Good tools, all of them, and all of them already installed on half the internet. So I went looking for the opposite: small extensions that solve one specific annoyance, have small user counts, and rarely appear in roundups. I picked five that kept appearing in the corners — a November 2026 roundup here, a bookmark-manager guide there — and I used all five for a month before writing a word about them.

The rule was simple. Each extension had to do one job, do it well, and stay out of my way. Four of them passed. One of them has a problem I need to tell you about first, because it is the kind of thing that only shows up after you have invested time in it.

## 1. Bookmanize: The Bookmark Manager Chrome Should Have Shipped

Chrome's bookmark manager is a filing cabinet from 2008. Bookmanize treats your bookmarks as a database: every bookmark on one screen, custom tags instead of rigid folders, instant search across titles, URLs and descriptions, bulk edit, bulk delete and bulk re-tagging, and keyboard shortcuts that actually work. It reads your existing Chrome bookmark tree, so adopting it is not a migration — your bookmarks are already there.

I have a lot of bookmarks. Searching them in Bookmanize genuinely feels instant, and tag-filtering on several thousand bookmarks does not slow down. The keyboard shortcut — Ctrl+Shift+F to jump to search — became muscle memory in a day. Your data stays on your computer; the listing says plainly that nothing is saved or stored anywhere else.

Here is the problem, and I am not going to bury it. Bookmanize is version 1.6.26, last updated February 29, 2024, by an independent developer named Anton. It is free, it works, and it has a Featured badge on the store, but it has not been updated in more than two and a half years, and recent reviewers report that export and import are broken — only tags transfer, not bookmarks. One reviewer put it bluntly: the extension appears to be abandoned. I kept using it, because what it does, it does well, and it does it locally. But I would not build a tagging system with hundreds of hours of work inside a tool whose export is broken. If you install it, keep your Chrome bookmarks as the source of truth, and treat Bookmanize as a faster window onto them, not as the home for them.

**Get it:** [Bookmanize on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/bookmanize-advanced-bookm/idbigcbhfbdclcbljcoopcjgkejaknnl)

## 2. Screen Shader: The Orange Tint That Fixed My Evenings

Screen Shader tints your screen a warm orange to reduce eye strain and help your brain's day-night cycle. It is free and open source, and it is the only extension I found that changes the tint automatically based on your local sunset and sunrise times, instead of a fixed schedule you have to configure. Keyboard shortcuts — Alt+Shift+Up to increase the shade, Alt+Shift+Down to decrease it — let you adjust without opening anything.

I installed it on a Monday and forgot about it by Tuesday, which is exactly the point. By Thursday I noticed I had stopped squinting at white documents at 10pm. The effect is subtle and it is real, and it costs nothing: no account, no settings page to babysit, no subscription. The listing has been around for years and the developer links the research it is based on, which is more intellectual honesty than most extensions bother with.

Its weakness is scope. It tints the browser, not the system. If your eye strain comes from your whole computer, a system-level tool like f.lux does the job everywhere, and Screen Shader cannot follow you out of Chrome. Within Chrome, though, it is the simplest version of the idea, and simple is what survives.

**Get it:** [Screen Shader on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/screen-shader-smart-scree/fmlboobidmkelggdainpknloccojpppi)

## 3. Power Thesaurus: The Right Word, One Highlight Away

I write for a living, and the word I want is never the word I have. Power Thesaurus — version 4.6.3, updated October 15, 2026, a 4.77 MiB download from PowerThesaurus.org — puts synonyms, antonyms and definitions one highlight away. Select any word on any page and a clean popup shows alternatives. Click the toolbar button for a full search. Right-click any word to open the full site. Themes switch between system, dark and light, and a quick toggle turns the on-page tools off when you want a distraction-free page.

The suggestions are crowdsourced and community-voted, which means the first result is usually the word people actually use, not the word a dictionary committee prefers. That matters when you are editing a sentence and need the word that fits the tone, not the word that fits the definition. There is a PRO membership with in-app purchases — infinite scrolling and extra results — but the free tier covers the daily job.

Its weakness is reliability. The most common complaint in recent reviews is that the service sometimes becomes unavailable or stops working properly, and the extension needs broad permissions — it can read and change data on all sites — which is a big ask for a thesaurus. I hit the outage once in a month, for an afternoon. It came back. If your writing depends on it, keep a second thesaurus open.

**Get it:** [Power Thesaurus on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/power-thesaurus/hhnjkanigjoiglnlopahbbjdbfhkndjk)

## 4. SwiftRead: Read Three Times Faster, or At Least Finish the Article

SwiftRead is a speed-reading extension built on RSVP — Rapid Serial Visual Presentation — which flashes words one at a time at a speed you choose, so your eyes stop moving and your inner voice stops sounding each word out. Version 6.3.0, updated March 23, 2026. It works on web pages, PDFs, and web-based readers including Kindle Cloud Reader and Libby, and it adds natural text-to-speech voices in more than 90 languages with synced highlighting, so you can listen while you follow along.

I was skeptical. RSVP sounds like a gimmick, and the claim of reading three times faster sounds like a poster in a subway. What actually happened is less dramatic and more useful: I finished articles I would otherwise have abandoned halfway. At a moderate speed with paced highlighting, a 3,000-word article takes about ten minutes instead of twenty, and my comprehension held up for anything that was not dense technical writing. For dense material I dropped the speed and used the guided word display instead, which is still faster than my normal reading because it removes the regressions — the little backward eye movements that waste time on every page.

The PRO version adds native support for reading PDFs, ePUBs and Kindle books, and the free tier is enough to learn whether the habit sticks. One caution from the developer's own knowledge base: the legacy extension is no longer supported, so make sure you install the current one from the official listing, and note that it is not available on Firefox, Safari or mobile browsers.

**Get it:** [SwiftRead on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/swiftread-read-faster-lea/ipikiaejjblmdopojhpejjmbedhlibno)

## 5. Video Speed Controller: The Lecture Watcher's Best Friend

The fifth pick has a naming problem. A dozen extensions on the Chrome Web Store call themselves Video Speed Controller, and they are not the same tool. The one I tested is the listing called "Speed Up — Video Speed Controller", version 2.0.0, updated March 13, 2026, a 23.75 KiB download that collects no personal data. It gives you one-click speed control from 0.5x to 2x, slow motion at 0.5x and 0.75x, play and pause from the popup, and a smart time estimate — a ten-minute video at 2x shows 5:00 remaining, which sounds trivial until you have spent a month guessing.

I watch a lot of lectures and talks. YouTube's built-in speed control is buried two clicks deep and resets itself. This extension remembers, shows the speed on screen, and works on any HTML5 video, not just YouTube. The keyboard shortcuts — D to speed up, S to slow down, R to reset, Z to rewind, X to fast-forward — became automatic within a day. If you watch video to learn rather than to relax, this is the single highest-value install on this list, and it is 24 kilobytes.

The naming problem is also the warning: check the developer and the version before you install any extension called Video Speed Controller, because the store has several, and they vary in quality. The one I linked above is the one I used for a month.

**Get it:** [Video Speed Controller on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/speed-up/adaimdljmdjpdggphjmifaadbgoikcoh)

## Five Scenarios That Decided It For Me

**The researcher with 3,000 bookmarks.** Bookmanize. Tag-filtering across thousands of bookmarks is instant, and the bulk re-tagging fixed a year's worth of messy filing in one evening. Just do not trust it as your only copy.

**The night-shift reader.** Screen Shader. White documents at midnight stopped hurting, and the sunset-based tint meant I never configured anything. If you read in Chrome after dark, install it and forget it.

**The writer on deadline.** Power Thesaurus. Highlight a word, get five better ones, keep writing. The outage I hit cost me an afternoon; the other twenty-nine days it saved me more time than that.

**The student with a reading list.** SwiftRead. A week's worth of articles finished in half the time, with comprehension intact for anything that was not dense theory. For dense theory, slow it down and use the guided display.

**The lecture watcher.** Video Speed Controller. Two times speed, remembered across videos, time estimates that are actually right. If you learn from video, this is the one install that pays for the whole list.

## Key Takeaways

- Bookmanize is the best bookmark manager I tested — and the riskiest, because it has not been updated since February 2024 and its export is broken.
- Screen Shader is the simplest eye-strain fix in Chrome: free, open source, sunset-based, zero configuration.
- Power Thesaurus puts the right word one highlight away, but it has occasional outages and asks for broad permissions.
- SwiftRead genuinely halves reading time on ordinary articles, and its text-to-speech covers more than 90 languages.
- Video Speed Controller is 24 kilobytes and the highest-value install here — but check the developer, because a dozen extensions share the name.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are these extensions free?

Yes, all five have a free tier that covers the core job. Power Thesaurus and SwiftRead have paid PRO tiers for extra features.

### Is Bookmanize safe if it has not been updated since 2024?

It works, it is local-only, and it declares no data collection. The risk is not safety, it is maintenance: export is reported broken, so keep Chrome's own bookmarks as your source of truth.

### Does Screen Shader work outside Chrome?

No. It tints the browser only. For system-wide tinting, use a system-level tool like f.lux.

### Does SwiftRead really triple reading speed?

The "three times faster" claim is the listing's, not mine. In my month of use, ordinary articles took about half the time at a moderate speed, with comprehension intact. Dense material needs a slower setting.

### Which Video Speed Controller should I install?

Check the developer and version. I tested the listing called "Speed Up — Video Speed Controller", version 2.0.0, updated March 13, 2026. Several extensions share the name.

### Do any of these need an account?

No. All five work without signing in. Power Thesaurus PRO needs an account for its paid features.

## Verdict

Install Video Speed Controller first. It is 24 kilobytes, free, and it changes every lecture and talk you watch from now on. Then Screen Shader, because it costs nothing and you will forget it is there until you use a computer without it.

Add SwiftRead if you have a reading backlog, and Power Thesaurus if you write for a living — both earn their place, with the reliability caveats above. Bookmanize is the one I recommend with a warning label: the best bookmark manager in Chrome, built by a developer who has not shipped an update since February 2024. Use it as a faster window onto your Chrome bookmarks, never as the only home for them.
